# P32 — Seek a separation bound for one graph family

**Dependencies:** P06, P12 and P31. **Objective:** replace a finite census pattern with a uniform theorem or a uniform counterfamily.

**Inspect first:** P06's universal formulas, P12's separator proof, P31's mechanisms; `docs/chapters/10_inverse_problems.md`, `13_research_program.md`; `tests/test_analytics.py`: `test_regular_triangle_free_pair_delays_visibility_to_four`. The cube–ladder example is one calibration, not an infinite-family result.

**Work:** choose one explicitly parameterized family, its observation variant and a proposed bound on first separation. State whether the claim concerns nonisomorphic members, a specified pair construction, or restricted transformations. Try an induction using decomposition responses, or a classification from low-volume statistics. The recent high-girth delayed-visibility idea may supply a counterfamily to fixed-depth bounds: treat its forest-statistic reduction and bridge-versus-bridgeless construction as unproved targets requiring full arguments, not established facts.

**Mathematical acceptance:** prove the bound uniformly in the family parameter, or construct arbitrarily large certified counterexamples with a uniform proof of lower-degree agreement and eventual disagreement. Keep equal-order assumptions explicit. If a family has exact all-volume collisions, report them rather than forcing a separation theorem.

**Verification:** generate small instances independently, compare all coefficients through predicted thresholds and test boundary parameters. Tests support the proof; they do not replace its quantifier over every family member.

**Done:** one narrowly stated proved theorem or decisive negative family, with generators and regression fixtures. **Typical failure:** extrapolating from the largest computed graph, or claiming unrestricted graph reconstruction from a theorem about a small special family.
