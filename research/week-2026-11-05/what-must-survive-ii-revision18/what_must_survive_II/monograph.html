1. The loop law

For a cubic graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices with adjacency matrix \(A\), the energy of a height function is \(\sum_{uv\in E}(h_u-h_v)^2=h^{T}(3I-A)h\). Therefore \(L_G=\sum_h\prod_v x^{h_v}q^{3h_v^2}\prod_{uv\in E}q^{-2h_uh_v}\), and the interaction is trivial as soon as either endpoint has height 0. Work in \(R=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}][[x]]\). There is a unique family \(B(h)\in R\), \(h\ge 0\), with \(B(h)\equiv 1\pmod x\), solving

\[B(h)=\sum_{h'\ge 0}x^{h'}q^{3h'^2-2hh'}B(h')^2 .\]

Uniqueness and existence follow order by order. The only implicit coefficient is that of \(B(0)\) at \(x^t\), where the equation reads \(b_t=2b_t+\cdots\) and so determines \(b_t\). Put

\[Z_v=\sum_h x^hq^{3h^2}B(h)^3,\quad p(h)=\frac{x^hq^{3h^2}B(h)^3}{Z_v},\quad 1+F(h,h')=\frac{q^{-2hh'}Z_v}{B(h)B(h')}.\]

Theorem 1, proved. For every finite cubic graph, \[L_G=Z_v^{-n/2}\Bigl(1+\sum_{\emptyset\ne C\subseteq E,\ C\text{ leafless}}\Theta(C)\Bigr),\qquad \Theta(C)=\sum_{h:V(C)\to\mathbb N}\prod_{v}p(h_v)\prod_{uv\in C}F(h_u,h_v).\] Consequently \(\log L_G=-\tfrac n2\log Z_v+\sum_Q N_Q(G)\,b_Q(x,q)\), summed over connected leafless graphs \(Q\) of maximum degree 3, with \(N_Q\) the ordinary subgraph count and \(b_Q=O(x^{|V(Q)|})\).

Proof. Each vertex lies on 3 edges, so \(\prod_v p(h_v)\prod_{e}(1+F_e)=Z_v^{n/2}\) times the weight of \(h\): the factors \(B^3\) cancel and \(Z_v^{-n+3n/2}\) remains. Summing gives \(L_G=Z_v^{-n/2}\sum_h\prod_v p\prod_e(1+F)\). Expand the edge product over subsets \(C\). The defining equation yields \(\sum_{h'}p(h')(1+F(h,h'))=B(h)^{-1}\sum_{h'}x^{h'}q^{3h'^2-2hh'}B(h')^2=1\), and \(\sum_h p(h)=1\), so \(\sum_{h'}p(h')F(h,h')=0\). A subset with a leaf therefore contributes 0, and vertices outside \(C\) sum to 1. Convergence is \(x\)-adic because \(p(h)=O(x^h)\).

The logarithm is a hard-core polymer expansion. Polymers are connected leafless subgraphs, 2 of them conflict when they share a vertex, and \(\Theta\) is multiplicative on vertex-disjoint pieces. Thus \(b_Q=\sum\phi\,\prod_i\Theta(C_i)\) over clusters whose union is \(Q\), with \(\phi\) the Ursell coefficient. Two polymers sharing a vertex must share an edge there, since the degree is at most 3. So a cluster of at least 2 polymers has total size at least \(|V(Q)|+2\). For the order count, note \(F(a,b)=O(1)\) for \(a,b\ge1\), \(F(0,a)=O(x)\), and \(F(0,0)=O(x^2)\); the last follows from \(Z_v-B(0)^2=B(0)^2(B(0)-1)+xq^3B(1)^3+O(x^2)\), whose order-\(x\) terms cancel. Charging each \((0,a)\) edge to its zero vertex and splitting each \((0,0)\) edge evenly gives a charge of at least 1 per vertex. Hence \(\Theta(Q)=O(x^{|V(Q)|})\), a height \(h\ge2\) costs excess \(h-1\), and a height 0 at degree \(d\) costs excess at least \(d-1\).

Corollary, proved. If \(G\) has girth above \(V\), then \(\log L_G\equiv-\tfrac n2\log Z_v\pmod{x^{V+1}}\). The first volume at which \(\log L_G\) departs from this universal series is exactly the girth, because \(C_g\) is the only leafless pattern on \(g\) vertices of girth \(g\). This identity is the Bethe free energy with Chertkov–Chernyak loop corrections, specialized to a translation-invariant fixed point, and the leafless expansion belongs to established loop calculus (Chertkov and Chernyak 2006; for non-binary and continuous alphabets, Mori 2013). The countable height alphabet is handled here by the \(x\)-adic argument above.

Checks. The formula was compared to brute-force enumeration of \(\log L_G\) through volume 8 on \(K_4\), \(K_{3,3}\), the prism, the cube and \(2K_4\), at 2 values of \(q\) in \(\mathbb F_p\), with no discrepancy.

5. The reflection law and the jet spaces

Feeding Theorem 7 into the jet decomposition exposed a symmetry that was not visible in the loop expansion. Put \(q=e^{\varepsilon}\), \(y=x/(1-x)\), and \(v=y(1+y)=x/(1-x)^2\).

Theorem 8 (reflection law), proved. 9. The hidden object: the cut lattice

The energy \(h^T(3I-A)h\) is the graph Laplacian form, and sections 1 to 5 never used its defining symmetry: it is unchanged by \(h\mapsto h+\mathbf 1\). For connected \(G\), the map \(h\mapsto\delta h\) identifies heights modulo constants with the integer cut lattice \(\Lambda_G\subset\mathbb Z^E\), and \(q^{h^TLh}=q^{|\delta h|^2}\). This lattice is classical: its determinant is the number of spanning trees \(\tau(G)\), and its discriminant group is the Jacobian of the graph (Bacher, de la Harpe and Nagnibeda 1997). The series itself is a discrete Gaussian, or solid-on-solid, height model with a chemical potential. So the correct description is a known lattice with a known type of statistical model on it, and I make no priority claim for that identification. What is specific here is the marking below and what it does.

Shell 3 is complete on caterpillars

Set \(k=|T|\), \(\ell=N+1-k\), \(g_p=x^{-p}-1\), \(h_q=x^q-x^{N-q}\), \(s_q=x^q+x^{N-q}\). Let \(r\) run over the fold values \(\min(t,N-t)\) of all cuts, with multiplicity, and \(\nu_p=\#\{r<p\}\). Expanding the ranks in Theorem 26 turns \(H_3\) into sums over at most 3 cuts: \[x^{-1}H_3=\sum_{a<b<c}F(a,b,c)+\sum_{s<t}G(s,t)+\sum_tO(t)+\text{const},\] where, with \(u=t-s\), \(v=t+s\), \(P(t)=x^{t-2}+x^{N+t}+2x^t+x^{2N-t}\), \(Q(t)=P(N-t)\), \(M(t)=x^t+x^{N-t}+x^{N+t}+x^{2N-t}\), \[F=x^{a-b+c}+x^{N+b-a-c}+x^{b+c-a}+x^{N+c-a-b}+x^{N-c+a}-x^{c-a-2}-x^{a+c}-x^{b+c}+x^{2N-b-c}+x^{c-2}+x^{N-c-2},\] \[G=\ell x^u+\ell x^{N+u}+(\ell-u+1)x^{N-u}+(t-1)x^v+(\ell-s)x^{2N-v}+(u-1)x^{u-2}+(1-t)P(t)+(\ell-t)Q(t)+(2t-\ell-1)M(t).\] and \(O(t)=\binom t2P+\binom{\ell-t}2Q+t(\ell-t)M\). The cross term \(P+Q-2M\) of the rank square collapses to \(x^{t-2}+x^{N-t-2}\), which is the last pair in \(F\).

Theorem 34 (shell 3 energy), proved. For fixed \(A\), \(x^{-1}H_3\) is again a 2-spin energy, with couplings that depend on fold data and not on spins. For 2 orientations with \(\tau_{pq}=\sigma_p\sigma_q-\sigma'_p\sigma'_q\) and equal \(B\), \[4x^{-1}\Delta H_3=\sum_{p<q}\tau_{pq}\bigl(h_q\Lambda_p+g_p\Pi_q\bigr),\] \[\Lambda_p=-(2p+1-\nu_p)h_p+1-x^{N-2p}+\sum_{r<p}\bigl(-h_r+x^{r-p}-x^{p-r}+x^{-2}(g_p-g_r)\bigr),\quad \Pi_q=x^{-2}\sum_{r>q}(s_q-s_r)-s_qh_q.\]

Proof. Each summand involves at most 3 spins and the total is reflection invariant, so only pair terms survive, and a third cut enters through its fold value alone. Taking the \(\sigma_p\sigma_q\) component of \(G\) and \(F\) for the 3 positions of \(r\) gives \(4J_{pq}\) with every term a multiple of \(h_q\), except \(x^{-2}(N-2q)s_q+2x^{-p-2}\theta h_q\) and \(g_px^{-2}\sum_{r>q}(s_q-s_r)\). Equal \(B\) means \(\sum\tau g_ph_q=0\), and applying \(\theta\) gives \(\sum\tau g_p\theta h_q=\sum\tau px^{-p}h_q\). These remove every multiple of \(g_ph_q\), including \(Sg_ph_q\) and \(kx^{-2}g_ph_q\), and they leave the identity \((N-2q)s_q+2\theta h_q=Nh_q\). Exact check: the unreduced coupling on 1500 random orientation pairs, the reduced form on all 7832 census classes.

Theorem 35 (caterpillar completeness), proved. Two caterpillars with equal shells 1, 2 and 3 are isomorphic. Precisely, if they have equal shells 1 and 2 and are not isomorphic, let \(r_1\) be the smallest spin site, choose the global sign so it is not flipped, and let \(q_*\) be the smallest flipped site. With \(\nu=\nu_{r_1}\), \[\Delta[q^3]M=\tfrac12\,\sigma_{r_1}\sigma_{q_*}\,(2r_1-\nu)\,x^{3n-3-q_*-r_1}+\text{lower},\qquad 2r_1-\nu\ge2.\]

Proof. Let \(c_q=\sum_{p<q}\tau_{pq}\). All nonzero \(\tau_{pq}\) have \(q\ge q_*\) and \(p\ge r_1\). In \(\sum\tau g_ph_q=0\), the coefficient of \(x^{N-q_0}\) for \(q_0<q_*+r_1\) is \(c_{q_0}\) alone, because \(x^{N-q-p}\) needs \(q+p\ge q_*+r_1\) and the remaining exponents are below \(N/2\). So \(c_q=0\) for \(q<q_*+r_1\). In particular \(\sum_{p<q_*}\sigma_p=0\). Now read Theorem 34 at \(E=2N-q_*-r_1\). Every \(g_p\Pi_q\) has degree at most \(2N-2q_*<E\). Cuts with fold below \(r_1\) carry no spin and sit below every \(p\), so they contribute \(-H_0\sum_qc_qh_q\) with \(H_0\) of degree \(N-r_0\), and this has degree at most \(2N-r_0-q_*-r_1<E\). The rest of \(\Lambda_p\) has degree at most \(N-r_1\), so only \(q=q_*\) with \(-x^{N-q_*}\) from \(h_{q_*}\) reaches \(E\). There \(p=r_1\) has coefficient \(2r_1+1-\nu\) and each \(p>r_1\) has coefficient 1, so the total at \(E\) is \[-2\sigma_{q_*}\Bigl(\sigma_{r_1}(2r_1+1-\nu)+\sum_{r_1<p<q_*}\sigma_p\Bigr)=-2\sigma_{r_1}\sigma_{q_*}(2r_1-\nu).\] Each fold below \(r_1\) has at most 2 cuts, so \(\nu\le2r_1-2\). Finally \(\Delta[q^3]M=(1-x^n)\Delta H_3\) by Theorem 26, and \(1+n+E=3n-3-q_*-r_1\). If shell 1 or 2 differs there is nothing to prove, and equal spins up to a global flip mean \(T'=T\) or \(T^R\), which is isomorphism. Exact check: degree and coefficient on all 7834 census pairs through \(n=30\) from the closed form, and on all 39 classes at \(n=11,14,17,18\) by direct sign enumeration of shell 3 with no closed form. For Theorem 28, \(r_1=s\), \(\nu=0\), \(q_*=2s+2\), which recovers the outside top term \(s\,x^{15s+19}\) up to the sign convention.

So shell 3 suffices for all \(n\), whatever Theorem 32's solution set turns out to be. This is the first compatibility argument here that uses a higher shell, and it uses exactly the mechanism expected from the program: the shell 2 relation is fed into shell 3. The scope is among caterpillars. Among all trees, caterpillars are not yet known to be recognizable from \(M\); the census finds no tree sharing shells 1 to 3 with a caterpillar through 19 vertices, but that is finite evidence. Completeness for general trees remains open, and nothing here extends automatically: general trees have more than a linear order of cuts.

Exchange across a balanced edge: the difference is a square

The first survivors of shell 3, on 16 vertices, differ by 2 leaf moves across a balanced edge, and this turns out to be general. Let a tree have an edge \(xy\) and let the rooted trees hanging from \(x\) and \(y\) be arms. For a rooted forest \(W\) put \(G_W(a)=\sum_hx^{|h|}q^{E(h)}\), summing over \(h\ge0\) on \(W\) with the roots attached to a vertex of height \(a\). Then \(G\) is multiplicative under disjoint union and \[L_{(U|V)}=\sum_{a,b\ge0}q^{(a-b)^2}x^{a+b}\,G_U(a)\,G_V(b).\] Symmetrizing in \(a,b\) gives the following.

Theorem 36 (exchange identity), proved. For rooted forests \(W_1,W_2,c_1,c_2\), write \(\mathcal W_{ij}(a,b)=G_{i}(a)G_{j}(b)-G_{i}(b)G_{j}(a)\). Then \[L_{(W_1c_1|W_2c_2)}-L_{(W_1c_2|W_2c_1)}=\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}q^{(a-b)^2}x^{a+b}\,\mathcal W_{W_1W_2}(a,b)\,\mathcal W_{c_1c_2}(a,b).\] In particular, with \(T=(2A|2B)\) and \(S=(A{+}B|A{+}B)\), \[L_T-L_S=\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}q^{(a-b)^2}x^{a+b}\,\bigl(G_A(a)G_B(b)-G_A(b)G_B(a)\bigr)^2.\] So for \(0<x,q<1\), \(L_T>L_S\) unless \(G_A/G_B\) is independent of height. These pairs can collide only through proportional rooted series, and Theorem 41 reduces that to equal series.

Exact check: the identity in exact rationals on small forests, and the factorization of \(L\) against brute force. Theorem 31 and the 16-vertex survivors are both instances.

The order of vanishing comes from a census. For \(F\) a set of edges of \(W\), let \(Q(F)\) be \(F\) with its ancestor order and each edge labelled by the size below it. Its \(d\)-census is the multiset of \(Q(F)\) with \(|F|\le d\). In a term of \(G_W(a)\) of energy \(k\), the edges with \(z\ne0\) form a set of size at most \(k\), and the volume \(a|W|+\sum z_fs_f\) and the constraint \(h\ge0\) depend only on \(Q\). So if \(W,W'\) have equal weight and equal \(d\)-census, \(G_W-G_{W'}\) has energy order at least \(d+1\).

Theorem 37 (first separating shell), proved. If \(W_1,W_2\) have equal \(d_1\)-census and \(c_1,c_2\) have equal \(d_2\)-census, both pairs with equal weight, then the 2 exchanged trees have equal shells through \(d_1+d_2+2\). Proof. \(\mathcal W_{W_1W_2}\) is a difference of products in which replacing \(W_2\) by \(W_1\) gives 0, so it has energy order at least \(d_1+1\); likewise \(d_2+1\). Terms with \(a=b\) vanish, and \(a\ne b\) costs energy at least 1. Theorem 38 (no finite window is complete), proved. For all \(E,V\) there are non-isomorphic trees, with the same \(n\), whose series \(M\) agree on all coefficients of energy at most \(E\) and on all coefficients of volume at most \(V\). So no fixed shell, no fixed volume, and no fixed union of the 2 is complete for trees.

Proof. The census generating function \(C(W)=\sum_F[Q(F)]\) lies in the polynomial ring on connected labelled types and is multiplicative under union, so \(\log C\), truncated at degree \(d\), is additive. With labels at most \(r\), it lies in a space of dimension polynomial in \(r\). Likewise, for \(a\le V\), \(G_t(a)=q^{a^2}(1+O(x))\) for a single rooted tree \(t\), so \(\log(q^{-\rho(W)a^2}G_W(a))\bmod x^{V+1}\) is additive, where \(\rho(W)\) is the number of attached arms, with coefficients Laurent polynomials in \(q\) of degree polynomial in \(r\). Add the coordinates for weight and number of roots. The number of rooted trees on at most \(r\) vertices grows exponentially, so for large \(r\) these additive vectors satisfy a nontrivial rational linear relation among distinct trees. Clearing denominators and separating signs gives disjointly supported forests \(A\ne B\), with equal weight, equal \(d\)-census, and \(G_A(a)\equiv G_B(a)\bmod x^{V+1}\) for \(a\le V\). Theorem 40 (2-terminal exchange), proved. Let \(M\) be a tree with terminals \(u,v\) whose terminal partition function \(K(a,b)=\sum_{h_u=a,h_v=b}x^{|h|}q^{E(h)}\) is symmetric, for example because an automorphism swaps \(u,v\). Proof. Symmetrize \(a,b\). Terms with \(a\ne b\) cost energy at least 1 on the path in \(M\). Theorem 41 (proportional means equal), proved. If \(G_A(a)/G_B(a)\) is independent of \(a\), then \(G_A=G_B\), and \(A,B\) have equal weight and equal edge-size histogram.

Proof. For \(a\ge k\) the constraint \(h\ge0\) is inactive at energy \(k\), so \([q^k]G_W(a)=x^{a|W|}[q^k]R_W\) with \(R_W=\prod_e\theta(x^{s_e},q)\). So the rooted question is whether \(W\mapsto G_W\) is injective on forests. As \(\log G\) is additive under union, that holds exactly when the functions \(\log G_t\), for rooted trees \(t\), are linearly independent over \(\mathbb Q\): any integer relation separates into 2 disjointly supported forests. And \(G_W(a)/x^{a|W|}R_W\) is a survival probability for a walk with independent discrete Gaussian steps on \(W\), whose large-\(a\) tail has exponents given by effective conductances from the root to vertex sets. Theorem 44 (ancestor form of shell 2), proved. Root any tree at a centroid, let \(s_e\le n/2\) be the size below \(e\), and let \(N[a,b]\), for \(b\ge2\) and \(a<n/2\), count pairs \(e>f\) of an edge above another with sizes \(a,b\). Then \[B=\sum_{\{e,f\}}x^{n-s_e-s_f}+x^{-1}\sum_e(s_e-1)h_{s_e}+\sum_{a,b}N[a,b]\,h_a\,(x^{-b}-x^{-1}),\quad h_a=x^a-x^{n-a}.\] So \(B\) is determined by \(A\) and \(N\).

Proof. Incomparable pairs have \(b=n-s_e-s_f\). A nested pair replaces this by \(s_e-s_f\), a change of \(h_{s_e}x^{-s_f}\). Below \(e\) there are \(s_e-1\) edges, and pendant ones contribute \(h_{s_e}x^{-1}\). Exact check: all trees with 3 to 14 vertices. A balanced edge has \(h=0\), which is why it can exchange freely.

Theorem 45 (rigidity of the ancestor matrix), proved. Let \(T\) be a caterpillar and \(S\) a tree with the same \(A\) and \(N\). For each size \(w\), the multiset of sizes of internal edges directly below the edges of size \(w\) is the same in \(S\) and \(T\). Non-pendant sizes occur at most twice (with two virtual incoming edges at a balanced edge), and only a size occurring twice can distribute differently. If its 2 edges have children of sizes \(\xi,\eta\) in \(T\) and both children lie under 1 edge in \(S\), that size contributes \(2(\xi-1)(\eta-1)\) to the difference, and \[\sum_vd_v(S)^2-\sum_vd_v(T)^2=2\sum_{\text{gathered }w}(\xi_w-1)(\eta_w-1).\] So \(S\) is a caterpillar or has strictly larger \(\sum d^2\). In particular, if \(T\) has no repeated size and no balanced edge, then \(S\cong T\).

Proof. Remove a balanced edge if present, and treat its 2 ends as 2 edges of equal size, which is consistent because \(d-1=\tau-\sum_c\tau_c\) with \(\tau=s-1\) holds at them too. In \(T\) the poset is 2 chains, and a size occurs once in each chain at most. For a size \(z\) occurring once, \(N\) gives its ancestor sizes, each once, so its parent size is as in \(T\). For \(z\) occurring twice, a size occurring twice above it is an ancestor of both copies. Let \(D\) be the lowest such, and let \(A',B'\) be the singly occurring sizes between \(D\) and \(z\) in the 2 chains. \(N\) vanishes between \(A'\) and \(B'\), so they cannot lie above a common copy. 2 sizes in \(A'\) are comparable, so they lie above the same copy, or the upper one would be above both, and \(N\) forbids that. Thus the 2 parent sizes are the lowest of \(A'\) and of \(B'\), or \(D\) when one is empty, as in \(T\). Summing over \(z\) gives the children statement. Now \(d_v-1=\tau_v-\sum_c\tau_c\) on internal vertices, and a vertex with parent size occurring once keeps its degree. A size occurring twice with children \(\xi,\eta\) changes \((\tau-\tau_\xi)^2+(\tau-\tau_\eta)^2\) to \(\tau^2+(\tau-\tau_\xi-\tau_\eta)^2\) when gathered, an increase of \(2\tau_\xi\tau_\eta\). \(\sum d_v\) is fixed. Exact check: on the 27-vertex pair the formula predicts \(2\cdot5\cdot5+2\cdot1\cdot2=54\), and direct computation gives 54.

Conjecture 46 (impostors preserve \(N\)). If a non-caterpillar has the \(A\) and \(B\) of a caterpillar, it has the same \(N\). By Theorem 45 this implies Conjecture 39. Status: true in all pairs through 18 vertices, where 384 caterpillar-versus-other pairs all have equal \(N\), and in all 6,251 mixed classes with 4 to 8 internal vertices, through 34, 30, 27, 24, 22 vertices. It is a genuine restriction: the term \(\sum N[a,b]h_a(x^{-b}-x^{-1})\) has a nontrivial kernel, since all caterpillar pairs with equal \(A,B\) in that census have different \(N\), and non-caterpillar pairs occur both ways. So the conjecture says the kernel's relations never join a caterpillar to a non-caterpillar. I have no proof.

The kernel. Since \(h_ax^{-b}=x^{a-b}-x^{n-a-b}\), each entry \((a,b)\), including \(b=1\), is a directed edge between 2 exponents, and shell 2 is a flow on the integers.

Theorem 47 (collisions are circulations), proved. Let trees have equal \(A\), and put \(F=\Delta N\) on \(b\ge2\) and \(F(a,1)=-\sum_{b\ge2}F(a,b)\). Then equal \(B\) holds if and only if \[\sum_{a-b=e}F(a,b)=\sum_{a+b=n-e}F(a,b)\quad\text{for all }e.\] For \(e\ge n/2\) the left side is empty, so all antidiagonal sums of \(F\) with \(a+b\le n/2\) vanish.

Proof. Theorem 44, reading coefficients of \(x^e\). Only rows \(a<n/2\) occur, so \(a-b<n/2\).

Theorem 48 (bridge covariance), proved. For any 2 trees with equal \(A\) and \(B\), \(\sum_{a,b}F(a,b)(n-2a)(b-1)=0\). For caterpillars with \(T=Y\sqcup Z\), \(T'=Y\sqcup Z^R\), \[\sum_{y\in Y,z\in Z}\sigma_y\sigma_z\,\min(y,z)\bigl(N-2\max(y,z)\bigr)=0,\] over spin sites. The kernel is the covariance of a Brownian bridge on \([0,N/2]\), up to a factor, so it is strictly positive. Consequently a caterpillar whose spins all have one sign has no partner among caterpillars: all its cuts that are neither balanced nor doubled lie on one side of the midpoint.

Proof. Take the coefficient of \((x-1)^2\) in Theorem 44, or in Theorem 29, using \(g_p\sim-p(x-1)\) and \(h_q\sim(2q-N)(x-1)\). If all signs agree every term is positive, and \(Y,Z\) are nonempty for a nontrivial change of orientation. Exact check: the relation on all 7,832 census pairs, invariance of \(\sum_{e>f}(n-2s_e)(s_f-1)\) on all classes through 16 vertices, and none of the 15,666 caterpillars in collision classes through \(n=30\) is one-sided.

What Conjecture 46 requires. Neutral cuts do not enter the energy of Theorem 29, so doubled cuts may be added to a colliding pair without changing the collision. If a caterpillar with a partner \(T'\) also had an available gathering, with a doubled size \(w\) having no once-occurring size above it and children with \(\xi+\eta+1\le w\), then the gathered tree would have the \(A,B\) of \(T'\) with \(N\) different from \(N(T')\). That would refute Conjecture 46 and might threaten Conjecture 39, since \(T'\) can have larger \(\sum d^2\) than its partner. It never happens in the census: the slack \(\xi+\eta+1-w\) over all those caterpillars is at least 4 through \(n=30\), with minimum 4, 5, 6, 5, 7, 6, 6, 10, 7, 9 at \(n=14,17,18,20,22,23,26,27,29,30\). So Conjecture 46 contains the following sharp question, which I could not settle: does a shell-2 collision force the 2 chain tops to be too large to fit under a common doubled edge? It would be enough to show that a partner forces \(\xi+\eta+1>w\), and that gatherings are the only way to preserve \(N\), which Theorem 45 gives, but the case \(F\ne0\) with no caterpillar sharing \(N(S)\) would still need Theorem 47.

Theorem 49 (reach of a collision), proved. Let 2 non-isomorphic caterpillars have equal \(A,B\), with spin sites split into flipped \(Z\) and unflipped \(Y\). Let \(Q\) be the largest site and \(M\) the largest site in the class not containing \(Q\). Then \[2Q+M\ge N.\] So a caterpillar all of whose spin sites are below \(N/3\) has no partner among caterpillars.

Proof. Nonzero \(\tau_{pq}\) occur exactly on pairs with one site in each class. In \(\sum\tau_{pq}(x^{q-p}-x^q-x^{N-q-p}+x^{N-q})=0\), read the coefficient of \(x^{N-\sigma}\) for \(N-\sigma>Q\). The first 2 monomials have exponent at most \(Q\), so it gives \(c_\sigma=\sum_{p+q=\sigma}\tau_{pq}\), where \(c_q=\sum_{p<q}\tau_{pq}\) and \(c_\sigma=0\) unless \(\sigma\) is a site. Take \(\sigma=M+Q\). The pair \((M,Q)\) is the unique pair across the classes with this sum, so the right side is \(\pm2\). But \(\sigma>Q\) is not a site, so the left side is 0. Hence \(N-\sigma\le Q\). Exact check: no violation on 7,834 census pairs. The inequality is nearly sharp: \(2Q+M-N\) has minimum 1 or 2 at every order through \(n=30\), and equality never occurs in the census, though I have no proof that it cannot.

Corollary 50 (shape of a dangerous gathering), proved. Suppose a caterpillar with a partner admits a gathering, with chain tops \(X\ge X'\) in the coordinates of \(T\). Then \(X\ge N/3\), \(X'\le N/6-2\), and the class not containing \(Q\) has a site \(M\ge2X'+4\). So both classes reach above the entire short chain: the flip cannot be confined to the short chain.

Proof. A gathering needs \(X+X'+2\le N/2\). All sites are at most \(X\), so \(3X\ge2Q+M\ge N\). Then \(M\ge N-2Q\ge N-2X\ge2X'+4\).

This narrows the question: only a long chain reaching past \(N/3\) with a short chain below \(N/6\), and a flip acting on the long chain, could give a counterexample. In that regime the kernel of Theorem 48 is small at \(Q\), since \(N-2Q\) is small, so the second moment does not exclude it, and the remaining coefficients of \(x^e\) with \(e\le Q\) are where a proof or a counterexample must come from.

Targeted search. The spin energy does not involve neutral cuts, so all colliding spin configurations with \(N\le42\) can be enumerated directly, about \(3^{20}\) at the top. No class contains a configuration with \(3Q\ge N\) and a short chain top \(X'\) with \(Q+X'+2\le N/2\). A gathering lower in a run of doubled sizes has \(\xi=\eta\), so all sites lie below \(N/4\), which Theorem 49 excludes. Hence no caterpillar with a partner admits a gathering through \(n=44\). So the only visible mechanism for refuting Conjecture 46 does not occur in this range.

A filter over all trees finds no non-caterpillar sharing \(A\), \(B\) and degrees with a caterpillar at any tested order, including \(n=10,14,16,18,20,21,22\), where there are 1, 20, 66, 200, 564, 564, 1521 impostors at \(A,B\); absence is rigorous given the code, since hash values differ. Shell 1 alone does not give this ordering, so the inequality uses \(B\). Theorem 22 (stable tower series), proved. For a cubic graph and fixed \(\kappa,\nu\), the coefficient of \(x^Vq^{3V^2-\kappa V+\nu}\) in \(L_G\) is independent of \(V\) for large \(V\), and it equals the number of pairs \((v,s)\), with \(v\) a vertex and \(s\ge0\) a height function on \(G-v\), having \[\kappa=6|s|+2S,\qquad \nu=3|s|^2+2|s|S+E'(s),\] where \(S=\sum_{w\sim v}s_w\) and \(E'(s)=3\sum s_w^2-2\sum_{ww'\in E(G-v)}s_ws_{w'}\).

Proof. Since \(E(h)\le3\sum h_v^2\le3V\max h\), an energy of at least \(3V^2-\kappa V\) forces \(\max h\ge V-\kappa/3\), so for large \(V\) there is a unique tower of height \(b=V-|s|\). Expand \(3b^2-2bS+E'(s)\). Exact check: no mismatches against direct histograms on \(K_4\), \(K_{3,3}\) and Petersen. The series gives a meaning to the \(q=\infty\) end. A nonneighbor unit costs \(\kappa=6\) and a neighbor unit \(\kappa=8\), so a tower at a specified vertex reads its adjacency, but the series sums over all centers and placements, and recovering individual adjacency from that aggregate is exactly the unresolved identification problem. Its low levels are weak as a separator, however: through \(|s|\le6\), 15 classes covering 33 cubic graphs on at most 14 vertices remain unseparated, including the late pair. This is not a shortcut to completeness, and I have not derived the promotion poles from it.
