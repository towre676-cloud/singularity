# Targeted literature comparison

Primary sources checked November 5, 2026. This is a focused comparison, not an exhaustive priority clearance. No source below is asserted to prove completeness of the present height series.

## Caterpillar reconstruction from a different invariant

Martin Loebl and Jean-Sebastien Sereni, *Isomorphism of weighted trees and Stanley's isomorphism conjecture for caterpillars*, Ann. Inst. Henri Poincare D 6 (2019), 357-384, DOI 10.4171/AIHPD/74.

https://www.numdam.org/item/10.4171/aihpd/74.pdf

They prove caterpillar reconstruction from the U-polynomial, which records component-size partitions after edge deletions. The shared reconstruction objective is established literature. Their input differs from three energy shells of M, so Theorem 35 would be a result about a different observation map. Neither M determining U nor U determining M is proved here. A priority statement for three-shell reconstruction requires a search for this exact height invariant and its equivalent formulations, not merely the word caterpillar.

## Composition factor reversal

Louis J. Billera, Hugh Thomas and Stephanie van Willigenburg, *Decomposable compositions, symmetric quasisymmetric functions and equality of ribbon Schur functions*, arXiv:math/0405434; Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2.

https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~billera/papers/ribbon_arXiv.pdf

They classify equality of ribbon Schur functions through factorization of compositions and reversal of corresponding factors. This is a direct antecedent for the periodic composition-product examples discussed in Revision 18. The source does not identify their entire equivalence relation with shell-2 equality for M. The monograph's implication from a ribbon equality to shell-2 equality needs its own bridge, while its nonperiodic reflection-symmetric examples need a separate classification. The new-looking spin formula must not be described as a new discovery of factor reversal itself.

## Finite truncation counterexamples and rooted reconstruction

Jose Aliste-Prieto, Anna de Mier and Jose Zamora, *On the smallest trees with the same restricted U-polynomial and the rooted U-polynomial*, arXiv:2002.08186; Discrete Mathematics (2021), article 112255.

https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.08186

Theorem 9 reconstructs rooted trees from the rooted U-polynomial. Theorem 12 explicitly constructs nonisomorphic trees agreeing on a prescribed restricted U-polynomial and differing at the next level. Thus both rooted reconstruction and failure of fixed truncations have close antecedents. Their invariant retains root-component and other component-size variables, unlike G's summed heights and quadratic energy. Rooted-U injectivity does not establish injectivity, or multiplicative independence on forests, of G. Their truncated identities also do not automatically imply the joint energy/volume agreement of Theorem 38. A transfer theorem would need to preserve ancestor constraints and the chemical-potential marking. This is the most directly useful comparison for the next proof strategy.

## Reflection positivity and partition-function matrices

Michael Freedman, Laszlo Lovasz and Alexander Schrijver, *Reflection positivity, rank connectivity, and homomorphism of graphs*, arXiv:math/0404468.

https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0404468

They characterize finite weighted-target homomorphism parameters by reflection positivity and rank conditions. This supplies classical context for sums of products and Gram-matrix arguments in partition functions. Finite height restrictions of our model are weighted-target partition functions; the unrestricted model has infinitely many heights. The finite-rank characterization cannot simply be applied unchanged. The exchange square and its Cauchy-Schwarz estimate should be presented as instances of established positivity algebra. The model-specific separation and rooted-injectivity questions remain to be proved.

## What can and cannot be claimed

The literature establishes several neighboring reconstruction, truncation and positivity results. It does not, in this search, settle the particular M_T or G_W questions. That last sentence reports the scope of retrieval, not proof that no earlier treatment exists. Appropriate labels remain: classical background; new derivation within this project; computed result; or open. A novelty claim needs a fuller comparison of equivalent integer-height, theta, rooted partition-function and graph-polynomial formulations.
