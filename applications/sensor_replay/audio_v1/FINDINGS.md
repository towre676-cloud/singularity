The audio-and-vibration pilot uses real synchronized sensor measurements from the official UFRJ MAFAULDA machinery-fault simulator. It is a prerecorded physical-data replay performed entirely in software. The source provides a tachometer, three underhang accelerometer axes, three overhang accelerometer axes, and a microphone; the diagnostic inputs here exclude the tachometer. The official description specifies 50 kHz sampling, five-second recordings and 250,000 samples per recording. Normal operation, 1.5 mm horizontal misalignment and 6 g imbalance form this deliberately limited three-state catalogue. One machine, one selected severity per fault and the small recording counts preclude claims about new machines, general fault coverage or sensor placement.

The original pilot acquired 24 complete recordings at eight predeclared nominal speed targets. Nearest filename speed selected each recording before any signal inspection. Four speed groups supplied 12 training recordings, two supplied six selection recordings, and two supplied six locked test recordings. Corresponding speed groups stayed together across all three states, and no windows from a recording were split across stages. A protocol was frozen at 2026-10-19 23:16:06 UTC before feature extraction. Fourteen fixed descriptors per diagnostic channel feed a one-nearest-neighbor classifier, standardized only with training data. All 127 nonempty subsets of the seven channels are evaluated on selection data. The exact objective minimizes channel count subject to balanced accuracy at least the full-panel selection score minus two percentage points; canonical bitmask order resolves ties. Greedy forward selection and exhaustive pruning of its output use the same criterion.

The original outcome is negative. Full seven-channel selection accuracy was 6/6, so the required balanced accuracy was 98%. Two singleton masks met it: the underhang axial channel and the underhang tangential channel. Exact selection, greedy and optimally pruned greedy all chose the canonical axial singleton, which achieved only 2/6 correct on the locked test recordings (33.33% balanced accuracy). The full panel and all six accelerometer channels each achieved 6/6; microphone-only achieved 4/6. The other tied singleton achieved 6/6 descriptively, but it was not selected prospectively and cannot be promoted using those test results. Exact optimality of a small development table did not establish physical transfer or an advantage over greedy.

A separate confirmation was then frozen at 2026-10-19 23:18:15 UTC, after observing that failure but before acquiring any new recordings. Its rule retained the union of all singleton masks attaining the maximum original selection accuracy. This fixed two-channel panel used the underhang axial and tangential channels. Eighteen complete, previously unused recordings at six intervening nominal speed targets supplied the new confirmation; the original training data, scaler and classifiers stayed unchanged. The original exact/greedy/pruned singleton, full panel, microphone-only and all-accelerometer panel were also fixed in advance. None of the original test recordings was relabeled as fresh confirmation.

On the 18 new confirmation recordings, the original singleton reached 12/18 correct (66.67% balanced accuracy), the tie-preserving two-channel union reached 15/18 (83.33%), and the full seven-channel panel, all six accelerometers and microphone-only each reached 16/18 (88.89%). The union therefore remained 5.56 percentage points below full, outside the earlier two-point preservation margin. Its two-channel accounting cost is 71.43% below seven channels, but a validated preservation claim is not supported. Microphone-only matched the full result with one channel on this confirmation and is a simpler comparator. These small results demonstrate a genuine selection-transfer problem rather than a proven measurement-saving success. A tie-preserving greedy rule could construct the same union, so there is no exact-optimization advantage here.

Independent verification recomputes every original selection mask using flattened masked vectors rather than the runner's decomposed distance calculation, independently checks the minimum cardinality and all reported test predictions, and checks split disjointness. A second verifier recomputes all confirmation predictions through explicit per-channel distances, checks the union-of-ties rule and confirms that no original recording is reused. Both verifiers pass. Feature arrays, protocols, source member hashes, selection snapshots, model parameters, results and verifier receipts are retained. The total acquired compressed source bytes are 279,004,318 across 42 complete recordings. Individual original CSV members were extracted from official ZIP archives by HTTP range, verified against their ZIP CRC32 and retained SHA-256 hashes; full multi-gigabyte archives were not downloaded or claimed to have been checksum-verified.

The official source is https://www02.smt.ufrj.br/~offshore/mfs/page_01.html . Its public landing page does not state an explicit dataset license, so this evidence package does not redistribute the raw CSV members or compressed signal payloads. It includes small derived feature arrays and the metadata necessary to reacquire the same original members. These observations support continued experiments with broader development coverage and independent sessions. They do not justify buying hardware on the expectation that the present selector has already demonstrated a reliable reduction.

The original protocol metadata say "before decompression" in the freeze-stage field. That wording is too broad: source payloads had already been decompressed as bytes for integrity verification before the protocol freeze. Numerical array parsing, feature extraction and model/selection/test evaluation began only after freezing. The historical protocol and hash remain unchanged, and protocol_errata.json records this metadata correction. The confirmation was frozen before its new recordings were acquired.
