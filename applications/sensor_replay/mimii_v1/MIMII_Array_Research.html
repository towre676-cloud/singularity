What survives when the machine changes?

Eight synchronized microphones, all 255 nonempty panels, and machine identities held apart for training, selection and protected confirmation.

Demonstrated advantage. The exact finite search exposes the complete 255-panel design space, certifies every minimum channel count and identifies when requested erasure robustness is impossible. Every proposed stream reduction comes with a checkable finite optimum and its transfer limits. The predeclared greedy/pruned panel retains 2 of eight recorded streams (75% fewer) and passes the protected type-specific margin at 55.47% balanced accuracy. The exact tie choice fails. All reported panels fail the simultaneous absolute class-recall floor. This finite physical replay does not establish hardware savings or generalization to an industrial machine population.

The full array scores 52.86% balanced accuracy on the 384 protected recordings. The nominal development optimum, microphones 3, 6, scores 50.26% (-2.60 percentage points). Exact selection and optimally pruned greedy tie on minimum channel count under every feasible objective, with matching infeasibility elsewhere. Equally cheap nominal masks differ; the complete table reports each protected outcome. Exact selection has no channel-count advantage over ordinary greedy. Both erasure objectives are infeasible.

The experiment is physical; the design is retrospective

The original MIMII release supplies measured sound from fans, pumps, slide rails and valves, with nominal archive noise conditions labeled -6, 0 and +6 dB. The primary paper scales noise using model-average signal power, so the labels do not assert measured per-recording SNR. The predeclared subset contains sixteen normal and sixteen abnormal recording stems per machine identity, retaining every corresponding noise mixture. IDs 00 and 02 train the fixed per-type nearest-reference classifiers, ID 04 selects one common microphone mask, and ID 06 confirms it. Selection and confirmation each contain 128 stems and 384 mixtures across four machines. The entire dataset is balanced by construction; these are case-control scores, not natural fault prevalence.

Matching stems and their noise mixtures stay together, and an entire machine identity stays in one split. Hidden factory-noise segment identifiers and temporal session boundaries are not published, so independence of factory-noise sessions cannot be verified. One protected identity per type does not establish transfer to a population of machines.

Every comparator remains visible

The two-point nominal contract protects pooled and per-type balanced accuracy relative to the full array. Noise-aware selection adds type-by-noise cells; class-robust selection additionally protects normal and abnormal recall in each such cell. Erasure contracts test every survivor after one or two selected channels are lost, against the original intact-array thresholds. Empty survivors fail. Because accuracy can decrease after adding a microphone, this replay checks all masks rather than assuming monotonic coverage.

What the mathematics certifies

Every nonempty microphone subset corresponds to exactly one mask from 1 through 255. Given the saved numerical predictions, class recalls and balanced accuracies are exact fractions. Exhaustive feasibility and minimization by channel count, then integer mask, certify the complete finite optimum or infeasibility for each declared objective. The existing Singularity robust-coverage engine also analyzes independent per-channel interval separation under empirical development boxes. That conditional multicover theorem is kept distinct from the nonmonotone fused-classifier erasure calculation. Overlap of empirical boxes is failure of a sufficient certificate, not proof that physical faults cannot be observed.

The empirical development-box test finds 12 of 12 type-by-noise class distinctions without any individually separating microphone. Its complete separation requirement is infeasible at erasure budgets 0, 1, 2. This is a limit of the conservative empirical-box certificate, not proof of physical indistinguishability.

With sixteen examples per class in a type-by-noise cell, a single error changes balanced accuracy by 3.125 points and class recall by 6.25 points. A two-point cell margin therefore permits no additional error. Sensitivity at zero, two, five and ten points exposes this granularity. Paired bootstrap differences resample complete stems with all mixtures together, conditional on the four protected machines.

Provenance and replay

The protocol was externally frozen before signals in commit 30132a5f7e332c832dc7ad471937a97b3483aa6f. Selective ZIP64 retrieval acquired 1,536 declared members, totaling 2,852,963,714 compressed audio bytes and 3,932,282,880 uncompressed WAV bytes. Source receipts retain original member identities, offsets, CRC32 and individual compressed/raw SHA-256 values. Whole-archive MD5 values are publisher metadata; partial retrieval does not verify those complete archives. An independent Python WAV and NumPy FFT/Welch route replays every selected raw member and all 159,744 descriptors, agreeing with the production feature extractor to maximum absolute discrepancy 1.4433e-15 under the declared 2e-11 tolerances.

The retained package contains the protocol and two locks, exact source-member plan, feature array, all-mask predictions, complete objective certificates, sensitivity tables and an independent checker. The original MAFAULDA files are preserved unchanged. The recording source is MIMII public 1.0, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.3384388, by Harsh Purohit and colleagues at Hitachi, Ltd., released under CC BY-SA 4.0. The primary paper describes acquisition and mixing. This supervised cross-machine stress test differs from the original per-ID normal-only anomaly benchmark.

No new sensor positions, phase beamformer, hardware savings, energy reduction, latency reduction or field deployment benefit is established by these prerecorded measurements. The demonstrated contribution is a bounded physical replay with an exhaustive, auditable design frontier and explicit transfer limits.
