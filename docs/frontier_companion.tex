\hypertarget{the-three-questions-remain-distinct}{%
\section{The three questions remain
distinct}\label{the-three-questions-remain-distinct}}

The programme asks how to reconstruct larger graph classes efficiently,
whether the fixed-pinning series is complete or admits a full-series
collision, and which formally invisible symmetric-interface directions
actual graphs can realize. A finite prefix collision answers none of
these by itself. The present work supplies additional necessary
invariants and structural constraints rather than changing that standard
of evidence.

For a simple graph G, write

\[
 E_G(h)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(h_u-h_v)^2,\qquad
 F_{G,\lambda}(x,q)=\sum_{h\ge0}x^{\sum h_v}
 q^{E_G(h)+\lambda\sum h_v^2}.
\]

The translation-invariant local series is L\_G=F\_\{G,0\}. The
dominating-terminal interior sequence uses A\_G=F\_\{G,2\}. These are
different objects: the pole arguments for L\_G do not transfer to A\_G.
The rational denominator argument works for both, and the centered-theta
limit specifically requires positive pinning. The tree results concern a
third object, an ordered terminal kernel.

\hypertarget{why-these-are-useful-together}{%
\section{Why these are useful
together}\label{why-these-are-useful-together}}

The new tools inspect different parts of the same population. Pole data
sees the common-translation direction that zero pinning leaves free.
Denominator data sees the largest energy present at a fixed volume, even
though positive real evaluation makes that contribution numerically
tiny. Centered theta data sees the low-energy distribution after its
universal quadratic decay has been removed. Tree minors inspect
relations among several boundary observations instead of treating each
kernel entry as an arbitrary scalar.

This separation matters. At rational q, a reduced denominator is
arithmetic information, not a numerical condition number. At positive
pinning, a large-R theta limit is a necessary limiting signature, not an
exact finite-state model of every coefficient. A positive full kernel is
not a positive coefficientwise truncation. Keeping these meanings
explicit makes the tools useful without turning any of them into an
unsupported completeness claim.

\hypertarget{arithmetic-information-from-one-coefficient}{%
\section{Arithmetic information from one
coefficient}\label{arithmetic-information-from-one-coefficient}}

Let a\_R={[}x\^{}R{]}F\_\{G,\lambda\}(x,A/B), where A/B is reduced,
0\textless A\textless B, and lambda is a nonnegative integer. Let Delta
be the maximum degree and m its multiplicity. Put c=Delta+lambda. Except
for the unpinned edgeless case, the largest energy is K=cR\^{}2,
attained exactly by placing all R units of height on a maximum-degree
vertex. Every other profile lies at least

\[
 g=\min\{R^2,\,2c(R-1)\}
\]

energy units below it. Consequently

\[
 B^K a_R=mA^K+B^g T,\qquad T\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Choose R\textgreater=2 with B\^{}\{2(R-1)\}\textgreater n.~The unknown
multiplicity is then smaller than the modulus B\^{}g. Denominator depth
recovers c, and a modular inverse recovers m. Composite B needs no prime
factorization. This gives a particularly useful checkpoint for a claimed
fixed-pinning full-series collision: it must preserve the maximum degree
and the number of maximum-degree vertices.

The decoder receives n, q and a\_R, not the graph. Independent
enumeration supplies test coefficients. The distinction prevents the
verification from silently obtaining the answer from graph metadata.

\hypertarget{what-limiting-observations-preserve-and-forget}{%
\section{What limiting observations preserve and
forget}\label{what-limiting-observations-preserve-and-forget}}

For positive lambda, set C=Laplacian(G)+lambda I and R=kn+r,
0\textless=r\textless n. Centering h at R/n gives

\[
 E_G(h)+\lambda\|h\|^2
 =\lambda R^2/n+y^{\mathsf T}Cy,
 \quad y=h-(R/n)\mathbf1.
\]

Along a fixed residue r, the centered admissible domains increase to an
affine root lattice. Hence the scaled coefficients converge
monotonically to a theta value. The lost orthant boundary has an
explicit Gaussian upper bound. The graph's order itself follows from

\[
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\log a_R}{R^2}
 =\frac{\lambda\log q}{n}.
\]

Thus an alleged full-series collision cannot hide a change in order by
changing the number of components or by exploiting a short coefficient
prefix. It must also match all the centered residue limits. Conversely,
matching those finitely many limits leaves the exact finite orthant
corrections unresolved. Those corrections remain a possible source of
distinguishing information.

At the working rational parameter q=1/2, the same information can be
enclosed without algebraic roots. Retain the graph-independent factor
instead:

\[
 \Psi_r(q)=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\\sum z_i=r}}
 q^{E_G(z)+\lambda\|z\|^2},\qquad
 q^{-\lambda(nk^2+2kr)}a_{kn+r}\uparrow\Psi_r(q).
\]

Every exponent in this form is integral. Exact rational Gaussian bounds
therefore certify intervals for Psi at every rational q in (0,1),
including one half. Disjoint intervals for a common residue prove
full-series inequivalence. Overlapping intervals are inconclusive, and
equal limiting values still do not prove equality of the original
coefficients.

\hypertarget{reconstruction-by-peeling-an-entire-graph-class}{%
\section{Reconstruction by peeling an entire graph
class}\label{reconstruction-by-peeling-an-entire-graph-class}}

A threshold graph is built from one vertex by repeatedly adding an
isolated vertex or a vertex adjacent to every existing vertex. Within
this promised class, the arithmetic decoder identifies a whole layer
that can be removed. If Delta=0, the graph is edgeless. If
0\textless Delta\textless n-1, it has exactly n-Delta-1 isolated
vertices: its nontrivial threshold component has a universal vertex and
therefore has order Delta+1. Removing k isolates divides the series by
f\_lambda(x,q)\^{}k, where

\[
 f_\lambda(x,q)=\sum_{a\ge0}x^a q^{\lambda a^2}.
\]

If Delta=n-1, the m maximum-degree vertices form precisely a universal
clique. Write G=K\_m join H. The residual graph stays in the threshold
class, and its uniform pinning changes from lambda to lambda+m. Put
\(b_r=[x^r]F_{H,\lambda+m}\). The exact join identity is

\[
 a_R=\sum_{j=0}^R C_j q^{-2j(R-j)}b_{R-j},
\]

\[
 C_j=q^{-j^2}[z^j]
 \left(\sum_{u\ge0}z^u q^{(n+\lambda)u^2}\right)^m.
\]

Since C\_0=1, this transformation is unit triangular. Both isolate
division and universal-clique removal recover the residual prefix
through the original cutoff. Order decreases at every step. The same
original cutoff satisfies the denominator condition at every smaller
order, regardless of the pinning increments. Thus every known-order
threshold graph is determined through the sufficient volume

\[
 R_* =\min\{R\ge2:B^{2(R-1)}>n\},\qquad q=A/B.
\]

This is logarithmic observation volume. It is not an optimal cutoff, a
constant-precision measurement claim, or a general graph reconstruction
algorithm. The decoder receives the exact prefix and the class promise;
it does not inspect a hidden graph. It returns a creation-word witness
and the inverse operations.

At q=1/2, the eight-vertex construction words 1000011 and 1101110 have
the same first coefficient: 81/64 for the local series, or 81/256 with
shift-two pinning. The sufficient volume-three prefix still reconstructs
each graph. This new class therefore goes beyond assuming that the first
coefficient already separates every member. It accompanies the
cycle-interior theorem without resolving fixed-pinning completeness on
arbitrary graphs.

\hypertarget{a-realizability-constraint-with-the-correct-semantics}{%
\section{A realizability constraint with the correct
semantics}\label{a-realizability-constraint-with-the-correct-semantics}}

A tree's unique terminal path factorizes its ordered kernel into
Gaussian edge matrices and positive diagonal branch messages.
Cauchy-Binet then supplies strict positivity of every finite minor with
increasing pin indices. The leading minor also records the path length.
These facts constrain a proposed ordered tree interface far more than
nonnegative individual coefficients do.

There is a useful warning supplied by an actual graph, rather than a
hypothetical pathology: the four-vertex path truncated at interior
volume one has a negative two-by-two determinant at positive x and q.
The full kernel is strictly totally positive. The common height box used
in FR03 is a product domain compatible with the factorization; the
volume simplex is not. A realizability test must therefore certify the
relevant complete coefficient order or a valid box before using a minor
as an obstruction.

These constraints apply to ordered kernels. Averaging a kernel with its
transpose is a different operation, and the new theorem does not
automatically impose the same strict positivity on every symmetric
interface. The surviving symmetric ambiguities consequently remain open.

\hypertarget{literature-and-proof-status}{%
\section{Literature and proof
status}\label{literature-and-proof-status}}

The two classical engines used here are Gaussian Poisson summation and
total positivity of the exponential kernel. A modern primary-source
treatment of theta transformations is Christina Roehrig, \emph{Siegel
theta series for indefinite quadratic forms}, Research in Number Theory
7, 45 (2021), https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-021-00272-y. Kenneth W.
Johnson and Donald St.~P. Richards, \emph{Hyperdeterminantal Total
Positivity}, SIGMA 21, 055 (2025),
https://doi.org/10.3842/SIGMA.2025.055, explicitly identifies exp(xy) as
the classical kernel and discusses composition through Binet-Cauchy.

The graph pole noncancellation, rational denominator decoder,
tree-distance specialization and pinned orthant-limit statements are
derived in the FR documents. These references support the underlying
classical machinery, not a claim that the project-specific results have
already appeared there. A full priority search and proof-assistant
formalization remain separate work.

\hypertarget{fr01-exact-connected-pole-divisors-and-a-controlled-reconstruction-sector}{%
\chapter{FR01: exact connected pole divisors and a controlled
reconstruction
sector}\label{fr01-exact-connected-pole-divisors-and-a-controlled-reconstruction-sector}}

This companion result concerns the complete local series at one fixed
real parameter, not a finite-prefix extrapolation. It is independent of
the NX36 implementation programme. It strengthens the existing
entire-normalization theorem SNG-F39: for a connected graph, every
possible root-of-unity pole is actually present. It also gives a
rigorous, explicitly restricted sector in which poles recover the
component-size population.

The new results are proved below in ordinary mathematical prose. The
Python module implements rational bounds, finite cyclotomic Gaussian
enclosures, and the combinatorial inversion. No proof-assistant
formalization is claimed.

\hypertarget{objects-and-the-question-left-by-entire-normalization}{%
\section{1. Objects and the question left by entire
normalization}\label{objects-and-the-question-left-by-entire-normalization}}

Let G be a finite simple graph on n vertices, let
0\textless q\textless1, and write

\[
 E_G(a)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(a_u-a_v)^2,
 \qquad
 L_G(x,q)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n}x^{\sum_v a_v}q^{E_G(a)}.
\]

For a connected graph, common translation partitions the original
labeled height population into disjoint infinite rays. The
representative of a ray has minimum zero. Consequently

\[
 L_G(x,q)=\frac{B_G(x,q)}{1-x^n},\qquad
 B_G(x,q)=\sum_{\substack{a\ge0\\\min a=0}}
                x^{\sum a_v}q^{E_G(a)}.
\]

SNG-F39 proves that B\_G is entire in x. This proves only that the poles
of L\_G are \emph{among} the nth roots of unity. A numerator zero could
in principle remove a denominator zero. The present first theorem rules
this out exactly at the roots belonging to the connected component
itself.

\hypertarget{the-connected-noncancellation-theorem}{%
\section{2. The connected noncancellation
theorem}\label{the-connected-noncancellation-theorem}}

\textbf{Theorem FR01.1.} For every connected n-vertex graph and every
fixed 0\textless q\textless1, B\_G(zeta,q) is a strictly positive real
number whenever zeta\^{}n=1. Thus L\_G has a simple pole at every nth
root of unity and no other poles. More precisely,

\[
 \lim_{r\uparrow1}(1-r)L_G(r\zeta,q)
       =\frac{B_G(\zeta,q)}{n}>0,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Res}_{x=\zeta}L_G(x,q)
       =-\frac{\zeta}{n}B_G(\zeta,q).
\]

The adjective positive concerns B\_G and the displayed radial
normalization; the complex residue itself need not be positive real.

\textbf{Proof.} Choose a root vertex 0. Every height class modulo common
integer translation has a unique anchored representative z with z\_0=0
and (z\_1,\ldots,z\_\{n-1\}) in Z\^{}(n-1). Its minimum-zero
representative is a=z-(min z)1. Therefore

\[
 E_G(a)=E_G(z),\qquad
 \sum a_v=\sum_{v\ne0}z_v-n\min z.
\]

The last term disappears from the phase at zeta\^{}n=1. If Q is the
reduced Laplacian obtained by deleting row and column 0, then

\[
 B_G(\zeta,q)
    =\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^{n-1}}
       q^{z^TQz}\zeta^{\mathbf1^Tz}.
\]

Connectedness makes Q positive definite: a vector of zero energy is
constant on every edge, hence constant on the connected graph, and
anchoring forces that constant to be zero. In particular this Gaussian
series converges absolutely. Write s=-log q\textgreater0 and
zeta=exp(2\emph{pi}i*k/n), and set alpha=(k/n)1. Gaussian Fourier
transform and Poisson summation give

\[
 \boxed{
 B_G(\zeta,q)=
 \frac{\pi^{(n-1)/2}}{s^{(n-1)/2}\sqrt{\det Q}}
 \sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^{n-1}}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\pi^2}{s}
 (m-\alpha)^TQ^{-1}(m-\alpha)\right].}
\]

Every summand on the right is a strictly positive real number, and the
sum converges. Hence B\_G(zeta,q)\textgreater0. Since 1-x\^{}n has a
simple zero at zeta, the quotient has exactly the claimed simple pole.
The radial formula follows from (1-r\^{}n)/(1-r)-\textgreater n; the
residue follows from the derivative -n*zeta\^{}(n-1). For n=1, Q has
dimension zero, B\_G=1 and L\_G=1/(1-x), so the same result holds
directly. This completes all cases. QED.

\textbf{Why the argument is specific.} The phase is a character of the
quotient height lattice only when zeta\^{}n=1. At an unrelated
unit-circle point, the factor zeta\^{}(-n min z) remains. Dropping that
factor would turn a proof for the graph's own denominator roots into a
false proof for every root of unity.

\textbf{Consequence FR01.2.} The full fixed-q local series determines
whether G is connected. For a connected graph it determines its vertex
count from the complete set of poles. This does not determine the
connected graph's isomorphism type. Full-series equality of two
connected graphs forces equal orders, but the connected fixed-q
completeness problem remains open.

\hypertarget{disconnected-products-and-the-cancellation-obstruction}{%
\section{3. Disconnected products and the cancellation
obstruction}\label{disconnected-products-and-the-cancellation-obstruction}}

Let G have connected components H\_1,\ldots,H\_c with sizes n\_i.
Independence of the original assignments gives

\[
 L_G(x,q)=\prod_{i=1}^c L_{H_i}(x,q)
         =\frac{\prod_i B_{H_i}(x,q)}{\prod_i(1-x^{n_i})}.
\]

At x=1, every numerator is positive by Theorem FR01.1. Therefore the
pole order at 1 is exactly c, with no additional hypothesis. In
particular a disconnected graph has pole order at least two there,
proving the first assertion of Consequence FR01.2.

At a primitive dth root zeta, components with d dividing n\_i each have
a simple pole. But a component with d not dividing n\_i has no
denominator pole there and its entire numerator has not been proved
nonzero there. Such an analytic factor could cancel a pole of a
different component. Thus the tempting general assertion

\[
 \operatorname{poleord}_{\zeta}L_G
 \stackrel{?}{=}\#\{i:d\mid n_i\}
\]

is \textbf{not} an unconditional result of the connected theorem. This
package neither exhibits such a cancellation nor proves that it cannot
occur. Unknown cancellation is kept explicit in the API.

At a given zeta the exact signed order, with zeros counted negatively,
is

\[
 \sum_{i:d\mid n_i}1
  -\sum_{i:d\nmid n_i}\operatorname{ord}_{\zeta} B_{H_i}.
\]

The second sum is the precise missing datum. A finite numerical sample
showing it to be zero cannot establish an all-graph noncancellation
theorem.

\hypertarget{a-uniform-zero-free-sector-from-a-spanning-tree}{%
\section{4. A uniform zero-free sector from a spanning
tree}\label{a-uniform-zero-free-sector-from-a-spanning-tree}}

Define the scalar Gaussian theta value

\[
 \vartheta(q)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}q^{k^2}
            =1+2\sum_{k\ge1}q^{k^2}.
\]

\textbf{Lemma FR01.3.} If H is connected on m vertices, then

\[
 B_H(1,q)\le\vartheta(q)^{m-1}.
\]

\textbf{Proof.} Choose a spanning tree T and orient it away from a root.
The integer differences on its m-1 edges are independent integer
coordinates on the anchored height lattice. Tree energy is the sum of
their squares. Every extra graph edge adds a nonnegative square. As
0\textless q\textless1, q\textsuperscript{(E\_H)\textless=q}(E\_T) term
by term. Summing over the tree coordinates factors the upper bound into
m-1 copies of vartheta. For m=1 it is the identity 1\textless=1. QED.

Since the normalized series has constant term 1 and nonnegative real
coefficients, for every \textbar x\textbar\textless=1,

\[
 |B_H(x,q)-1|\le B_H(1,q)-1
                   \le\vartheta(q)^{m-1}-1.
\]

\textbf{Theorem FR01.4.} Fix an integer N\textgreater=1. If

\[
 \boxed{\vartheta(q)^{N-1}<2,}
\]

then the numerator of every connected graph of size at most N is
zero-free on the entire closed unit disk. Consequently, for any graph
whose component sizes are at most N, the primitive-d root pole order is
exactly

\[
 p_d=\sum_{k\ge1}b_{kd},
\]

where b\_m is the number of m-vertex components. This is a sufficient
small-q sector, not an assertion that zeros appear outside that sector.

The strict inequality matters: an enclosure reaching 2 does not give the
strict distance from zero used by the triangle inequality.

For a simple explicit sufficient inequality, k\^{}2\textgreater=k gives

\[
 \vartheta(q)\le\frac{1+q}{1-q},\qquad
 \left(\frac{1+q}{1-q}\right)^{N-1}<2.
\]

For implementation we use a much sharper rational enclosure. For
K\textgreater=0,

\[
 \vartheta(q)\le U_K(q):=
 1+2\sum_{k=1}^Kq^{k^2}
 +\frac{2q^{(K+1)^2}}{1-q^{2K+3}}.
\]

Indeed each successive exponent increment in the omitted tail is at
least 2K+3. Exact rational comparison of U\_K(q)\^{}(N-1) with 2
certifies the condition. At q=1/16 the implementation certifies all
components of size at most six. At q=1/2 the bound fails already for
size two; this refusal does not contradict the unconditional connected
theorem at q=1/2.

\hypertarget{inverting-the-component-size-population}{%
\section{5. Inverting the component-size
population}\label{inverting-the-component-size-population}}

Assume the full profile p\_1,\ldots,p\_N has been justified, for example
by Theorem FR01.4. Descending inversion gives

\[
 \boxed{b_m=p_m-\sum_{k\ge2}b_{km},\qquad m=N,N-1,\ldots,1.}
\]

Equivalently, with the classical integer Mobius function,

\[
 b_m=\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)p_{km}.
\]

These sums are finite because no component exceeds N. For the component
population (1,1,2,3,3,6), the profile is
(p\_1,\ldots,p\_6)=(6,2,3,0,0,1), and inversion returns exactly two
isolated vertices, one size-two component, two size-three components,
and one size-six component. No multiplicity is divided by an
automorphism count.

\textbf{Corollary FR01.5.} On cluster graphs (disjoint unions of
complete graphs) whose largest component has size at most N, the
complete fixed-q local series is a complete isomorphism invariant
whenever the small-q hypothesis of Theorem FR01.4 holds. The proof
recovers b\_m from the divisor; these multiplicities determine the graph
because a complete component of each size has only one isomorphism type.

For general graphs the same result recovers component sizes only, not
the isomorphism types of their connected components. There is no claim
that a finite coefficient prefix reveals the poles or provides a
completeness cutoff. The theorem uses the full analytic function
determined by the series.

\hypertarget{an-unconditional-all-q-class}{%
\section{6. An unconditional all-q
class}\label{an-unconditional-all-q-class}}

Suppose every nontrivial component has the same size m\textgreater=2,
with any number of isolated vertices. At every denominator root of a
nontrivial component, all nontrivial components satisfy zeta\^{}m=1 and
hence have positive normalized numerators. Isolated factors 1/(1-x)
never vanish. Thus the divisor formula p\_d=sum\_\{d\textbar n\_i\}1 is
valid for this class at every 0\textless q\textless1.

In particular the complete fixed-q local series distinguishes every
graph

\[
 kK_m\;\sqcup\;\ell K_1
 \quad(k\ge1,\ m\ge2,\ \ell\ge0)
\]

from every other graph in that class. The largest primitive pole order
is m; its multiplicity is k; the pole order at 1 is k+ell. Purely
isolated graphs are handled separately by (1-x)\^{}(-ell). This is an
infinite class of disconnected interiors beyond the cycle-union
reconstruction family, but its result concerns the unpinned local series
specified here.

\hypertarget{generic-parameters-in-a-fixed-finite-family}{%
\section{7. Generic parameters in a fixed finite
family}\label{generic-parameters-in-a-fixed-finite-family}}

For a fixed connected H and a fixed unit root zeta, B\_H(zeta,q) is
holomorphic in \textbar q\textbar\textless1. Uniform absolute
convergence on compact subdisks follows by domination by
B\_H(1,\textbar q\textbar) and the Gaussian lattice bound. Its value at
q=0 is 1, since connected zero-energy normalized profiles contain only
the all-zero assignment. Therefore it is not identically zero and its
zeros are locally discrete.

For any fixed finite graph family, there are only finitely many
components and relevant roots. Outside a locally discrete exceptional
set of q in (0,1), every off-denominator cancellation is absent and the
divisor inversion is valid. On every compact subinterval strictly inside
(0,1) the exceptional set is finite. This does not locate it, certify
q=1/2, or exclude rational exceptional values. No such arithmetic
strengthening is claimed.

\hypertarget{exact-finite-gaussian-enclosures}{%
\section{8. Exact finite Gaussian
enclosures}\label{exact-finite-gaussian-enclosures}}

\texttt{anchored\_gaussian\_box} uses integer differences r\_e on a
rooted spanning tree, rather than bounding the heights themselves. It
sums exactly over \textbar r\_e\textbar\textless=H in the cyclotomic
ring Q{[}zeta{]}. There are (2H+1)\^{}(n-1) profiles, checked against a
budget before enumeration.

Let theta\_H=1+2 sum\_\{k=1\}\^{}H q\textsuperscript{(k}2). As
E\_G\textgreater=sum\_e r\_e\^{}2, the absolute mass outside this
difference box is bounded by

\[
 \vartheta(q)^{n-1}-\vartheta_H(q)^{n-1}
 \le U_K(q)^{n-1}-\vartheta_H(q)^{n-1},\qquad K\ge H.
\]

This gives a rational absolute complex-error radius around the exact
cyclotomic partial sum. Different spanning-tree boxes need not sum the
same finite populations; their certified balls nevertheless contain the
same full theta value. Tests exercise this root-choice failure mode. For
K\_2, the character at -1 reduces independently to sum\_\{z=-H\}\^{}H
(-1)\^{}z q\textsuperscript{(z}2), and at q=1/2,H=4 the enclosure
already proves strict positivity by rational arithmetic. A triangle at a
primitive third root is checked independently in direct rooted height
coordinates.

The finite Gaussian calculation is evidence and an evaluable bound. The
all-graph noncancellation theorem follows from Poisson positivity, not
from checking many boxes or observing positive floating-point
approximations.

\hypertarget{scope-relative-to-the-main-frontiers}{%
\section{9. Scope relative to the main
frontiers}\label{scope-relative-to-the-main-frontiers}}

The series A\_H(x,q)=sum\_a x\^{}(sum a) q\^{}(E\_H(a)+2 sum a\_v\^{}2),
used by dominating-terminal pinning, has an absolute quadratic pinning
term. It is entire in x and does not have the translation denominator
1-x\^{}n. Therefore this pole theorem does not prove completeness for
the fixed-pinned A\_H series. It provides a separate invariant for
unpinned L\_H and a controlled class where full-series reconstruction is
proved.

The work does not realize a symmetric-interface null vector, settle
fixed-pinning completeness, extend a finite-prefix cutoff, or duplicate
NX36's proposed parameter-observability and ideal-closure projects. The
surviving questions remain explicit: unit-circle zeros of arbitrary
off-denominator numerators; effective localization of their q
exceptions; full-series collisions in connected graphs; and
reconstruction under quadratic pinning. These are logical next problems,
not completed claims.

\hypertarget{what-information-can-survive-a-rational-specialization}{%
\section{1. What information can survive a rational
specialization?}\label{what-information-can-survive-a-rational-specialization}}

The existing exact machinery retains an energy polynomial at each
volume. If that polynomial is available, its largest exponent is an
obvious source of graph information. A more difficult question is
whether the information remains available after replacing the polynomial
variable by a single rational number. Different integer energy
polynomials can have the same rational value. Indeed, the example below
has two different maximum degrees and an equal first coefficient at the
project's working value \(q=1/2\).

Nevertheless, sufficiently concentrated height profiles leave a
recoverable denominator signature. The signature is not the whole degree
distribution. It is the largest degree and the number of vertices having
that degree. The decoder needs only one rational coefficient, the graph
order, and the declared pinning parameter. It does not receive the
graph, the coefficient's energy support, a marked series, or a second
value of \(q\).

The same argument applies directly to the dominating-terminal interior
sequence already used by the charge interface. Introduce an integer
diagonal shift \(\lambda\ge0\) and write

\[
F_{G,\lambda}(x,q)
 =\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{n}}
 x^{\sum_vh_v}\,
 q^{E_G(h)+\lambda\sum_vh_v^2},
\qquad
E_G(h)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(h_u-h_v)^2.
\]

The case \(\lambda=0\) is the raw local series \(L_G\). The case
\(\lambda=2\) is the existing interior series \(A_G\) for two dominating
terminals. This is the diagonal pinning already present in that
interface, rather than a new observation variable. Define

\[
a_R(G;q,\lambda)=[x^R]F_{G,\lambda}(x,q),
\qquad
\Delta=\max_v\deg_G(v),
\]

\[
m=\#\{v:\deg_G(v)=\Delta\},
\qquad c=\Delta+\lambda.
\]

Every graph in this note is finite, simple, undirected, and has known
order \(n\ge1\). The supplied coefficient is a coefficient of the full
nonnegative-height series. Multiplying by \(1-x^n\), imposing minimum
zero, restricting span, or changing to componentwise normalization
changes the input and is outside the decoder's contract.

\hypertarget{the-concentration-gap}{%
\section{2. The concentration gap}\label{the-concentration-gap}}

\textbf{SNG-R02.1 --- Extremal-energy gap.} Let \(R\ge2\). If \(c\ge1\),
the largest exponent in \(a_R(G;q,\lambda)\), considered as a polynomial
in \(q\), is

\[
K=cR^2,
\]

and its coefficient is exactly \(m\). Every other exponent is at most
\(K-g\), where

\[
\boxed{g=\min\{R^2,\;2c(R-1)\}.}
\]

To see this, expand the quadratic form as

\[
E_G(h)+\lambda\sum_vh_v^2
 =\sum_v(\deg_G(v)+\lambda)h_v^2
   -2\sum_{uv\in E(G)}h_uh_v
 \le c\sum_vh_v^2.
\]

All heights are nonnegative, so the last edge term can only lower the
energy. Placing the entire volume at a vertex of maximum degree gives
energy \(cR^2\). There are exactly \(m\) such labeled assignments. No
division by graph automorphisms is involved.

There are two other kinds of assignment, and both must be considered. A
spike placed at a lower-degree vertex has energy at most \((c-1)R^2\),
hence gap at least \(R^2\). An assignment with at least two positive
coordinates satisfies

\[
\sum_vh_v^2\le(R-1)^2+1
 =R^2-2(R-1),
\]

because concentrating all but one of the units in a single coordinate
maximizes that sum. Its energy is at most \(cR^2-2c(R-1)\). Taking the
smaller of the two guaranteed gaps proves the result.

The minimum in the displayed formula is essential. A large maximum
degree does not make a lower-degree spike disappear. For example, at
\(R=2\) a maximum-degree-four vertex and a degree-three vertex produce
spike energies separated by only four, even though the nonsingle-spike
bound alone would suggest a gap of eight. The implementation has an
explicit regression test for that case.

If \(c=0\), then \(\lambda=0\) and \(G\) is edgeless. Every profile has
energy zero, and

\[
a_R(G;q,0)=\binom{n+R-1}{R}.
\]

This case is handled separately. An edgeless graph with \(\lambda=2\)
belongs to the nondegenerate \(c=2\) case and must not use the unpinned
edgeless shortcut.

\hypertarget{a-denominator-invariant-without-factoring-the-denominator}{%
\section{3. A denominator invariant without factoring the
denominator}\label{a-denominator-invariant-without-factoring-the-denominator}}

Fix a reduced rational

\[
q=A/B,\qquad 0<A<B,\qquad \gcd(A,B)=1.
\]

The integer \(B\) may be composite. For an integer \(u\ne0\), let

\[
\operatorname{ord}_B(u)=\max\{r\ge0:B^r\mid u\}.
\]

For composite \(B\), this is a divisibility depth and should not be
confused with a valuation: in general it is not additive under
multiplication. What we use is only that multiplying by a unit modulo
\(B\) preserves this depth.

For a positive reduced denominator \(D\), define its clearing depth

\[
\kappa_B(D)=\min\{k\ge0:D\mid B^k\}.
\]

If no such integer exists, \(D\) contains a prime absent from \(B\) and
cannot occur in a coefficient of this rational specialization. The
implementation computes the clearing depth without factoring either
integer and without constructing a full power \(B^k\). For \(D>1\), put
\(u=\operatorname{bitlength}(D)\). If every prime factor of \(D\) occurs
in \(B\), then each prime exponent in \(D\) is at most \(\log_2 D<u\),
whereas its exponent in \(B\) is at least one. Consequently
\(D\mid B^u\). Conversely, a prime absent from \(B\) makes that
divisibility impossible. Thus the single modular-power test

\[
\operatorname{pow}(B,u,D)=0
\]

certifies that some clearing depth exists; failure rejects the
unsupported denominator. The predicate \(D\mid B^k\) is monotone in
\(k\). Binary search between zero and \(u\) therefore returns its first
true value. If an explicit maximum depth is declared, an additional
modular-power test at the smaller upper bound detects a limit violation
before the search. The special case \(D=1\) has depth zero after input
validation.

This takes \(O(\log\operatorname{bitlength}(D))\) modular-power queries,
rather than one gcd division per unit of clearing depth. Each modular
power has its own exact-integer bit cost; this query count is not a
constant-time or unit-cost claim for arbitrarily long integers.
Composite \(B\) requires no special case. A retained test with
\(D=2^{20001}3^{12001}\), \(B=12\), recovers depth 12,001, accepts that
exact limit, and rejects both limit 12,000 and an added unsupported
prime factor. For example,

\[
\kappa_6(72)=3,
\qquad
\kappa_{12}(8)=2,
\qquad
\kappa_{12}(18)=2.
\]

Taking a single prime valuation or treating \(B\) as prime would give
the wrong general API.

\hypertarget{the-inverse-theorem}{%
\section{4. The inverse theorem}\label{the-inverse-theorem}}

Choose any \(R\ge2\) satisfying the strict inequality

\[
\boxed{B^{2(R-1)}>n.}
\]

Let \(D\) be the positive reduced denominator of the exact rational
coefficient \(a_R(G;A/B,\lambda)\), and put \(k=\kappa_B(D)\).

\textbf{SNG-R02.2 --- Single-coefficient rational recovery.} With the
hypotheses above,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta=\left\lceil\frac{k}{R^2}\right\rceil-\lambda
}
\]

except that the unpinned edgeless case has \(k=0\), \(\Delta=0\), and
\(m=n\). Once \(\Delta\) is recovered and \(c=\Delta+\lambda\ge1\), put

\[
K=cR^2,
\qquad
g=\min\{R^2,2c(R-1)\},
\]

\[
M=B^g,
\qquad
N=B^K a_R(G;A/B,\lambda)\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
m\equiv N(A^K)^{-1}\pmod M,
\qquad 1\le m\le n<M.
}
\]

Consequently the least nonnegative residue on the right is the actual
multiplicity, not merely a congruence class containing several
admissible values.

\textbf{Proof.} The concentration gap gives

\[
N=mA^K+B^gT
\]

for some integer \(T\): every term below the leading exponent
contributes at least \(g\) powers of \(B\) after multiplication by
\(B^K\). Because \(R\ge2\) and \(c\ge1\),

\[
g\ge2(R-1),
\qquad
B^g>n\ge m.
\]

Write \(h=\operatorname{ord}_B(m)\). The last inequality gives \(h<g\).
The leading summand has exactly divisibility depth \(h\), since \(A\) is
coprime to \(B\); the other summand is divisible by \(B^g\). After
division by \(B^h\), the first summand is nonzero modulo \(B\), whereas
the second is zero modulo \(B\). Hence

\[
\operatorname{ord}_B(N)=h.
\]

No additivity assertion about composite-base divisibility depth has been
used.

Now \(a_R=N/B^K\). For every \(0\le r\le K\), the rational number
\(B^r a_R\) is an integer exactly when \(B^{K-r}\mid N\). Its first
integral value therefore occurs at \(r=K-h\), so

\[
k=K-h=cR^2-h.
\]

Moreover \(B^{2(R-1)}>n\ge m\) implies \(h<2(R-1)<R^2\). Thus

\[
(c-1)R^2<k\le cR^2,
\]

which proves \(c=\lceil k/R^2\rceil\), and subtraction of the declared
shift gives \(\Delta\). Finally reduce the congruence for \(N\) modulo
\(B^g\) and invert \(A^K\), which is a unit modulo \(B^g\). Since
\(m\le n<B^g\), the residue is unique in the admissible range. This
proves the multiplicity formula.

The smallest permitted observation volume is

\[
R_*(n,B)=\min\{R\ge2:B^{2(R-1)}>n\}.
\]

It grows logarithmically with known order for fixed \(B\). The code
finds it using integer multiplication, avoiding logarithm rounding at
the strict boundary. Larger supplied volumes are valid as well.

\hypertarget{what-this-says-about-fixed-pinning-completeness}{%
\section{5. What this says about fixed-pinning
completeness}\label{what-this-says-about-fixed-pinning-completeness}}

For known order and the same declared shift, equality of the entire
fixed-rational series forces equality of \(\Delta\) and \(m\), because
equality includes the coefficient at \(R_*\). In fact, agreement at that
single coefficient is enough for these two invariants. Thus any proposed
full-series collision with different maximum degrees, or different
numbers of maximum-degree vertices, is impossible.

This statement is useful both for \(L_G\) and for the fixed-pinning
interior sequence \(A_G\). It supplies a genuine necessary condition for
future collision searches without confusing a finite observation with
full equality. The conclusion goes only one way:

\[
F_{G,\lambda}(x,A/B)=F_{H,\lambda}(x,A/B)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
(\Delta_G,m_G)=(\Delta_H,m_H)
\]

when the graph orders agree. Equal recovered extrema do not imply equal
series, equal graphs, equal degree distributions, or equal component
structure. No such converse is asserted.

Known order is part of the input. This theorem does not infer the number
of vertices from one coefficient, and it does not compare series with
different numbers of vertices by silently inserting isolated vertices.
Diagonal shift is also part of the input: \(A_H\) and \(L_H\) must not
be decoded using each other's convention.

Let \(G\) be the complete multipartite graph with twelve singleton parts
and two parts of size three. Let \(H\) be the complete multipartite
graph with nine parts of size two. Both have order eighteen. Their

\hypertarget{a-restricted-reconstruction-theorem-beyond-cycle-interiors}{%
\section{7. A restricted reconstruction theorem beyond cycle
interiors}\label{a-restricted-reconstruction-theorem-beyond-cycle-interiors}}

Consider the complete split graph obtained by joining a clique of size
\(c\) to an independent set of size \(n-c\). The word ``complete'' here
describes the full join: every clique vertex is connected to every
independent vertex. The family is a strict subclass of the split graphs
and is not the family of all graphs admitting a clique/independent
partition.

If \(0<c\le n-2\), the clique vertices have degree \(n-1\), whereas the
independent vertices have degree \(c\). Thus

\[
\Delta=n-1,
\qquad m=c.
\]

The case \(c=0\) is edgeless. The presentations \(c=n-1\) and \(c=n\)
describe the same complete graph, so the canonical answer for a complete
graph is \((c,n-c)=(n,0)\). With this convention, the recovered extremum
identifies the isomorphism type throughout this known-order family,
using one rational coefficient at \(R_*\). The same is true using its
pinned \(A_H\) coefficient at shift two.

\textbf{SNG-R02.3 --- Complete-split reconstruction.} Within the
declared known-order complete-split family, either the raw local
coefficient or the shift-two pinned coefficient at \(R_*\) uniquely
determines the graph up to isomorphism. The reconstruction consists of
the arithmetic decoder followed by the elementary degree cases above.

The caller must supply the family-membership hypothesis. An arbitrary
graph can share the same extremal degrees with a complete split graph.
The software therefore does not claim to recognize complete split graphs
from this invariant alone.

This is a reconstruction result beyond cycle interiors, but it is
deliberately limited. These graphs are highly structured; their
isomorphism types are controlled by a single nontrivial integer
parameter. The theorem does not supply a universal graph reconstruction
algorithm or an efficient coefficient compiler for arbitrary dense
graphs.

\hypertarget{computation-reproducibility-and-cost}{%
\section{8. Computation, reproducibility, and
cost}\label{computation-reproducibility-and-cost}}

\hypertarget{follow-on-questions-without-claiming-their-answers}{%
\section{9. Follow-on questions without claiming their
answers}\label{follow-on-questions-without-claiming-their-answers}}

The theorem naturally suggests asking which other extremal statistics
can be recovered after rational specialization. Peeling the leading
energy term from one coefficient is insufficient to recover a whole
degree distribution: lower-energy nonsingle profiles overlap the
contributions from lower-degree spikes, and subtraction must respect
that interference. A valid next theorem would need a second isolation
argument, rather than a repeated invocation of this one.

The coefficient's numerator is as important as its denominator.
Denominator depth identifies the leading diagonal size up to
cancellation by its multiplicity; the residue then removes the
cancellation. This pattern may be useful for other finite lattice
populations with an isolated extremal energy, provided one proves an
explicit gap and a uniform bound on its leading multiplicity. That is a
general method to investigate, not an already established extension to
arbitrary interactions.

For the present fixed-pinning frontier, the immediate contribution is
precise: any equal full pinned sequence at rational \(q\) and known
order must preserve maximum degree and its multiplicity. Candidate
searches can impose these two conditions before expensive coefficient
comparisons. The complete-split family is reconstructible from the
resulting arithmetic data. The larger questions of general completeness,
a genuine full-series collision, and the realization of
symmetric-interface ambiguities remain open.

\textbf{Status.} Proved project specialization with exact finite checks.
The Gaussian total-positivity mechanism and Cauchy--Binet composition

\hypertarget{which-object-is-positive}{%
\section{1. Which object is positive?}\label{which-object-is-positive}}

Let \(T=(V,E)\) be a finite connected tree, let \(a,b\in V\) be distinct
ordered terminals, and let \(0<x,q<1\). Pin their heights to
\(s,t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\). The ordered kernel is

\[
K_T(s,t;x,q)=
\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{V\setminus\{a,b\}}}
 x^{\sum_{v\ne a,b}h_v}
 q^{\sum_{uv\in E}(h_u-h_v)^2},\qquad h_a=s,\ h_b=t.
\]

The terminals receive no factor of \(x\). The kernel retains original
labeled interior assignments. It is not a quotient by automorphisms.
Every summand is nonnegative, and

\[
0<K_T(s,t;x,q)\le (1-x)^{-(|V|-2)}.
\]

The right side follows by dropping every energy factor, which is at most
one. This gives absolute convergence without a height cutoff. For
\(H\ge0\), write \(K_T^{[H]}\) when every interior vertex independently
has height in \(\{0,\ldots,H\}\). This is a Cartesian height box. It is
a different object from keeping only profiles whose \textbf{sum} of
interior heights is at most \(R\).

A kernel is strictly totally positive when, for every \(k\ge1\) and
every pair of strictly increasing nonnegative integer pin lists
\(s_1<\cdots<s_k\) and \(t_1<\cdots<t_k\),

\[
\det\bigl(K_T(s_i,t_j;x,q)\bigr)_{i,j=1}^k>0.
\]

The two pin lists need not be equal. The matrix need not be symmetric: a
tree with an asymmetric terminal placement generally has
\(K_T(s,t)\ne K_T(t,s)\).

\hypertarget{gaussian-edges-are-strictly-totally-positive}{%
\section{2. Gaussian edges are strictly totally
positive}\label{gaussian-edges-are-strictly-totally-positive}}

For one edge put

\[
G(r,c)=q^{(r-c)^2}
      =q^{r^2+c^2}\bigl(q^{-2r}\bigr)^c.
\]

For increasing rows \(r_1<\cdots<r_k\), the numbers \(z_i=q^{-2r_i}\)
are strictly increasing and positive. Removing positive row and column
factors reduces a Gaussian minor to

\[
\det(z_i^{c_j})_{i,j=1}^k,
\qquad 0<z_1<\cdots<z_k,\quad 0\le c_1<\cdots<c_k.
\]

Here is a direct proof of positivity, so the result does not depend on a
numerical minor test. A nonzero real polynomial with \(k\) nonzero
monomials has at most \(k-1\) distinct positive zeros. To see this,
divide by its lowest power of \(z\); differentiation removes its
constant term and leaves at most \(k-1\) monomials. Rolle's theorem and
induction give the claim. If the displayed evaluation determinant
vanished, a nonzero linear combination of its columns would give a
polynomial with at most \(k\) monomials and \(k\) distinct positive
zeros, a contradiction.

The determinant therefore never vanishes in the connected region of
increasing positive nodes. Its sign is constant there. To determine that
sign, bring the nodes together near \(1\), writing
\(z_i=1+\varepsilon u_i\) with increasing \(u_i\). Taylor expansion
gives the first nonzero term

\[
\varepsilon^{k(k-1)/2}
\frac{\prod_{i<j}(u_j-u_i)\prod_{i<j}(c_j-c_i)}
     {\prod_{m=0}^{k-1}m!},
\]

which is positive. Thus every ordered Gaussian minor is positive.

This proof needs distinct row and column indices. Repeated pins give a
zero determinant, as they should. Reversing one pin order changes the
determinant sign and does not contradict positivity.

\hypertarget{the-tree-factorization-and-the-full-positivity-theorem}{%
\section{3. The tree factorization and the full positivity
theorem}\label{the-tree-factorization-and-the-full-positivity-theorem}}

Let \(p_0=a,p_1,\ldots,p_d=b\) be the unique terminal path, so \(d\ge1\)
is terminal distance. Delete its path edges. Every remaining component
meets the path at exactly one vertex. Integrate the heights in these
off-path components first. At each path vertex \(p_i\), this produces a
strictly positive branch factor \(B_i(h)\). Internal path vertices
additionally carry their factor \(x^h\); the terminal vertices do not.

For a common finite box the factorization is

\[
K_T^{[H]}(s,t)=B_0^{[H]}(s)
\bigl[G D_1^{[H]}G D_2^{[H]}\cdots
G D_{d-1}^{[H]}G\bigr]_{s,t}
B_d^{[H]}(t),
\]

where each intermediate Gaussian index ranges from \(0\) to \(H\), and

\[
D_i^{[H]}(h,h)=x^h B_i^{[H]}(h)>0.
\]

When the terminals are adjacent, the bracket is simply \(G(s,t)\).
Positive endpoint factors preserve signs of all minors. Cauchy--Binet
expands every minor of a product as a sum of products of minors, and
every term in this expansion is positive whenever the intermediate index
sets have the required size.

\textbf{Theorem FR03-A (box and full strict positivity).} If \(d\ge2\),
every ordered minor of \(K_T^{[H]}\) of size \(k\le H+1\) is strictly
positive. If \(k>H+1\), its rank is at most \(H+1\), so all such larger
minors vanish. If \(d=1\), the boxed kernel is strictly totally positive
at every order because there is no intermediate path-index bottleneck.
In all cases the complete analytic kernel \(K_T\) is strictly totally
positive at every finite order.

For the last statement, the entries of \(K_T^{[H]}\) increase to those
of \(K_T\), and convergence of entries gives convergence of each finite
determinant. A limit of positive determinants need only be nonnegative
in general, so one additional argument is essential. Fix the
intermediate index set \(\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\) at every internal path
vertex in one Cauchy--Binet term. For every \(H\ge k-1\), that term is
present. Its branch factors have strictly positive lower bounds obtained
by assigning zero to all off-path interior heights. Its Gaussian minors
and powers of \(x\) are fixed and strictly positive. This single term
supplies a uniform positive lower bound for the full determinant. Its
limit is therefore strictly positive.

This is a structural necessary condition for ordered tree kernels. It is
not a sufficient characterization of trees, and this note does not
classify all matrices satisfying it.

\hypertarget{the-first-determinant-coefficient-measures-terminal-geometry}{%
\section{4. The first determinant coefficient measures terminal
geometry}\label{the-first-determinant-coefficient-measures-terminal-geometry}}

Write

\[
c=\sum_{v\in\{p_0,\ldots,p_d\}}\deg_T(v)-2d.
\]

Each of the \(d\) path edges contributes twice to the degree sum. Thus
\(c\ge0\) counts precisely the edges leaving the terminal path. It
counts attachment edges, not all edges deeper inside the pendant
branches.

For \(k\ge1\), take the principal pin minor

\[
\Delta_k(x,q)=\det\bigl(K_T(i,j;x,q)\bigr)_{i,j=0}^{k-1},
\quad S_k=\frac{k(k-1)}2,
\quad Q_k=\frac{k(k-1)(2k-1)}6.
\]

\textbf{Theorem FR03-B (all-order leading-minor hierarchy).} The exact
first term is

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_k(x,q)=
x^{(d-1)S_k}q^{cQ_k}
\left[\prod_{r=1}^{k-1}(1-q^{2r})^{k-r}\right]^d
+O\!\left(x^{(d-1)S_k+1}\right).
}
\]

The coefficient in the box is strictly positive for every real
\(q\in(0,1)\). Consequently the displayed volume is an exact first
nonzero degree, not just a lower bound.

To prove this, apply exterior Cauchy--Binet to the path factorization as
a formal series in \(x\). Every internal index set of size \(k\)
consists of distinct nonnegative integers and has sum at least \(S_k\).
Equality holds for the unique set \(I=\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\). There are
\(d-1\) internal path vertices, so the first possible degree is
\((d-1)S_k\), and at that degree there is one choice at every vertex.
All off-path interior heights must be zero at this first degree. A
branch edge incident to a path vertex of pin height \(i\) then
contributes \(q^{i^2}\); multiplying over the selected indices and
attachment edges yields \(q^{cQ_k}\).

The remaining factor is one Gaussian principal minor per path edge. Its
determinant is explicit:

\[
\det(q^{(i-j)^2})_{i,j=0}^{k-1}
=\prod_{r=1}^{k-1}(1-q^{2r})^{k-r}.
\]

Indeed, extract \(q^{i^2}\) from each row and \(q^{j^2}\) from each
column, then use the ordinary Vandermonde determinant on \(q^{-2i}\).
The negative powers contributed by the Vandermonde cancel the extracted
powers exactly. This proves the formula.

The formal Cauchy--Binet argument is legitimate coefficient by
coefficient: to compute through a fixed degree \(R\), any interior
height above \(R\) is irrelevant. One can therefore first use a finite
box \(H\ge R\), prove the finite identity, and extract the desired
coefficient.

The order-two case is particularly readable:

\[
\boxed{
K_T(0,0)K_T(1,1)-K_T(0,1)K_T(1,0)
=x^{d-1}q^c(1-q^2)^d+O(x^d).
}
\]

Thus the first nonzero volume recovers \(d\). Once \(d\) is known,
dividing the leading coefficient by \((1-q^2)^d\) gives \(q^c\), which
uniquely determines the nonnegative integer \(c\) at any fixed
\(q\in(0,1)\). This recovers terminal distance and the number of
branches incident to the terminal path. It does not recover the shapes
of those branches or the entire tree.

\(h_u+h_v\le1\). The resulting polynomial kernel is

\[

\bigl[1+xq^2(q^{-2s}+q^{-2t})\bigr].

Its determinant on pins \(0,1\) is

\[
\boxed{\det(K_{\le1}(s,t))_{s,t=0,1}
=-x^2q^2(1-q^2)^2<0.}
\]

This is an actual tree, so a negative determinant of a volume-truncated
kernel cannot reject tree realizability. The cutoff couples \(u\) and

x^2(1-q^2)^3.

\hypertarget{what-this-contributes-to-the-remaining-frontier}{%
\section{7. What this contributes to the remaining
frontier}\label{what-this-contributes-to-the-remaining-frontier}}

These results add a nonlinear necessary condition and a geometry decoder
for an important class of realizable \textbf{ordered} interfaces. They
can accompany a future candidate search by filtering an ordered tree
completion, provided that the data include enough volume and the test
respects the stated kernel semantics. They cannot directly eliminate a
candidate living only in a symmetrized interface: averaging a matrix

The natural next problems are to decode pendant-branch statistics from
subsequent determinant coefficients, understand which analogous sign
constraints survive on unicyclic graphs, and determine which ordered
positivity constraints descend to symmetrized data under additional
terminal symmetries. None of these is counted as an implemented NX36
unit, a full-series collision, or a complete reconstruction theorem for
arbitrary pinned graphs.

\hypertarget{the-question-and-the-change-of-scale}{%
\section{1. The question and the change of
scale}\label{the-question-and-the-change-of-scale}}

Let \(G\) be a finite simple graph with \(n\ge1\) labeled vertices, let
\(\lambda\) be a fixed positive integer, and fix \(0<q<1\). Write

\[
 A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 =\sum_{\substack{h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{n}\\\sum_i h_i=R}}
 q^{E_G(h)+\lambda\sum_i h_i^2},
 \qquad
 E_G(h)=\sum_{\{u,v\}\in E(G)}(h_u-h_v)^2.
\]

For the dominating-terminal interface already used in the project, the
relevant pinning is \(\lambda=2\). These are coefficients of the raw
nonnegative-height series. No minimum-height normalization is applied
and no multiplicity is divided by an automorphism-group order.

A large volume forces a large common mean height. The pinning alone
charges approximately \(\lambda R^2/n\) for that mean, while graph
energy is unchanged by common translation. Consequently the original
coefficients become very small even when their fluctuations retain
substantial structure. The right object is the rescaled coefficient

\[
 S_{G,R}(q)=q^{-\lambda R^2/n}A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q).
\]

The rescaled sequence does not ordinarily have a single limit. There are
\(n\) residue classes, because an integer height vector cannot be
translated by a nonintegral common height. Each residue class has its
own affine lattice of fluctuations, its own limiting theta value, and an
explicit boundary error.

\hypertarget{order-is-already-an-intrinsic-full-series-invariant}{%
\section{2. Order is already an intrinsic full-series
invariant}\label{order-is-already-an-intrinsic-full-series-invariant}}

\textbf{Theorem FR04.1 (quadratic coefficient decay).} For every
\(R\ge0\),

\[
 q^{\lambda R^2/n+|E(G)|+\lambda n/4}
 \le A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 \le \binom{R+n-1}{n-1}q^{\lambda R^2/n}.
 \tag{1}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\log A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q)}{R^2}
 =\frac{\lambda}{n}\log q.}
 \tag{2}
\]

Thus equality of the complete pinned series at the same known
\(\lambda>0\) and \(q\in(0,1)\) forces equal graph order.

\textbf{Proof.} Cauchy--Schwarz gives \(\sum_i h_i^2\ge R^2/n\), and
graph energy is nonnegative. Every summand is therefore at most
\(q^{\lambda R^2/n}\). There are exactly \(\binom{R+n-1}{n-1}\) labeled
weak compositions of \(R\), which proves the upper bound.

For the lower bound, put \(R=kn+r\), where \(0\le r<n\), and choose one
profile with \(r\) coordinates equal to \(k+1\) and the rest equal to
\(k\). Every edge contributes either zero or one, so its graph energy is
at most \(|E(G)|\). Its squared norm is

\[
 (n-r)k^2+r(k+1)^2
 =R^2/n+r(n-r)/n
 \le R^2/n+n/4.
\]

Its single positive summand proves the lower bound. Taking logarithms of
(1), dividing by \(R^2\), and using
\(\log\binom{R+n-1}{n-1}=O_n(\log R)\) proves (2). The number
\(\lambda\log q\) is nonzero, so its quotient by the limit determines
\(n\). This last statement is a full-series assertion. It does not
supply a universal finite-volume procedure for reading the limit from an
arbitrary prefix. ∎

The theorem has a useful methodological consequence. A completeness
question formulated for the full pinned series need not assume graph
order is externally provided: the complete series contains it. An
algorithm using only finitely many coefficients must still declare how
it knows or bounds the order.

\hypertarget{the-centered-lattice-and-the-exact-boundary-decomposition}{%
\section{3. The centered lattice and the exact boundary
decomposition}\label{the-centered-lattice-and-the-exact-boundary-decomposition}}

Let \(L_G\) be the graph Laplacian and set

\[
 C_G=L_G+\lambda I.
\]

Since \(L_G\mathbf1=0\) and \(\lambda>0\),

\[
 C_G\mathbf1=\lambda\mathbf1,
 \qquad C_G\succeq\lambda I>0.
\]

For \(R=kn+r\), translate each profile by the integer common height
\(k\):

\[
 z=h-k\mathbf1,
 \quad \sum_i z_i=r,
 \quad z_i\ge-k.
\]

Its centered version is

\[
 y=z-\frac r n\mathbf1=h-\frac Rn\mathbf1\in\Lambda_r.
\]

The cross term vanishes because \(\sum_i y_i=0\). Hence the exact
identity is

\[
 \boxed{E_G(h)+\lambda\|h\|^2
 =\lambda R^2/n+y^{\mathsf T}C_Gy.}
 \tag{3}
\]

The nonnegative-height condition becomes a moving orthant boundary:

\[
 h_i\ge0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y_i\ge-R/n.
\]

Define the complete affine-root-lattice theta value

\[
 \Theta_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 =\sum_{y\in\Lambda_r}q^{y^{\mathsf T}C_Gy}.
 \tag{4}
\]

This is a scalar evaluated at the declared fixed parameter. It is not
being identified with the theta series as a formal function of \(q\).
All sums converge absolutely: positive definiteness bounds them by
products of one-dimensional Gaussian sums.

\textbf{Theorem FR04.2 (residue-class limits).} For \(R=kn+r\),

\[
 S_{G,R}(q)
 =\sum_{\substack{y\in\Lambda_r\\y_i\ge-R/n\ \forall i}}
 q^{y^{\mathsf T}C_Gy}
 \nearrow \Theta_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 \qquad(k\to\infty).
 \tag{5}
\]

For \(n\ge2\) every increase \(R\mapsto R+n\) is strict. For \(n=1\),
the rescaled coefficients and the unique theta limit are identically
one. Furthermore,

\[
 \boxed{\Theta_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 =\Theta_{G,n-r}^{(\lambda)}(q),\qquad 1\le r<n.}
 \tag{6}
\]

Consequently there are at most \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\) distinct
limiting values after reflection.

\textbf{Proof.} Formula (3) gives (5) at every finite \(R\). Along one
residue class the affine lattice is fixed and the lower coordinate
threshold drops by one each time. The domains are nested and exhaust the
entire lattice. All summands are positive, so monotone convergence
proves the limit. When \(n\ge2\), there are lattice vectors with one
coordinate exactly equal to the newly admitted lower height, and the
remaining coordinates can be chosen to have the required sum. At least
one positive summand is therefore added. For \(n=1\), \(y=0\) is the
only lattice vector.

Reflection \(y\mapsto-y\) preserves the quadratic form. If
\(y=z-r\mathbf1/n\), then

\[
 -y=(\mathbf1-z)-(n-r)\mathbf1/n,
 \quad \sum_i(1-z_i)=n-r.
\]

It maps \(\Lambda_r\) bijectively to \(\Lambda_{n-r}\), proving (6). ∎

\hypertarget{a-gaussian-error-bound-with-all-constants-visible}{%
\section{4. A Gaussian error bound with all constants
visible}\label{a-gaussian-error-bound-with-all-constants-visible}}

Put

\[
 T_{n,r}(q)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}q^{\lambda(z-r/n)^2},
 \qquad a_R=R/n+1.
\]

\textbf{Theorem FR04.3 (boundary correction).} With \(r=R\bmod n\),

\[
 0\le\epsilon_{G,R}
 :=\Theta_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q)-S_{G,R}(q)
 \le nT_{n,r}(q)^{n-1}
 \frac{q^{\lambda a_R^2}}
 {1-q^{\lambda(2a_R+1)}}.
 \tag{7}
\]

The error is identically zero for \(n=1\). For \(n\ge2\), it is positive
at every finite \(R\). The formula deliberately drops graph energy and
the sum constraint when bounding omitted vectors, so it is conservative.

\textbf{Proof.} An omitted vector has at least one coordinate
\(y_i<-R/n\). On the shifted integer lattice the first such value is
exactly \(-R/n-1\), followed by \(-R/n-2,\ldots\). Since
\(y^{\mathsf T}C_Gy\ge\lambda\sum_i y_i^2\), a union bound over the
chosen bad coordinate, followed by removal of the zero-sum constraint,
gives

\[
 \epsilon_{G,R}
 \le nT_{n,r}(q)^{n-1}
 \sum_{m\ge0}q^{\lambda(a_R+m)^2}.
\]

Successive exponents differ by \(\lambda(2a_R+2m+1)\), which is at least
\(\lambda(2a_R+1)\). The tail is bounded by a geometric series with the
first term and largest successive ratio shown in (7). ∎

Combining the exact decomposition with positivity of the theta limit
gives

\[
 A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 =q^{\lambda R^2/n}
 \left(\Theta_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q)-\epsilon_{G,R}\right),
 \tag{8}
\]

\[
 \log A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 =\frac{\lambda R^2}{n}\log q
 +\log\Theta_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q)+o(1)
 \quad(R\equiv r\pmod n).
 \tag{9}
\]

There is a quadratic scale, a bounded residue-dependent term, and a
Gaussian boundary correction. A further consequence along a residue
class is

\[
 \frac{A_{G,R+n}^{(\lambda)}(q)}{A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q)}
 q^{-\lambda(2R+n)}\longrightarrow1.
 \tag{10}
\]

These are asymptotic structural identities, not claims that finitely
many nearby coefficients certify full-series equality.

\hypertarget{exact-arithmetic-without-pretending-fractional-powers-are-rational}{%
\section{5. Exact arithmetic without pretending fractional powers are
rational}\label{exact-arithmetic-without-pretending-fractional-powers-are-rational}}

Even for rational \(q\), the scaling factor \(q^{-\lambda R^2/n}\) need
not be rational. An implementation must not silently replace such
algebraic numbers by rounded floats and call the result an exact
certificate.

The centered-theta APIs first choose a transparent exact sector:

\[
 t\in\mathbb Q\cap(0,1),\qquad q=t^{n^2}.
 \tag{11}
\]

Write \(w_i=nz_i-r\). The centered exponent is now

\[
 q^{y^{\mathsf T}C_Gy}
 =t^{\,n^2E_G(z)+\lambda\sum_i(nz_i-r)^2},
 \tag{12}
\]

an integer power of a rational number. Equivalently,

\[
 S_{G,R}(t^{n^2})
 =t^{-n\lambda R^2}A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(t^{n^2}).
\]

The exponent in each centered summand is nonnegative. Thus every finite
sum, interval endpoint, and error bound in the checker is an exact
\texttt{Fraction}. This arithmetic restriction belongs to these
centered-theta APIs, while Theorems FR04.1--FR04.3 hold for every real
\(0<q<1\). The affine-lift APIs below remove the restriction and work at
every rational parameter.

For any positive integer \(b\), define the rational tail bound

\[
 U(b)=\frac{t^{\lambda b^2}}
 {1-t^{\lambda(2bn+n^2)}}
 \ge\sum_{m\ge0}t^{\lambda(b+nm)^2}.
 \tag{13}
\]

For a declared integer half-width \(H\ge0\), put

\[
 T^{\mathrm{up}}_{n,r,H}
 =\sum_{z=-H}^{H+1}t^{\lambda(nz-r)^2}
 +U(n(H+2)-r)+U(n(H+1)+r).
 \tag{14}
\]

This bounds the complete one-coordinate Gaussian. The asymmetric finite
window \([-H,H+1]\) is intentional: the reflection \(z\mapsto1-z\)
preserves it for the pair \(r,n-r\). In particular the two finite theta
lower sums agree exactly, not merely approximately.

\hypertarget{exact-affine-lifts-at-the-working-parameter-q12}{%

A graph-independent change of coordinates also makes the complete
calculation rational at every rational \(q\in(0,1)\). Retain the
residue-dependent scale instead of extracting its fractional power.
Define

\[
 \Psi_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 =\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\\sum_i z_i=r}}
 q^{E_G(z)+\lambda\sum_i z_i^2}
 =q^{\lambda r^2/n}\Theta_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q).
 \tag{16}
\]

This scale depends on \(n,r,\lambda,q\), but not on the graph edges. It
therefore preserves all comparisons between graphs of the same order.
For \(R=kn+r\), the exact increasing approximation is

\[
 P_{G,R}(q)
 :=q^{-\lambda(nk^2+2kr)}A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 =\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n,\ \sum_i z_i=r\\z_i\ge-k}}
 q^{E_G(z)+\lambda\sum_i z_i^2}
 \nearrow\Psi_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q).
 \tag{17}
\]

Every exponent in the sum is a nonnegative integer. Every normalization
exponent is an integer. Exact arithmetic at \(q=1/2\), or any other
rational parameter, needs neither algebraic-number support nor an
approximation to an \(n\)-th root. The original centered-theta APIs
remain useful for expressing the intrinsic Gaussian scale, while the
affine-lift APIs are the natural implementation for exact fixed-rational
experiments.

Reflection now includes its scale factor:

\[
 \boxed{\Psi_{G,n-r}^{(\lambda)}(q)
 =q^{\lambda(n-2r)}\Psi_{G,r}^{(\lambda)}(q),\qquad1\le r<n.}
 \tag{18}
\]

This follows directly from \(z\mapsto\mathbf1-z\): graph energy is
unchanged, and \(\|\mathbf1-z\|^2=\|z\|^2+n-2r\).

Let

\[
 T(q)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}q^{\lambda z^2},
 \qquad
 V(b)=\frac{q^{\lambda b^2}}{1-q^{\lambda(2b+1)}}
 \quad(b\ge1).
\]

The same geometric-tail proof gives

\[
 0\le\Psi_{G,r}(q)-P_{G,R}(q)
 \le nT(q)^{n-1}V(k+1).
 \tag{19}
\]

For an exact rational upper bound, use

\[
 T^{\mathrm{up}}_H(q)
 =\sum_{z=-H}^{H+1}q^{\lambda z^2}+V(H+2)+V(H+1).
 \tag{20}
\]

A signed-lattice window with \(-H\le z_i\le H+1\) and \(\sum z_i=r\)
therefore has omitted mass at most

\[
 n\bigl(T^{\mathrm{up}}_H(q)\bigr)^{n-1}
 \bigl(V(H+2)+V(H+1)\bigr).
 \tag{21}
\]

These bounds drop both the graph energy and the sum constraint after
selecting an omitted coordinate. They are intentionally conservative,
but every endpoint is rational. \texttt{affine\_lift\_enclosure}
implements the lattice window; \texttt{affine\_boundary\_enclosure}
implements the one-coefficient approximation. The retained receipt
checks their intersection for every residue at \(q=1/2\), verifies (18)
exactly, and obtains disjoint intervals for the three-vertex path and
the disconnected three-vertex single-edge graph. Thus the method
supplies a valid full-series inequivalence certificate at the project's
working parameter, without requiring connectedness of the candidates.

\hypertarget{what-this-contributes-to-the-fixed-pinning-frontier}{%
\section{6. What this contributes to the fixed-pinning
frontier}\label{what-this-contributes-to-the-fixed-pinning-frontier}}

If two graphs have equal complete pinned series at the same
\(\lambda,q\), Theorem FR04.1 forces equal order, and Theorem FR04.2
forces equality of every residue-class theta limit. This gives a
hierarchy of necessary conditions:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \text{full equality}&\Longrightarrow (n_G=n_H,\ \Theta_{G,r}=\Theta_{H,r}\ \forall r),\\
 &\Longrightarrow \text{equal quadratic decay rate}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{15}
\]

No converse arrow is asserted. Passing from the full coefficients to the
theta limits removes the moving nonnegative boundary. Equation (8)
displays exactly what was removed: the entire sequence of boundary
deficits. Equal limiting values do not, by themselves, force those
deficits to agree. A prefix collision does not establish equality of the
limits, and equality of limits does not establish a full-series
collision.

This distinction gives the next structural question a precise form. A
genuine full-series collision must match both the centered bulk theta
data and every boundary correction. A completeness proof could instead
explain why matching all these corrections forces a graph isomorphism.
The present result supplies the decomposition and certified access to
its limiting part; it proves neither of those further statements.

A practical use is one-sided rejection. Suppose two candidate graphs
have the same order and fixed pinning. If exact theta enclosures for one
residue are disjoint, their theta limits differ, so their complete
pinned series differ. That is a valid full-series inequivalence
certificate even when an earlier finite prefix agreed. The converse
remains one-sided: overlapping intervals merely mean that this
computation has not separated the candidates.

\hypertarget{the-inverse-problem-and-its-answer}{%
\section{The inverse problem and its
answer}\label{the-inverse-problem-and-its-answer}}

Write

\[
 F_{G,\lambda}(x,q)=\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{V(G)}}
 x^{\sum_vh_v}q^{E_G(h)+\lambda\sum_vh_v^2},
 \qquad E_G(h)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(h_u-h_v)^2.
\]

Here \(\lambda\) is a known nonnegative integer and \(q=A/B\in(0,1)\) is
a known reduced rational, with \(B\ge2\). The raw local series is
\(\lambda=0\); the dominating-terminal interior sequence already used in
the project is \(\lambda=2\). Heights and assignment multiplicities
retain their original meanings. No graph-isomorphism quotient enters the
sum.

A threshold graph is obtained from one vertex by successively adjoining
an isolated vertex or a vertex adjacent to every earlier vertex. Encode
those choices by a binary creation word, choosing its first bit to be
zero. This elementary characterization is classical; see Diaconis,
Holmes and Janson, \emph{Threshold Graph Limits and Random Threshold
Graphs}, Internet Mathematics 5 (2008), 267--320, DOI
\href{https://doi.org/10.1080/15427951.2008.10129166}{10.1080/15427951.2008.10129166},
also \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.2448}{arXiv:0908.2448}. The
arithmetic reconstruction below is derived here; no broad historical
priority claim is made.

\textbf{Theorem FR05.1.} Suppose the unknown graph \(G\) is promised to
be a threshold graph of known order \(n\ge1\). Define

\[
 R_* = \min\{R\ge2:B^{2(R-1)}>n\}.
\]

Then the exact coefficient prefix of \(F_{G,\lambda}(x,A/B)\) through
volume \(R_*\) determines \(G\) up to isomorphism. An explicit algorithm
reconstructs its canonical creation word using only \(n,q,\lambda\) and
that prefix. Every peeling step uses the same supplied prefix budget:
the observation cutoff never grows during reconstruction.

Equivalently, for two promised threshold graphs of the same known order,

\[
 [x^r]F_{G,\lambda}(x,q)=[x^r]F_{H,\lambda}(x,q)
 \quad(0\le r\le R_*)
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad G\cong H.
\]

This is a completeness theorem within a specified graph class, rather
than a report that a finite census happened to separate. Its cutoff is
sufficient; no claim of minimality is made. For fixed denominator \(B\),
it is \(O(\log_B n)\). The strict inequality matters: at \(q=1/2\),
order 256 requires \(R_*=6\), whereas order 128 requires \(R_*=5\).

\hypertarget{why-the-high-volume-denominator-reveals-a-layer}{%
\section{Why the high-volume denominator reveals a
layer}\label{why-the-high-volume-denominator-reveals-a-layer}}

FR02 recovers the maximum degree \(\Delta\) and its multiplicity \(m\)
from the single coefficient at any volume satisfying the cutoff. For
clarity, its mechanism is restated in the form required here. Put
\(D=\Delta+\lambda\). If \(D>0\), the maximum possible energy at volume
\(R\) is

\[
 K=D R^2,
\]

attained exactly by placing all mass on one of the \(m\) maximum-degree
vertices. For a profile with at least two positive coordinates,

\[
 E_G(h)+\lambda\sum_vh_v^2
 \le D\sum_vh_v^2
 \le D((R-1)^2+1).
\]

A spike at a lower-degree vertex instead loses at least \(R^2\) energy.
Both alternatives must be included. Thus every nonleading profile lies
at least

\[
 g=\min\{R^2,\ 2D(R-1)\}\ge2(R-1)
\]

below the leading energy. For \(a_R=[x^R]F_{G,\lambda}(x,A/B)\),
clearing the full energy denominator gives an integer of the form

\[
 B^K a_R=mA^K+B^g T,\qquad T\in\mathbb Z.
\]

Because \(B^g>n\ge m\), the leading multiplicity cannot disappear
arbitrarily far into a denominator cancellation. Let \(\kappa\) be the
least nonnegative integer such that the reduced denominator of \(a_R\)
divides \(B^\kappa\). Then

\[
 D=\left\lceil\frac\kappa{R^2}\right\rceil,
 \qquad \Delta=D-\lambda,
\]

and, once \(K\) and \(g\) are known,

\[
 m\equiv (B^Ka_R)A^{-K}\pmod{B^g},\qquad 1\le m\le n<B^g.
\]

The residue is the actual integer \(m\), not merely a congruence class
left to guess. Coprimality of \(A\) and \(B\) makes the inverse valid,
including composite \(B\). The edgeless unpinned case has \(D=0\) and is
handled separately. This inversion depends on exact coefficients:
floating approximations discard the denominator information that drives
it.

\hypertarget{two-exact-operations-remove-every-threshold-layer}{%
\section{Two exact operations remove every threshold
layer}\label{two-exact-operations-remove-every-threshold-layer}}

The class structure supplies the interpretation of the decoded extremum.
If \(\Delta=0\), the residual graph is edgeless. If \(0<\Delta<n-1\),
all isolated vertices can be removed at once: their number is

\[
 k=n-\Delta-1.
\]

Indeed, after deleting trailing isolated additions from a threshold
creation word, its last remaining addition is universal. That vertex is
adjacent to every nonisolated vertex, so the nonisolated induced graph
has order \(\Delta+1\). If \(\Delta=n-1\), the recovered multiplicity
\(m\) is precisely the number of universal vertices, and those vertices
form a clique.

The corresponding transformations of the series preserve the observation
budget. These identities also explain why the uniform diagonal shift
must be tracked rather than silently reset after a join.

Here

\[
 f_\lambda(x,q)=\sum_{h\ge0}x^h q^{\lambda h^2}.
\]

Its constant coefficient is one, so division in the truncated
formal-series ring is exact. If \(c_r=[x^r]f_\lambda^k\), the residual
coefficients satisfy

\[
 b_0=1,\qquad
 b_R=a_R-\sum_{j=1}^R c_jb_{R-j}.
\]

For universal vertices, write their heights as \(u_1,\ldots,u_m\), with
sum \(j\), and let the remaining heights have total volume \(k\).
Expanding all clique and join edges gives

\[
 E_G(u,v)+\lambda(\sum_i u_i^2+\sum_wv_w^2)
 =E_H(v)+(\lambda+m)\sum_wv_w^2
 +(n+\lambda)\sum_i u_i^2-j^2-2jk.
\]

Consequently define

\[
 C_{m,j}^{(n,\lambda)}(q)
 =q^{-j^2}[z^j]\left(\sum_{u\ge0}z^u q^{(n+\lambda)u^2}\right)^m.
\]

If \(b_k=[x^k]F_{H,\lambda+m}(x,q)\), then

\[
 a_R=\sum_{j=0}^R C_{m,j}^{(n,\lambda)}(q)
 q^{-2j(R-j)}b_{R-j}.
\]

The coefficient of \(b_R\) is \(C_{m,0}=1\). The exact inverse is
therefore

\[
 b_R=a_R-\sum_{j=1}^R C_{m,j}^{(n,\lambda)}(q)
 q^{-2j(R-j)}b_{R-j}.
\]

Negative displayed powers of \(q\) are evaluated as exact rationals.
Their appearance in the transform does not change the underlying
nonnegative energy of actual assignments. At \(q=A/B\), no numerical
interpolation or rounding is involved.

\hypertarget{proof-that-the-algorithm-terminates-and-is-unique}{%
\section{Proof that the algorithm terminates and is
unique}\label{proof-that-the-algorithm-terminates-and-is-unique}}

Every iteration removes at least one vertex. Threshold membership is
preserved by deleting isolated or universal vertices. The initial
condition \(B^{2(R_*-1)}>n\) remains sufficient for every smaller
residual order, so FR02 can be applied repeatedly at the same volume.
The current shift remains a known nonnegative integer. Recovered layer
counts are exact, and both series inversions are unit triangular.
Induction on the remaining order proves that the residual series is the
series of the residual threshold graph after each step.

The empty residual is represented by the formal series one. The
implementation never instantiates a zero-vertex \texttt{Graph}, whose
public contract requires positive order. Reversing the removed layers
reconstructs the binary creation word; its first bit is set to zero
because the first vertex has no earlier vertices. At every branch, the
next layer is forced by \(\Delta\) and \(m\). This proves uniqueness of
the resulting word and completes the reconstruction theorem.

The implementation uses an iterative loop rather than recursive function
calls, so a long alternating creation word does not hit Python's
recursion limit. This is an engineering detail with a mathematical
consequence: there is no hidden finite layer bound in the implemented
peeling procedure.

\hypertarget{a-single-declared-closure-reconstructs-threshold-interiors}{%
\section{A single declared closure reconstructs threshold
interiors}\label{a-single-declared-closure-reconstructs-threshold-interiors}}

\textbf{Corollary FR05.2.} Let a two-terminal block have nonadjacent
terminals, each adjacent to every vertex of an unknown threshold
interior \(H\) of known order \(n\). Use any fixed, known closing
context covered by the existing SNG-F53/F54 raw-series convention. Its
closure coefficients through the same cutoff \(R_*\) reconstruct \(H\)
up to isomorphism.

Indeed, \texttt{charges.dominating\_sequence} is exactly

\[
 A_H(x,q)=F_{H,2}(x,q).
\]

The implemented \texttt{closure\_operator} gives \(Z_C=T_C A_H\), with
\(T_C\) independent of the interior graph, lower triangular and unit
diagonal. Its forward-substitution inverse recovers the \(A_H\) prefix
at the original volume budget. Applying Theorem FR05.1 with
\(\lambda=2\) completes the reconstruction. Consequently, two
nonisomorphic threshold interiors of equal known order are distinguished
by \textbf{each} such declared single closure by volume \(R_*\); no
search over closing contexts is required. This statement concerns the
proved raw closure operator, not an arbitrary renormalization, unproved
observation chart or unspecified experimental language. It reuses the
existing transport theorem rather than introducing another context
compiler.

\hypertarget{fr01-connected-pole-divisors}{%
\section{FR01: connected pole
divisors}\label{fr01-connected-pole-divisors}}

\textbf{Verdict: sound, subject to the stated connectedness and
positive-parameter hypotheses.} Let \(G\) be connected of order \(n\),
\(0<q<1\), and \(B_G=(1-x^n)L_G\). The previously established entire
continuation of \(B_G\) is used, rather than asserted as a new result.
At \(\zeta^n=1\), translation classes of integral heights can be
represented either by their minimum-zero representative or by an
anchored representative \(u\in\mathbb Z^{n-1}\). The volume phase is
independent of the representative because translation changes volume by
a multiple of \(n\). Therefore

\[
 B_G(\zeta,q)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb Z^{n-1}}
 \exp(-\beta u^TQ u)\exp(2\pi i\alpha^Tu),\qquad \beta=-\log q>0,
\]

where \(Q\) is the positive-definite reduced graph Laplacian and
\(\alpha=(k/n)\mathbf1\) when \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i k/n}\). Poisson
summation gives

\[
 B_G(\zeta,q)=\frac{(\pi/\beta)^{(n-1)/2}}{\sqrt{\det Q}}
 \sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^{n-1}}
 \exp\!\left[-\frac{\pi^2}{\beta}(m-\alpha)^TQ^{-1}(m-\alpha)\right]>0.
\]

The absolute convergence and strict positivity are immediate from
positive definiteness. Consequently every \(n\)-th root is a genuine
simple pole of \(L_G\), with residue \(-\zeta B_G(\zeta,q)/n\). Order
one is covered by \(B_G=1\).

\textbf{The disconnected caveat is essential.} A different component
numerator might vanish at a root that is not one of its own denominator
roots. A sum of denominator pole orders is therefore not established at
unrestricted positive \(q\). The implementation correctly refuses the
general claim. The same-size component case is safe, including arbitrary
isolated vertices, because every nontrivial component has the same
denominator-root set and every isolated numerator is one.

The uniform zero-free certificate is sound: a spanning-tree difference
parametrization gives

\[
 \sup_{|x|\le1}|B_H(x,q)-1|
 \le \vartheta(q)^{|H|-1}-1,
 \quad \vartheta(q)=\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}q^{z^2}.
\]

Thus any rigorous upper bound \(U\ge\vartheta(q)\) with \(U^{N-1}<2\)
excludes all unit-disc numerator zeros for all connected components of
order at most \(N\). The scalar theta tail bound used in the code
follows from successive exponent increments at least \(2T+3\) beyond
index \(T+1\). Gaussian-box errors likewise dominate omitted
spanning-tree difference assignments. Cyclotomic coefficient vectors are
exact partial sums; their absolute-error bound concerns evaluation on
the complex unit circle, not a coordinatewise positivity assertion.

\hypertarget{fr02-denominator-reconstruction}{%
\section{FR02: denominator
reconstruction}\label{fr02-denominator-reconstruction}}

\textbf{Verdict: sound, including composite denominator bases and
uniform pinning.} For \(q=A/B\) in reduced form, \(0<A<B\), diagonal
shift \(\lambda\ge0\) an integer, and \(\Lambda=\Delta+\lambda>0\), the
maximum energy at volume \(R\ge2\) is

\[
 K=\Lambda R^2,
\]

achieved precisely by spikes on the \(m\) maximum-degree vertices. Every
other profile has energy at most \(K-g\), with the valid universal gap

\[
 g=\min\{R^2,\,2\Lambda(R-1)\}.
\]

The minimum is necessary: a spike at a lower-degree vertex has gap only
\(R^2\), which need not exceed \(2\Lambda(R-1)\). Non-spikes are bounded
using \(E_G(h)+\lambda\sum h_i^2\le\Lambda\sum h_i^2\) and
\(\sum h_i^2\le(R-1)^2+1\).

With \(B^{2(R-1)}>n\), we have \(B^g>n\ge m\), so

\[
 B^K a_R=mA^K+B^gT,\qquad T\in\mathbb Z.
\]

If \(\operatorname{ord}_B(m)=\max\{e:B^e\mid m\}\), then the least
denominator-clearing depth \(k\) of \(a_R\) is exactly
\(K-\operatorname{ord}_B(m)\). This does \textbf{not} require \(B\) to
be prime. Prime-by-prime, it is the identity

\[
 \max_{p\mid B}\left\lceil\frac{K v_p(B)-v_p(m)}{v_p(B)}\right\rceil
 =K-\min_{p\mid B}\left\lfloor\frac{v_p(m)}{v_p(B)}\right\rfloor.
\]

Because \(\operatorname{ord}_B(m)<R^2\), the decoder recovers

\[
 \Delta=\left\lceil\frac{k}{R^2}\right\rceil-\lambda,
 \qquad
 m\equiv B^K a_R\,A^{-K}\pmod{B^g},\quad 1\le m\le n<B^g.
\]

The residue therefore determines \(m\) uniquely. The unpinned edgeless
case \(\Lambda=0\) has integer coefficient equal to the number of
compositions and must be treated separately; the implementation does so.
Pinned edgeless graphs with \(\lambda=2\) instead have
\(\Lambda=2,m=n\), and are handled by the general branch.

An independent implementation, written directly from compositions and
graph edge energies rather than the new decoder, checked \textbf{450
exact cases}: every labeled graph of orders one through four,
\(q=1/2,2/3,5/6\), and shifts zero and two. Every recovered maximum
degree and multiplicity agreed. This is finite corroboration of the
theorem, not a substitute for the gap proof.

The new module's input validation and final modular-divisibility
clearing procedure were inspected. For denominator D\textgreater1, every
supported prime exponent is smaller than D.bit\_length(), so the
modular-power support test uses a valid universal upper bound;
divisibility is monotone and binary search returns the exact least
clearing depth. The optional maximum-depth bound and D=1 case are
handled correctly. The caller supplies graph order, family membership
where used, and the actual coefficient; the decoder does not prove
coefficient realizability. A logarithmic observation volume does not
imply a cheap oracle for computing that coefficient.

\hypertarget{fr03-ordered-tree-positivity-and-distance}{%
\section{FR03: ordered tree positivity and
distance}\label{fr03-ordered-tree-positivity-and-distance}}

\textbf{Verdict: sound for the full ordered kernel and for product
height boxes, with explicit failure under a simplex volume truncation.}
For a tree, integrate every off-path branch into positive diagonal
messages. The remaining terminal path is a product of Gaussian edge
kernels and positive diagonal weights. The Gaussian factor is

\[
 q^{(s-t)^2}=q^{s^2}q^{t^2}\exp(2\beta st),\qquad \beta=-\log q>0.
\]

The exponential kernel is strictly totally positive. Cauchy--Binet
preserves positivity under finite products with positive diagonals. A
common box \(0\le h_v\le H\) has positive minors of order at most
\(H+1\) when an internal path vertex exists. Increasing \(H\) gives the
full kernel for \(0<x,q<1\); strict positivity persists because a fixed
positive intermediate-index contribution supplies a uniform positive
lower bound. Absolute convergence follows from domination by
\((1-x)^{-(n-2)}\).

For consecutive terminal pins \(0,\ldots,k-1\), let \(d\) be terminal
distance, \(c\) the number of edges leaving their unique path,
\(S=k(k-1)/2\), and \(T=k(k-1)(2k-1)/6\). Exterior Cauchy--Binet has the
unique minimum-volume intermediate index set \(\{0,\ldots,k-1\}\) at
each internal path vertex. The Gaussian principal determinant is a
Vandermonde product, giving the full hierarchy

\[
 \det[K(i,j)]_{i,j=0}^{k-1}
 =x^{(d-1)S}q^{cT}
 \left[\prod_{r=1}^{k-1}(1-q^{2r})^{k-r}\right]^d
 +O\!\left(x^{(d-1)S+1}\right).
\]

In particular the two-by-two determinant starts at volume \(d-1\), with
coefficient \(q^c(1-q^2)^d\), so its first nonzero degree recovers
terminal distance. Off-path contributions enter the leading coefficient
only through the number of incident branch edges.

Two qualifications must remain explicit. First, an average of an ordered
kernel and its transpose is not automatically totally positive, so this
theorem is not directly a constraint on an arbitrary symmetrized
interface. Second, global volume truncation can destroy positivity: for
the four-vertex path truncated at interior volume one,

\[
 K_{\le1}(s,t)=q^{s^2+t^2}\{1+xq^2(q^{-2s}+q^{-2t})\},
\]

and its zero/one minor is \(-x^2q^2(1-q^2)^2<0\). This is a determinant
of a truncated kernel, not the correctly truncated determinant of the
full kernel. In particular its degree-two coefficient omits kernel
degree-two contributions.

The path-message implementation was inspected. It preserves raw
interior-volume weighting, leaves terminals unweighted by \(x\), and
boxes every interior coordinate separately. Its preflight budget bounds
scalar summands, not arbitrary-precision bit complexity.

\hypertarget{fr04-centered-pinned-theta-limits}{%
\section{FR04: centered pinned theta
limits}\label{fr04-centered-pinned-theta-limits}}

\textbf{Verdict on the mathematical statement: sound.} For a connected
or disconnected finite graph, uniform real pinning \(\lambda>0\) makes
\(L_G+\lambda I\) positive definite; connectedness is unnecessary. Write
\(R=kn+r\), \(0\le r<n\), and \(z=h-k\mathbf1\). Then \(\sum z_i=r\),
\(z_i\ge-k\), and

\[
 q^{-\lambda R^2/n}A_R
 =\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n,\ \sum z_i=r\\z_i\ge-k}}
 q^{E_G(z)+\lambda\sum_i(z_i-r/n)^2}.
\]

The summation domains increase with \(k\), so the normalized sequence
converges monotonically to the affine root-lattice theta series on the
sum-zero coset. Reflection gives residue symmetry
\(r\leftrightarrow n-r\), using \(z\mapsto\mathbf1-z\) for \(r>0\) and
\(z\mapsto-z\) for \(r=0\). Positive pinning controls Gaussian tails
without a connectivity hypothesis.

Balanced profiles and the composition count give

\[
 q^{\lambda R^2/n+|E(G)|+\lambda n/4}
 \le A_R\le {R+n-1\choose n-1}q^{\lambda R^2/n},
 \qquad
 \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\log A_R}{R^2}=\frac{\lambda\log q}{n}.
\]

Thus the complete pinned series at known \(q,\lambda\) determines graph
order, independently of a supplied order metadata field. Equality of the
complete series implies equality of the affine theta limit packet;
equality of the packet alone is not proved sufficient for
complete-series equality. Exact rational arithmetic with \(q=t^{n^2}\)
gives integral exponents when \(\lambda\) is an integer; arbitrary
positive real \(\lambda\) is a theorem-level parameter, not
automatically an exact-rational runtime input.

The FR04 implementation was independently inspected after it became
available. Its reflection-friendly box \([-H,H+1]\) has the correct
positive and negative first omitted Gaussian indices; the boundary
enclosure uses the exact first omitted centered magnitude \(R+n\).
Twenty-eight additional exact cross-checks on connected and disconnected
graphs verified box nesting against a larger direct lattice window and
checked subsequent residue-subsequence values against the boundary
enclosure. All 32 then-current unit tests across FR01--FR04 passed with
the source package on the Python path. These checks concern exact
arithmetic and enclosure containment, not an empirical proof of the
limiting theorem.

\hypertarget{fr04-rational-affine-lift-at-the-working-parameter}{%
\subsection{FR04 rational affine lift at the working
parameter}\label{fr04-rational-affine-lift-at-the-working-parameter}}

The additional affine-lift APIs remove any need to choose a special
rational parameter. For every rational \(0<q<1\) and integer pinning
\(\lambda>0\), define

\[
 \Psi_r(q)=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\\sum z_i=r}}
 q^{E_G(z)+\lambda\|z\|^2}
 =q^{\lambda r^2/n}\Theta_r(q).
\]

For \(R=kn+r\), the exact rational normalized coefficient satisfies

\[
 q^{-\lambda(nk^2+2kr)}A_R(q)\uparrow\Psi_r(q).
\]

All exponents in the finite arithmetic are integers, including at the
working parameter \(q=1/2\). Reflection gives
\(\Psi_{n-r}=q^{\lambda(n-2r)}\Psi_r\) for canonical residues
\(1\le r<n\). Residue zero reflects to charge \(n\), outside that
canonical packet, so it should not be silently identified with a second
residue-zero value.

The implementation uses the reflection-friendly product box
\([-H,H+1]\). Its omitted one-coordinate positive and negative Gaussian
tails start at magnitudes \(H+2\) and \(H+1\), respectively, exactly as
coded. For a coefficient at \(R=kn+r\), the missing negative-coordinate
tail starts at magnitude \(k+1\). Dropping the charge constraint and
using a union bound with a rigorous one-dimensional Gaussian upper bound
therefore gives the stated rational enclosure. A further \textbf{90
independent exact cases} across connected and disconnected graphs,
\(q=1/2,2/3,5/6\), and pinning one and two checked nested boxes,
boundary containment, subsequent normalized coefficients and reflected
finite-box sums. No new runtime or proof correction was required.

\hypertarget{fr05-promised-threshold-graph-reconstruction}{%
\section{FR05: promised threshold-graph
reconstruction}\label{fr05-promised-threshold-graph-reconstruction}}

\textbf{Verdict on the inverse theorem: sound for the promised threshold
family.} Fix a known order \(n\), rational \(q=A/B\in(0,1)\), and
integer diagonal shift \(\lambda\ge0\). Choose one original volume
\(R_*\ge2\) with \(B^{2(R_*-1)}>n\), and supply the coefficient prefix
through \(R_*\). Every recursive smaller order satisfies that same
cutoff, even as the diagonal shift increases.

A threshold graph has at most one nontrivial connected component, and
that component has a universal vertex. Therefore if \(0<\Delta<n-1\),
the number of isolated vertices is exactly \(k=n-\Delta-1\). Removing
them divides the prefix by the unit series

\[
 f_\lambda(x)^k,\qquad f_\lambda(x)=\sum_{h\ge0}q^{\lambda h^2}x^h.
\]

If \(\Delta=n-1\), the multiplicity \(m\) recovered by FR02 is exactly
the number of universal vertices. Write \(G=K_m\vee H\), let \(u\) be
the universal-clique heights with sum \(j\), and let the residual
heights have sum \(S\). The exact energy identity is

\[
 E_G+\lambda\|h,u\|^2
 =E_H+(m+\lambda)\|h\|^2
 +(n+\lambda)\|u\|^2-j^2-2jS.
\]

Hence, with

\[
 C_{m,j}=q^{-j^2}[z^j]
 \left(\sum_{u\ge0}q^{(n+\lambda)u^2}z^u\right)^m,
\]

and \(b_s=[x^s]F_{H,\lambda+m}\), the observed coefficients satisfy

\[
 a_R=\sum_{j=0}^R C_{m,j}q^{-2j(R-j)}b_{R-j},\qquad C_{m,0}=1.
\]

This is a unit lower-triangular transformation, so the entire required
residual prefix is recovered recursively without increasing the
observation volume. The residual graph remains threshold. The unpinned
edgeless branch, the one-vertex branch, and the empty residual must be
handled explicitly; the empty residual has formal series one and is not
represented by the nonempty-graph constructor.

An independent implementation checked \textbf{378 exact cases}, covering
all binary threshold constructions through six vertices,
\(q=1/2,2/3,5/6\), and initial shifts zero and two. At each elimination
step the transformed prefix was compared against direct weak-composition
enumeration of the actual residual graph with its updated pinning. Every
identity and decoded layer size agreed. The NX36 handoff was searched
for threshold-graph reconstruction; this specific inverse theorem and
its implementation are not among those packets.

The final implementation was inspected, including the iterative
layer-peeling loop, canonical creation-word assembly, and exact empty
residual check. Its original recursive depth limitation was removed
before integration. All \textbf{42} unit tests across FR01--FR05 passed
in the final scoped review.

The theorem proves completeness of this finite prefix \textbf{inside the
promised threshold family}, rather than general graph completeness. The
coefficient-computation oracle remains a separate cost. Series
multiplication and inversion use \(O(nR_*^2)\) arithmetic operations in
the direct implementation. The finalized FR02 helper replaces repeated
gcd clearing with logarithmically many modular-divisibility tests in
denominator bit length. Those modular tests and exact rational bit sizes
must still be accounted for separately from the convolution count.

\hypertarget{fr05-corollary-one-known-raw-closure-reconstructs-threshold-interiors}{%
\subsection{FR05 corollary: one known raw closure reconstructs threshold
interiors}\label{fr05-corollary-one-known-raw-closure-reconstructs-threshold-interiors}}

The corollary was checked against the existing \texttt{charges.py}
contract. Let the two nonadjacent terminals both dominate an interior
\(H\) of known order \(n\). Its compressed sequence is exactly
\(A_H=F_{H,2}\). For any supported known closing context \(C\), attached
through those terminals with disjoint interiors, the existing operator
satisfies

\[
 Z_C=T_C A_H,\qquad T_C\text{ unit lower triangular},
\]

and depends on \(C,n,q\), not the interior edges. Here \(Z_C\) is the
\textbf{raw local series} of the glued graph. Its prefix through \(R_*\)
recovers the same prefix of \(A_H\) by forward substitution. FR05 then
reconstructs \(H\) in the promised threshold family. Consequently each
one such context distinguishes every pair of nonisomorphic threshold
interiors of the same known order by that volume cutoff. A terminal edge
in the context is permitted because the dominating block's terminal edge
is absent. No conversion to a minimum-zero normalized series is implicit
in this corollary, and it does not establish reconstruction for
arbitrary interiors. An independent end-to-end check counted raw
closed-graph profiles directly for all threshold creation words through
five interior vertices and three contexts (empty terminals, terminal
edge, and one shared neighbor), then used the existing triangular
inverse and FR05: all \textbf{93} cases reconstructed the original
threshold interior.
