1 Frontier companion: arithmetic, poles, and positive interfaces

Release scope. This is a separate research companion to the published v0.4.0 baseline, not an implementation of NX36. It adds four narrowly stated mathematical tools, exact executable checks, and a map of their implications. The detailed proofs are the four FR documents assembled after this introduction in the companion manuscript. They have ordinary mathematical proofs and exact computational checks; no new Lean coverage is claimed.

1.1 The three questions remain distinct

The programme asks how to reconstruct larger graph classes efficiently, whether the fixed-pinning series is complete or admits a full-series collision, and which formally invisible symmetric-interface directions actual graphs can realize. A finite prefix collision answers none of these by itself. The present work supplies additional necessary invariants and structural constraints rather than changing that standard of evidence.

For a simple graph G, write

EG(h)=∑uv∈E(G)(hu−hv)2,FG,λ(x,q)=∑h≥0x∑hvqEG(h)+λ∑hv2. E_G(h)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(h_u-h_v)^2,\qquad F_{G,\lambda}(x,q)=\sum_{h\ge0}x^{\sum h_v} q^{E_G(h)+\lambda\sum h_v^2}.

The translation-invariant local series is L_G=F_{G,0}. The dominating-terminal interior sequence uses A_G=F_{G,2}. These are different objects: the pole arguments for L_G do not transfer to A_G. The rational denominator argument works for both, and the centered-theta limit specifically requires positive pinning. The tree results concern a third object, an ordered terminal kernel.

1.2 Why these are useful together

The new tools inspect different parts of the same population. Pole data sees the common-translation direction that zero pinning leaves free. Denominator data sees the largest energy present at a fixed volume, even though positive real evaluation makes that contribution numerically tiny. Centered theta data sees the low-energy distribution after its universal quadratic decay has been removed. Tree minors inspect relations among several boundary observations instead of treating each kernel entry as an arbitrary scalar.

This separation matters. At rational q, a reduced denominator is arithmetic information, not a numerical condition number. At positive pinning, a large-R theta limit is a necessary limiting signature, not an exact finite-state model of every coefficient. A positive full kernel is not a positive coefficientwise truncation. Keeping these meanings explicit makes the tools useful without turning any of them into an unsupported completeness claim.

1.3 Arithmetic information from one coefficient

Let a_R=[x^R]F_{G,}(x,A/B), where A/B is reduced, 0<A<B, and lambda is a nonnegative integer. Let Delta be the maximum degree and m its multiplicity. Put c=Delta+lambda. Except for the unpinned edgeless case, the largest energy is K=cR^2, attained exactly by placing all R units of height on a maximum-degree vertex. Every other profile lies at least

g=min{R2,2c(R−1)} g=\min\{R^2,\,2c(R-1)\}

energy units below it. Consequently

BKaR=mAK+BgT,T∈ℤ. B^K a_R=mA^K+B^g T,\qquad T\in\mathbb Z.

Choose R>=2 with B^{2(R-1)}>n. The unknown multiplicity is then smaller than the modulus B^g. Denominator depth recovers c, and a modular inverse recovers m. Composite B needs no prime factorization. This gives a particularly useful checkpoint for a claimed fixed-pinning full-series collision: it must preserve the maximum degree and the number of maximum-degree vertices.

The decoder receives n, q and a_R, not the graph. Independent enumeration supplies test coefficients. The distinction prevents the verification from silently obtaining the answer from graph metadata.

1.4 What limiting observations preserve and forget

For positive lambda, set C=Laplacian(G)+lambda I and R=kn+r, 0<=r<n. Centering h at R/n gives

EG(h)+λ∥h∥2=λR2/n+y𝖳Cy,y=h−(R/n)𝟏. E_G(h)+\lambda\|h\|^2 =\lambda R^2/n+y^{\mathsf T}Cy, \quad y=h-(R/n)\mathbf1.

Along a fixed residue r, the centered admissible domains increase to an affine root lattice. Hence the scaled coefficients converge monotonically to a theta value. The lost orthant boundary has an explicit Gaussian upper bound. The graph’s order itself follows from

limR→∞logaRR2=λlogqn. \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\log a_R}{R^2} =\frac{\lambda\log q}{n}.

Thus an alleged full-series collision cannot hide a change in order by changing the number of components or by exploiting a short coefficient prefix. It must also match all the centered residue limits. Conversely, matching those finitely many limits leaves the exact finite orthant corrections unresolved. Those corrections remain a possible source of distinguishing information.

At the working rational parameter q=1/2, the same information can be enclosed without algebraic roots. Retain the graph-independent factor instead:

Ψr(q)=∑z∈ℤn∑zi=rqEG(z)+λ∥z∥2,q−λ(nk2+2kr)akn+r↑Ψr(q). \Psi_r(q)=\sum_{\substack{z\in\mathbb Z^n\\\sum z_i=r}} q^{E_G(z)+\lambda\|z\|^2},\qquad q^{-\lambda(nk^2+2kr)}a_{kn+r}\uparrow\Psi_r(q).

Every exponent in this form is integral. Exact rational Gaussian bounds therefore certify intervals for Psi at every rational q in (0,1), including one half. Disjoint intervals for a common residue prove full-series inequivalence. Overlapping intervals are inconclusive, and equal limiting values still do not prove equality of the original coefficients.

1.5 A realizability constraint with the correct semantics

A tree’s unique terminal path factorizes its ordered kernel into Gaussian edge matrices and positive diagonal branch messages. Cauchy-Binet then supplies strict positivity of every finite minor with increasing pin indices. The leading minor also records the path length. These facts constrain a proposed ordered tree interface far more than nonnegative individual coefficients do.

There is a useful warning supplied by an actual graph, rather than a hypothetical pathology: the four-vertex path truncated at interior volume one has a negative two-by-two determinant at positive x and q. The full kernel is strictly totally positive. The common height box used in FR03 is a product domain compatible with the factorization; the volume simplex is not. A realizability test must therefore certify the relevant complete coefficient order or a valid box before using a minor as an obstruction.

These constraints apply to ordered kernels. Averaging a kernel with its transpose is a different operation, and the new theorem does not automatically impose the same strict positivity on every symmetric interface. The surviving symmetric ambiguities consequently remain open.

2 FR01: exact connected pole divisors and a controlled reconstruction sector

This companion result concerns the complete local series at one fixed real parameter, not a finite-prefix extrapolation. It is independent of the NX36 implementation programme. It strengthens the existing entire-normalization theorem SNG-F39: for a connected graph, every possible root-of-unity pole is actually present. It also gives a rigorous, explicitly restricted sector in which poles recover the component-size population.

The new results are proved below in ordinary mathematical prose. The Python module implements rational bounds, finite cyclotomic Gaussian enclosures, and the combinatorial inversion. No proof-assistant formalization is claimed.

2.1 1. Objects and the question left by entire normalization

Let G be a finite simple graph on n vertices, let 0<q<1, and write

EG(a)=∑uv∈E(G)(au−av)2,LG(x,q)=∑a∈ℤ≥0nx∑vavqEG(a). E_G(a)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(a_u-a_v)^2, \qquad L_G(x,q)=\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^n}x^{\sum_v a_v}q^{E_G(a)}.

For a connected graph, common translation partitions the original labeled height population into disjoint infinite rays. The representative of a ray has minimum zero. Consequently

LG(x,q)=BG(x,q)1−xn,BG(x,q)=∑a≥0mina=0x∑avqEG(a). L_G(x,q)=\frac{B_G(x,q)}{1-x^n},\qquad B_G(x,q)=\sum_{\substack{a\ge0\\\min a=0}} x^{\sum a_v}q^{E_G(a)}.

SNG-F39 proves that B_G is entire in x. This proves only that the poles of L_G are among the nth roots of unity. A numerator zero could in principle remove a denominator zero. The present first theorem rules this out exactly at the roots belonging to the connected component itself.

2.2 2. The connected noncancellation theorem

Theorem FR01.1. For every connected n-vertex graph and every fixed 0<q<1, B_G(zeta,q) is a strictly positive real number whenever zeta^n=1. Thus L_G has a simple pole at every nth root of unity and no other poles. More precisely,

limr↑1(1−r)LG(rζ,q)=BG(ζ,q)n>0,Resx=ζLG(x,q)=−ζnBG(ζ,q). \lim_{r\uparrow1}(1-r)L_G(r\zeta,q) =\frac{B_G(\zeta,q)}{n}>0, \qquad \operatorname{Res}_{x=\zeta}L_G(x,q) =-\frac{\zeta}{n}B_G(\zeta,q).

The adjective positive concerns B_G and the displayed radial normalization; the complex residue itself need not be positive real.

Proof. Choose a root vertex 0. Every height class modulo common integer translation has a unique anchored representative z with z_0=0 and (z_1,…,z_{n-1}) in Z^(n-1). Its minimum-zero representative is a=z-(min z)1. Therefore

EG(a)=EG(z),∑av=∑v≠0zv−nminz. E_G(a)=E_G(z),\qquad \sum a_v=\sum_{v\ne0}z_v-n\min z.

The last term disappears from the phase at zeta^n=1. If Q is the reduced Laplacian obtained by deleting row and column 0, then

BG(ζ,q)=∑z∈ℤn−1qzTQzζ𝟏Tz. B_G(\zeta,q) =\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z^{n-1}} q^{z^TQz}\zeta^{\mathbf1^Tz}.

Connectedness makes Q positive definite: a vector of zero energy is constant on every edge, hence constant on the connected graph, and anchoring forces that constant to be zero. In particular this Gaussian series converges absolutely. Write s=-log q>0 and zeta=exp(2pii*k/n), and set alpha=(k/n)1. Gaussian Fourier transform and Poisson summation give

BG(ζ,q)=π(n−1)/2s(n−1)/2detQ∑m∈ℤn−1exp[−π2s(m−α)TQ−1(m−α)]. \boxed{ B_G(\zeta,q)= \frac{\pi^{(n-1)/2}}{s^{(n-1)/2}\sqrt{\det Q}} \sum_{m\in\mathbb Z^{n-1}} \exp\!\left[-\frac{\pi^2}{s} (m-\alpha)^TQ^{-1}(m-\alpha)\right].}

Every summand on the right is a strictly positive real number, and the sum converges. Hence B_G(zeta,q)>0. Since 1-x^n has a simple zero at zeta, the quotient has exactly the claimed simple pole. The radial formula follows from (1-r^n)/(1-r)->n; the residue follows from the derivative -n*zeta^(n-1). For n=1, Q has dimension zero, B_G=1 and L_G=1/(1-x), so the same result holds directly. This completes all cases. QED.

Why the argument is specific. The phase is a character of the quotient height lattice only when zeta^n=1. At an unrelated unit-circle point, the factor zeta^(-n min z) remains. Dropping that factor would turn a proof for the graph’s own denominator roots into a false proof for every root of unity.

Consequence FR01.2. The full fixed-q local series determines whether G is connected. For a connected graph it determines its vertex count from the complete set of poles. This does not determine the connected graph’s isomorphism type. Full-series equality of two connected graphs forces equal orders, but the connected fixed-q completeness problem remains open.

2.3 3. Disconnected products and the cancellation obstruction

Let G have connected components H_1,…,H_c with sizes n_i. Independence of the original assignments gives

LG(x,q)=∏i=1cLHi(x,q)=∏iBHi(x,q)∏i(1−xni). L_G(x,q)=\prod_{i=1}^c L_{H_i}(x,q) =\frac{\prod_i B_{H_i}(x,q)}{\prod_i(1-x^{n_i})}.

At x=1, every numerator is positive by Theorem FR01.1. Therefore the pole order at 1 is exactly c, with no additional hypothesis. In particular a disconnected graph has pole order at least two there, proving the first assertion of Consequence FR01.2.

At a primitive dth root zeta, components with d dividing n_i each have a simple pole. But a component with d not dividing n_i has no denominator pole there and its entire numerator has not been proved nonzero there. Such an analytic factor could cancel a pole of a different component. Thus the tempting general assertion

poleordζLG=?#{i:d∣ni} \operatorname{poleord}_{\zeta}L_G \stackrel{?}{=}\#\{i:d\mid n_i\}

is not an unconditional result of the connected theorem. This package neither exhibits such a cancellation nor proves that it cannot occur. Unknown cancellation is kept explicit in the API.

At a given zeta the exact signed order, with zeros counted negatively, is

∑i:d∣ni1−∑i:d∤niordζBHi. \sum_{i:d\mid n_i}1 -\sum_{i:d\nmid n_i}\operatorname{ord}_{\zeta} B_{H_i}.

The second sum is the precise missing datum. A finite numerical sample showing it to be zero cannot establish an all-graph noncancellation theorem.

2.4 4. A uniform zero-free sector from a spanning tree

Define the scalar Gaussian theta value

ϑ(q)=∑k∈ℤqk2=1+2∑k≥1qk2. \vartheta(q)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}q^{k^2} =1+2\sum_{k\ge1}q^{k^2}.

Lemma FR01.3. If H is connected on m vertices, then

BH(1,q)≤ϑ(q)m−1. B_H(1,q)\le\vartheta(q)^{m-1}.

Proof. Choose a spanning tree T and orient it away from a root. The integer differences on its m-1 edges are independent integer coordinates on the anchored height lattice. Tree energy is the sum of their squares. Every extra graph edge adds a nonnegative square. As 0<q<1, q(E_H)<=q(E_T) term by term. Summing over the tree coordinates factors the upper bound into m-1 copies of vartheta. For m=1 it is the identity 1<=1. QED.

Since the normalized series has constant term 1 and nonnegative real coefficients, for every |x|<=1,

|BH(x,q)−1|≤BH(1,q)−1≤ϑ(q)m−1−1. |B_H(x,q)-1|\le B_H(1,q)-1 \le\vartheta(q)^{m-1}-1.

Theorem FR01.4. Fix an integer N>=1. If

ϑ(q)N−1<2, \boxed{\vartheta(q)^{N-1}<2,}

then the numerator of every connected graph of size at most N is zero-free on the entire closed unit disk. Consequently, for any graph whose component sizes are at most N, the primitive-d root pole order is exactly

pd=∑k≥1bkd, p_d=\sum_{k\ge1}b_{kd},

where b_m is the number of m-vertex components. This is a sufficient small-q sector, not an assertion that zeros appear outside that sector.

The strict inequality matters: an enclosure reaching 2 does not give the strict distance from zero used by the triangle inequality.

For a simple explicit sufficient inequality, k^2>=k gives

ϑ(q)≤1+q1−q,(1+q1−q)N−1<2. \vartheta(q)\le\frac{1+q}{1-q},\qquad \left(\frac{1+q}{1-q}\right)^{N-1}<2.

For implementation we use a much sharper rational enclosure. For K>=0,

ϑ(q)≤UK(q):=1+2∑k=1Kqk2+2q(K+1)21−q2K+3. \vartheta(q)\le U_K(q):= 1+2\sum_{k=1}^Kq^{k^2} +\frac{2q^{(K+1)^2}}{1-q^{2K+3}}.

Indeed each successive exponent increment in the omitted tail is at least 2K+3. Exact rational comparison of U_K(q)^(N-1) with 2 certifies the condition. At q=1/16 the implementation certifies all components of size at most six. At q=1/2 the bound fails already for size two; this refusal does not contradict the unconditional connected theorem at q=1/2.

2.5 5. Inverting the component-size population

Assume the full profile p_1,…,p_N has been justified, for example by Theorem FR01.4. Descending inversion gives

bm=pm−∑k≥2bkm,m=N,N−1,…,1. \boxed{b_m=p_m-\sum_{k\ge2}b_{km},\qquad m=N,N-1,\ldots,1.}

Equivalently, with the classical integer Mobius function,

bm=∑k≥1μ(k)pkm. b_m=\sum_{k\ge1}\mu(k)p_{km}.

These sums are finite because no component exceeds N. For the component population (1,1,2,3,3,6), the profile is (p_1,…,p_6)=(6,2,3,0,0,1), and inversion returns exactly two isolated vertices, one size-two component, two size-three components, and one size-six component. No multiplicity is divided by an automorphism count.

Corollary FR01.5. On cluster graphs (disjoint unions of complete graphs) whose largest component has size at most N, the complete fixed-q local series is a complete isomorphism invariant whenever the small-q hypothesis of Theorem FR01.4 holds. The proof recovers b_m from the divisor; these multiplicities determine the graph because a complete component of each size has only one isomorphism type.

For general graphs the same result recovers component sizes only, not the isomorphism types of their connected components. There is no claim that a finite coefficient prefix reveals the poles or provides a completeness cutoff. The theorem uses the full analytic function determined by the series.

2.6 6. An unconditional all-q class

Suppose every nontrivial component has the same size m>=2, with any number of isolated vertices. At every denominator root of a nontrivial component, all nontrivial components satisfy zeta^m=1 and hence have positive normalized numerators. Isolated factors 1/(1-x) never vanish. Thus the divisor formula p_d=sum_{d|n_i}1 is valid for this class at every 0<q<1.

In particular the complete fixed-q local series distinguishes every graph

kKm⊔ℓK1(k≥1,m≥2,ℓ≥0) kK_m\;\sqcup\;\ell K_1 \quad(k\ge1,\ m\ge2,\ \ell\ge0)

from every other graph in that class. The largest primitive pole order is m; its multiplicity is k; the pole order at 1 is k+ell. Purely isolated graphs are handled separately by (1-x)^(-ell). This is an infinite class of disconnected interiors beyond the cycle-union reconstruction family, but its result concerns the unpinned local series specified here.

2.7 7. Generic parameters in a fixed finite family

For a fixed connected H and a fixed unit root zeta, B_H(zeta,q) is holomorphic in |q|<1. Uniform absolute convergence on compact subdisks follows by domination by B_H(1,|q|) and the Gaussian lattice bound. Its value at q=0 is 1, since connected zero-energy normalized profiles contain only the all-zero assignment. Therefore it is not identically zero and its zeros are locally discrete.

For any fixed finite graph family, there are only finitely many components and relevant roots. Outside a locally discrete exceptional set of q in (0,1), every off-denominator cancellation is absent and the divisor inversion is valid. On every compact subinterval strictly inside (0,1) the exceptional set is finite. This does not locate it, certify q=1/2, or exclude rational exceptional values. No such arithmetic strengthening is claimed.

2.8 8. Exact finite Gaussian enclosures

anchored_gaussian_box uses integer differences r_e on a rooted spanning tree, rather than bounding the heights themselves. It sums exactly over |r_e|<=H in the cyclotomic ring Q[zeta]. There are (2H+1)^(n-1) profiles, checked against a budget before enumeration.

Let theta_H=1+2 sum_{k=1}^H q(k2). As E_G>=sum_e r_e^2, the absolute mass outside this difference box is bounded by

ϑ(q)n−1−ϑH(q)n−1≤UK(q)n−1−ϑH(q)n−1,K≥H. \vartheta(q)^{n-1}-\vartheta_H(q)^{n-1} \le U_K(q)^{n-1}-\vartheta_H(q)^{n-1},\qquad K\ge H.

This gives a rational absolute complex-error radius around the exact cyclotomic partial sum. Different spanning-tree boxes need not sum the same finite populations; their certified balls nevertheless contain the same full theta value. Tests exercise this root-choice failure mode. For K_2, the character at -1 reduces independently to sum_{z=-H}^H (-1)^z q(z2), and at q=1/2,H=4 the enclosure already proves strict positivity by rational arithmetic. A triangle at a primitive third root is checked independently in direct rooted height coordinates.

The finite Gaussian calculation is evidence and an evaluable bound. The all-graph noncancellation theorem follows from Poisson positivity, not from checking many boxes or observing positive floating-point approximations.

2.9 9. Scope relative to the main frontiers

The series A_H(x,q)=sum_a x^(sum a) q^(E_H(a)+2 sum a_v^2), used by dominating-terminal pinning, has an absolute quadratic pinning term. It is entire in x and does not have the translation denominator 1-x^n. Therefore this pole theorem does not prove completeness for the fixed-pinned A_H series. It provides a separate invariant for unpinned L_H and a controlled class where full-series reconstruction is proved.

The work does not realize a symmetric-interface null vector, settle fixed-pinning completeness, extend a finite-prefix cutoff, or duplicate NX36’s proposed parameter-observability and ideal-closure projects. The surviving questions remain explicit: unit-circle zeros of arbitrary off-denominator numerators; effective localization of their q exceptions; full-series collisions in connected graphs; and reconstruction under quadratic pinning. These are logical next problems, not completed claims.

3 FR02: arithmetic recovery from a single fixed-pinning coefficient

Status. Proved project result, exact decoder, independently enumerated finite checks. This companion does not implement an NX36 work packet. It establishes a new necessary condition for fixed-pinning equality and a reconstruction theorem for one restricted family. It does not prove completeness of the unmarked graph series.

3.1 1. What information can survive a rational specialization?

The existing exact machinery retains an energy polynomial at each volume. If that polynomial is available, its largest exponent is an obvious source of graph information. A more difficult question is whether the information remains available after replacing the polynomial variable by a single rational number. Different integer energy polynomials can have the same rational value. Indeed, the example below has two different maximum degrees and an equal first coefficient at the project’s working value q=1/2q=1/2.

Nevertheless, sufficiently concentrated height profiles leave a recoverable denominator signature. The signature is not the whole degree distribution. It is the largest degree and the number of vertices having that degree. The decoder needs only one rational coefficient, the graph order, and the declared pinning parameter. It does not receive the graph, the coefficient’s energy support, a marked series, or a second value of qq.

The same argument applies directly to the dominating-terminal interior sequence already used by the charge interface. Introduce an integer diagonal shift λ≥0\lambda\ge0 and write

FG,λ(x,q)=∑h∈ℤ≥0nx∑vhvqEG(h)+λ∑vhv2,EG(h)=∑uv∈E(G)(hu−hv)2. F_{G,\lambda}(x,q) =\sum_{h\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^{n}} x^{\sum_vh_v}\, q^{E_G(h)+\lambda\sum_vh_v^2}, \qquad E_G(h)=\sum_{uv\in E(G)}(h_u-h_v)^2.

The case λ=0\lambda=0 is the raw local series LGL_G. The case λ=2\lambda=2 is the existing interior series AGA_G for two dominating terminals. This is the diagonal pinning already present in that interface, rather than a new observation variable. Define

aR(G;q,λ)=[xR]FG,λ(x,q),Δ=maxvdegG(v), a_R(G;q,\lambda)=[x^R]F_{G,\lambda}(x,q), \qquad \Delta=\max_v\deg_G(v),

3.3 3. A denominator invariant without factoring the denominator

Fix a reduced rational

3.5 5. What this says about fixed-pinning completeness

For known order and the same declared shift, equality of the entire fixed-rational series forces equality of Δ\Delta and mm, because equality includes the coefficient at R*R_*. In fact, agreement at that single coefficient is enough for these two invariants. Thus any proposed full-series collision with different maximum degrees, or different numbers of maximum-degree vertices, is impossible.

This statement is useful both for LGL_G and for the fixed-pinning interior sequence AGA_G. It supplies a genuine necessary condition for future collision searches without confusing a finite observation with full equality. The conclusion goes only one way:

FG,λ(x,A/B)=FH,λ(x,A/B)⇒(ΔG,mG)=(ΔH,mH) F_{G,\lambda}(x,A/B)=F_{H,\lambda}(x,A/B) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad (\Delta_G,m_G)=(\Delta_H,m_H)

when the graph orders agree. Equal recovered extrema do not imply equal series, equal graphs, equal degree distributions, or equal component structure. No such converse is asserted.

Known order is part of the input. This theorem does not infer the number of vertices from one coefficient, and it does not compare series with different numbers of vertices by silently inserting isolated vertices. Diagonal shift is also part of the input: AHA_H and LHL_H must not be decoded using each other’s convention.

3.7 7. A restricted reconstruction theorem beyond cycle interiors

Consider the complete split graph obtained by joining a clique of size cc to an independent set of size n−cn-c. SNG-R02.3 — Complete-split reconstruction. 3.9 9. Follow-on questions without claiming their answers

The theorem naturally suggests asking which other extremal statistics can be recovered after rational specialization. Peeling the leading energy term from one coefficient is insufficient to recover a whole degree distribution: lower-energy nonsingle profiles overlap the contributions from lower-degree spikes, and subtraction must respect that interference. A valid next theorem would need a second isolation argument, rather than a repeated invocation of this one.

The coefficient’s numerator is as important as its denominator. Denominator depth identifies the leading diagonal size up to cancellation by its multiplicity; the residue then removes the cancellation. This pattern may be useful for other finite lattice populations with an isolated extremal energy, provided one proves an explicit gap and a uniform bound on its leading multiplicity. That is a general method to investigate, not an already established extension to arbitrary interactions.

For the present fixed-pinning frontier, the immediate contribution is precise: any equal full pinned sequence at rational qq and known order must preserve maximum degree and its multiplicity. Candidate searches can impose these two conditions before expensive coefficient comparisons. The complete-split family is reconstructible from the resulting arithmetic data. The larger questions of general completeness, a genuine full-series collision, and the realization of symmetric-interface ambiguities remain open.

4.1 1. Which object is positive?

Let T=(V,E)T=(V,E) be a finite connected tree, let a,b∈Va,b\in V be distinct ordered terminals, and let 0<x,q<10<x,q<1. Pin their heights to s,t∈ℤ≥0s,t\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}. The ordered kernel is

0<KT(s,t;x,q)≤(1−x)−(|V|−2). 0<K_T(s,t;x,q)\le (1-x)^{-(|V|-2)}.

The right side follows by dropping every energy factor, which is at most one. This gives absolute convergence without a height cutoff. For H≥0H\ge0, write KT[H]K_T^{[H]} when every interior vertex independently has height in {0,…,H}\{0,\ldots,H\}. This is a Cartesian height box. det(KT(si,tj;x,q))i,j=1k>0. \det\bigl(K_T(s_i,t_j;x,q)\bigr)_{i,j=1}^k>0.

The two pin lists need not be equal. The matrix need not be symmetric: a tree with an asymmetric terminal placement generally has KT(s,t)≠KT(t,s)K_T(s,t)\ne K_T(t,s).

4.2 2. Gaussian edges are strictly totally positive

For one edge put

G(r,c)=q(r−c)2=qr2+c2(q−2r)c. G(r,c)=q^{(r-c)^2} =q^{r^2+c^2}\bigl(q^{-2r}\bigr)^c.

To see this, divide by its lowest power of zz; differentiation removes its constant term and leaves at most k−1k-1 monomials. Rolle’s theorem and induction give the claim. 4.7 7. What this contributes to the remaining frontier

These results add a nonlinear necessary condition and a geometry decoder for an important class of realizable ordered interfaces. They can accompany a future candidate search by filtering an ordered tree completion, provided that the data include enough volume and the test respects the stated kernel semantics. They cannot directly eliminate a candidate living only in a symmetrized interface: averaging a matrix with its transpose does not follow from the path factorization, and no preservation theorem for that averaging is proved here.

The natural next problems are to decode pendant-branch statistics from subsequent determinant coefficients, understand which analogous sign constraints survive on unicyclic graphs, and determine which ordered positivity constraints descend to symmetrized data under additional terminal symmetries. None of these is counted as an implemented NX36 unit, a full-series collision, or a complete reconstruction theorem for arbitrary pinned graphs.

5.1 1. The question and the change of scale

5.2 2. Order is already an intrinsic full-series invariant

Theorem FR04.1 (quadratic coefficient decay). For every R≥0R\ge0,

limR→∞logAG,R(λ)(q)R2=λnlogq. \boxed{\displaystyle \lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\log A_{G,R}^{(\lambda)}(q)}{R^2} =\frac{\lambda}{n}\log q.} \tag{2}

Proof. Cauchy–Schwarz gives ∑ihi2≥R2/n\sum_i h_i^2\ge R^2/n, and graph energy is nonnegative. Every summand is therefore at most qλR2/nq^{\lambda R^2/n}. There are exactly (R+n−1n−1)\binom{R+n-1}{n-1} labeled weak compositions of RR, which proves the upper bound.

Its single positive summand proves the lower bound. Taking logarithms of (1), dividing by R2R^2, and using log(R+n−1n−1)=On(logR)\log\binom{R+n-1}{n-1}=O_n(\log R) proves (2). The number λlogq\lambda\log q is nonzero, so its quotient by the limit determines nn. This last statement is a full-series assertion. It does not supply a universal finite-volume procedure for reading the limit from an arbitrary prefix. ∎

The theorem has a useful methodological consequence. A completeness question formulated for the full pinned series need not assume graph order is externally provided: the complete series contains it. An algorithm using only finitely many coefficients must still declare how it knows or bounds the order.

5.6 6. What this contributes to the fixed-pinning frontier

If two graphs have equal complete pinned series at the same λ,q\lambda,q, Theorem FR04.1 forces equal order, and Theorem FR04.2 forces equality of every residue-class theta limit. This gives a hierarchy of necessary conditions:

full equality⇒(nG=nH,ΘG,r=ΘH,r∀r),⇒equal quadratic decay rate. \begin{aligned} \text{full equality}&\Longrightarrow (n_G=n_H,\ \Theta_{G,r}=\Theta_{H,r}\ \forall r),\\ &\Longrightarrow \text{equal quadratic decay rate}. \end{aligned} \tag{15}

No converse arrow is asserted. Passing from the full coefficients to the theta limits removes the moving nonnegative boundary. Equation (8) displays exactly what was removed: the entire sequence of boundary deficits. Equal limiting values do not, by themselves, force those deficits to agree. A prefix collision does not establish equality of the limits, and equality of limits does not establish a full-series collision.

This distinction gives the next structural question a precise form. A genuine full-series collision must match both the centered bulk theta data and every boundary correction. A completeness proof could instead explain why matching all these corrections forces a graph isomorphism. The present result supplies the decomposition and certified access to its limiting part; it proves neither of those further statements.

A practical use is one-sided rejection. Suppose two candidate graphs have the same order and fixed pinning. If exact theta enclosures for one residue are disjoint, their theta limits differ, so their complete pinned series differ. That is a valid full-series inequivalence certificate even when an earlier finite prefix agreed. The converse remains one-sided: overlapping intervals merely mean that this computation has not separated the candidates.

6.3 FR03: ordered tree positivity and distance

Verdict: sound for the full ordered kernel and for product height boxes, with explicit failure under a simplex volume truncation.
