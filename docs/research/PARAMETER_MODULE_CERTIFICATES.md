# NX14: resource-bounded parameter-module certificates

The original `parametric.py` already computes polynomial observation modules. This addition supplies portable module-generation evidence, a small replay route, explicit chart guards, and a bounded next-budget attempt. It does not replace that implementation or promote isolated rational ranks to parameter-wide theorems.

## Objects and the two stabilization questions

For polynomial observations \(L_j(q)\) and generators \(M_i(q)\), put

\[
 N_0=\langle L_j\rangle_{\mathbb Q[q]},\qquad
 N_{r+1}=N_0+\sum_iN_rM_i.
\]

A module chain can change without changing rational-function rank. For example, the full-rank submodule generated by \((q,0),(0,1)\) is properly contained in the module generated by \((1,0),(0,1)\). Both have rank two over \(\mathbb Q(q)\). Hence the constructor compares complete Hermite bases, rather than stopping when the number of basis rows stops increasing.

At stage \(r\), let \(I_r\) contain the original observation rows and the preceding basis multiplied by each generator. The receipt stores a basis \(B_r\), a polynomial matrix \(F_r\), and another polynomial matrix \(C_r\), with

\[
 B_r=F_r I_r,\qquad I_r=C_rB_r.
\]

The first identity proves every proposed basis row is generated by real experiments. The second proves no experiment in the stage was lost. Once \(B_r=B_{r-1}\), every original observation belongs to this module and every generator preserves it. Induction on word length therefore proves it contains every future experiment. These are exact polynomial identities; no sampled evaluation is used in this implication.

**Proposition NX14.1.** If all displayed identities replay, the last two bases coincide, and the original system has the declared dimensions, then the final basis generates exactly the complete polynomial observation module.

**Proof.** The stage-zero identities give equality with the original observation module. Each subsequent pair gives equality with the stated sum of observations and transforms, so induction identifies every stored stage. Equality of adjacent stages implies closure under right multiplication by every generator. The final module contains all finite words, and the forward identities show all its rows remain polynomial combinations of those words. Both inclusions follow. □

The constructor records elementary polynomial row operations while computing Hermite form. The verifier does not run the Hermite constructor: it multiplies the supplied polynomial matrices and checks these two inclusions directly. Generic rank is checked separately. Tiny determinant tests use a separate sparse-polynomial Leibniz calculation, so they do not merely compare two calls to Hermite form.

## Divisors, exceptional parameters, and actual experiments

If the final basis has generic rank \(r\), form its maximal determinantal divisor

\[
 D_r(q)=\operatorname{monic}\gcd_{|J|=r}\det B_{[:,J]}(q).
\]

The receipt wrapper only enumerates these minors when their number is below its declared limit. For a univariate polynomial matrix, specialization has rank below \(r\) exactly when \(D_r\) vanishes. This statement concerns all maximal minors. A determinant of one selected generic experiment basis can vanish at a perfectly observable parameter.

The permanent false-exception fixture uses \(a=q-1/2\), observations \((1,0),(0,a)\), and \(M=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). The original observation determinant is \(a\), but \((1,0)M=(0,1)\). The complete module is the entire two-dimensional polynomial module and \(D_2=1\). There is no exception at one half.

The true-exception fixture has \(L=(1,a)\) and \(M=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\). Every word gives \((1,2^ka)\). Consequently

\[
 \operatorname{rank}(q)=\begin{cases}1&q=1/2,\\2&q\ne1/2,\end{cases}
 \qquad D_2=q-1/2.
\]

These fixtures have certified charts containing actual observation/word pairs. Each chart records the selected coordinate minor and its determinant. The chart is valid precisely where that determinant and the declared denominator are nonzero. The atlas additionally checks whether the gcd of its chart determinants equals the module divisor. When that equality holds, the charts cover the entire regular locus. It is not enough that every displayed sample point works. In the two permanent fixtures the coverage equality does hold. Exceptional ranks are computed exactly, and replay recomputes the complete exceptional list rather than trusting an omitted entry. Algebraic isolating intervals are refined against the exact rational domain endpoints before inclusion is decided. A common algebraic factor of the divisor and denominator is excluded as a pole, just as a rational pole is excluded. The regression with a domain endpoint equal to an initial isolation bound prevents accidentally omitting an interior irrational root.

A module basis may contain polynomial combinations that are not single physical experiments. The schema keeps this distinction explicit. Its `chain` rows certify algebraic generation; `charts` store real witness words. Costs of the latter belong to the original experiment system and are not inferred from polynomial coefficients.

## Rational-function guard

The fixture with numerator generator \(N=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)\), observation \((1,1)\), and common denominator \(h(q)=2q-1\) represents \(M=N/h\). At every \(q\ne1/2\), a length-\(k\) word is multiplied by the nonzero scalar \(h(q)^{-k}\). Thus pointwise row spans agree with those of the numerator language on the guarded domain. The excluded point is an undefined pole, not an observed rank drop. Direct rational specializations at one quarter and three quarters have rank two; attempting one half raises an explicit error.

This scalar-denominator argument permits the stated rank comparison. It does not identify the polynomial modules over the unlocalized ring, and it does not authorize treating poles as admissible experiments.

This is the bounded refusal permitted by NX14-B, rather than a concealed incomplete computation. It identifies the next bottleneck: a full module/divisor calculation at dimension 34 with explicitly larger resource allowances. The graph matrices were actually compiled and the two rational closures were computed. Their full ranks are finite specialization evidence, not an interval theorem and not an all-budget cyclotomic factor theorem.

`parameter_module_receipt` exposes dimension, degree, coefficient-bit, module-step, maximal-minor and wall-clock limits. The deterministic resource record retains achieved polynomial degree, coefficient bits and module steps; machine-dependent time and memory are not serialized as reproducible mathematical data. The wrapper's refusal state has no rank field. The distinction between `complete`, `module_complete_factor_partial`, and `budget_refusal` is part of the API contract. A partial factorization never supplies a complete exceptional atlas.

## Reproduction and compatibility

Implementation: `src/singularity/parameter_receipts.py`. Tests: `tests/test_parameter_module_receipts.py`. Receipt: `results/parameter_module_receipts.json`. Run `python scripts/parameter_module_receipts.py --check`. The tests alter a module generator, a factor, and a witness word and require rejection. Rank-zero systems and the denominator guard are retained. The existing `parametric.py` public meanings remain unchanged.

The accepted finite scope is the replay architecture, the two complete interior-exception atlases, the guarded rational example, and the documented R5 bounded attempt. Multi-parameter determinantal varieties, larger-budget factor classification, and a universal factor theorem remain separate questions.
