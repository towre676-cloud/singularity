# Classical sources for the operator chapter

The following primary publisher records were checked on 19 October 2026
(UTC). The bibliography retains the existing citation keys. These sources
establish the classical setting; they do not establish priority for this
project's graph interfaces or certify its implementation.

## Kalman's realization paper

R. E. Kalman, *Mathematical Description of Linear Dynamical Systems*,
*Journal of the Society for Industrial and Applied Mathematics, Series A:
Control* **1**(2) (1963), 152–192. DOI:
[10.1137/0301010](https://doi.org/10.1137/0301010).

The [SIAM publisher record](https://epubs.siam.org/doi/10.1137/0301010)
confirms the title, author, original journal, issue, pages, and DOI. Its
abstract relates state descriptions and input/output behavior, explains
controllable and observable components, and describes irreducible
realizations and minimal state dimension. Its online posting date is
18 July 2006; the issue belongs to January 1963. Cite the original issue
year. The publisher's current website branding is not the historical
journal title.

## Schützenberger's automata paper

M. P. Schützenberger, *On the definition of a family of automata*,
*Information and Control* **4**(2–3) (1961), 245–270. DOI:
[10.1016/S0019-9958(61)80020-X](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(61)80020-X).

The [Elsevier publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S001999586180020X)
confirms the September 1961 issue, pagination, and DOI. Its indexed publisher
content was available even though direct full-page retrieval failed. For
the relationship to weighted automata and rational/recognizable series,
the author-written Berstel–Reutenauer preface cited below supplies a directly
readable account. Avoid attributing every modern minimization formulation
to this paper without checking the relevant theorem.

## Carlyle and Paz's finite-rank realization paper

J. W. Carlyle and A. Paz, *Realizations by stochastic finite automata*,
*Journal of Computer and System Sciences* **5**(1) (1971), 26–40. DOI:
[10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-0000(71)80005-3).

The [Elsevier publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022000071800053)
confirms the February 1971 issue and citation. The
[author-institution publication record](https://cris.technion.ac.il/en/publications/realizations-by-stochastic-finite-automata/)
also gives the DOI and full abstract. The stated result connects finite rank
of real-valued functions on words with finite-state realizations allowing
real initial, transition, and output weights, and discusses multiple
outputs. That supports citing finite-rank realization as established
background. It does not justify identifying an arbitrary all-initial-state
observable quotient with the reachable minimal realization of one fixed
initial-state series without addressing reachability.

## Berstel and Reutenauer's monograph

Jean Berstel and Christophe Reutenauer, *Noncommutative Rational Series
with Applications*, Encyclopedia of Mathematics and its Applications
**137**, Cambridge University Press. DOI:
[10.1017/CBO9780511760860](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511760860).
Hardback ISBN: 9780521190220.

The [Cambridge book record](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/noncommutative-rational-series-with-applications/CADC75E4ADDA69E99BB8B0D8FE9AD119)
confirms the authors, title, series, DOI, ISBN and 262-page physical extent.
It lists print availability on 14 October 2010 and digital publication on
5 June 2013. Chapter metadata likewise uses print year 2010. These dates
must be distinguished from the imprint year below.

The [publisher's frontmatter PDF](https://assets.cambridge.org/97805211/90220/frontmatter/9780521190220_frontmatter.pdf),
PDF page four, has copyright year 2011 and states **“First published 2011.”**
The existing `BerstelReutenauer2011` citation therefore keeps 2011, with the
publisher's differing availability dates recorded rather than concealed.
The contents locate minimal linear representations at page 34 and the
minimization algorithm at page 37. The author-written preface explicitly
places rational/recognizable equivalence and minimal representation in
the first two chapters. These are the most direct background references
for the operator chapter's finite-dimensional linear algebra.

## Scope of the project contribution

The invariant observation space, unobservable subspace, finite-rank linear
representation, and minimal linear realization belong to this classical
literature. Singularity provides a declared graph experiment language,
checked graph-to-operator semantics, exact replayable evidence, costed
distinguishing experiments, parameter calculations, and explicit
realizability and compression examples. Distinguish these concrete results
from generic linear-systems principles. Minimum dimension on the full
formal state space is also distinct from minimum dimension on a restricted
graph-realizable family, and from realization of a series with one fixed
initial state.
