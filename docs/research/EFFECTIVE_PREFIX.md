# NX24 — Mechanism-certified finite prefixes

This release completes NX24-A/B/C for explicitly restricted families. It does not give a universal cutoff for arbitrary graph-height series. The new graph theorem is intentionally narrow: a single weighted edge with a known number of isolated vertices. Its constructive mechanism is an all-coefficient ideal-generation identity, rather than a guessed recurrence or temporary ideal stabilization. A supplemental binomial-ODE family demonstrates a common comparison recurrence for a genuinely nonrational series.

## Unified contracts and existing baselines

`FiniteStatePrefixCertificate` defines the original sequence itself by exact rational matrices \(f_n=cA^ns_0\). For two declared systems of dimensions \(d_1,d_2\), put their transitions in a block-diagonal matrix and subtract their output rows. The difference is a scalar output of a \(d_1+d_2\)-dimensional system. Cayley–Hamilton gives a constant recurrence of that degree, so agreement through index \(d_1+d_2-1\) controls every later coefficient. This conclusion concerns these declared linear-state families, not an external sequence lacking a proved adapter.

`PeriodicPrefixCertificate` wraps the existing periodic graph theorem without enlarging its scope. For the same order \(n\) and phase modulus \(M\), the existing palindromic numerator gives the sufficient cutoff \(\lfloor n(M-1)/2\rfloor\). Direct original profile enumeration remains the coefficient oracle.

Certificates have a strict schema with reduced rational numerator/positive-denominator pairs. Unknown kinds, extra fields, zero denominators, guessed recurrence payloads and unsupported family names are rejected. The finite-state constructor bounds dimension. No constructor accepts finite samples as a mechanism. A repeated ideal, a parameter count, and an arbitrarily long zero prefix are deliberately rejected rather than interpreted as proof.

## A new nonperiodic graph-family ideal theorem

Let \(G_k\) be one edge with \(k\ge0\) known isolated labeled vertices. Give the edge its ordinary parameter \(q\). The original nonnegative-height series is
\[
 F_{k,q}(x)=\sum_{h\in\mathbb N^{k+2}}x^{|h|}q^{(h_1-h_2)^2}.
\]
Write the two endpoint heights as a common nonnegative minimum plus a signed difference. A zero difference has one orientation; a positive difference has two. Each common translation adds volume two. Hence
\[
 F_{k,q}(x)=\frac{1+2\sum_{d\ge1}q^{d^2}x^d}{(1-x)^k(1-x^2)}.
\]
This identity comes from a direct bijection of original labeled profiles. It is not inferred from observed coefficients. Its first coefficient is
\[
 f_1(q)=k+2q.
\]
For two parameters \(q,\eta\), put
\[
 S_m(q,\eta)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}q^{m-1-i}\eta^i,
 \qquad q^m-\eta^m=(q-\eta)S_m(q,\eta).
\]
The latter is a polynomial identity valid even when \(q=\eta\): there is no forbidden division by a parameter difference.

Let \(c_{r,d}\) be the nonnegative integer multiplicity obtained by multiplying the known denominator series; explicitly it counts the compatible common translations and isolated-vertex compositions. Then
\[
 f_r(q)-f_r(\eta)
 =2(q-\eta)\sum_{d=1}^r c_{r,d}S_{d^2}(q,\eta).
\]
Therefore every coefficient difference lies in the ideal generated by the first difference, and conversely the first difference is one of those generators:
\[
 \langle f_0(q)-f_0(\eta),f_1(q)-f_1(\eta),\ldots\rangle
 =\langle2(q-\eta)\rangle\subset\mathbb Q[q,\eta].
\]
This proves an explicit sufficient cutoff **one**, with polynomial witnesses for every future coefficient. `ideal_difference_quotient` evaluates those witnesses directly by telescoping, including the singular-looking diagonal \(q=\eta\), without cancellation assumptions. This is the requested coefficient-generation mechanism; finite agreement beyond the cutoff merely checks its implementation.

### Nonrationality and exact exceptional strata

For \(0<q<1\), the numerator
\[
 B_q(x)=1+2\sum_{d\ge1}q^{d^2}x^d
\]
is entire by the root test and has infinitely many nonzero coefficients. If \(F_{k,q}\) were rational, multiplying by \((1-x)^k(1-x^2)\) would make \(B_q\) rational. An entire rational function is polynomial, a contradiction. Thus the new cutoff genuinely covers a nonperiodic nonrational graph-series family.

The endpoint parameter strata are handled exactly rather than discarded:
\[
 F_{k,0}=\frac1{(1-x)^k(1-x^2)},\qquad
 F_{k,1}=\frac1{(1-x)^{k+2}}.
\]
At zero only equal endpoint heights contribute; at one all original profiles have unit weight. The same first-coefficient generation theorem remains valid. Comparisons require the same declared \(k\); comparing different known graph orders through this theorem would be an invalid extension.

This is a theorem about the weighted-edge parameter family, not a full-series completeness theorem for all graphs at one fixed positive parameter. It does not resolve the surviving symmetric-interface realizability problems.

## Supplemental polynomial-index comparison recurrence

Let \(H_a(x)=(1-x)^{-a}\) as a formal power series over \(\mathbb Q[a]\). From the formal ODE
\[
 (1-x)H'_a=aH_a,\qquad H_a(0)=1,
\]
its coefficients satisfy
\[
 (n+1)h_{n+1}(a)=(n+a)h_n(a),\qquad
 h_n(a)=\frac{a(a+1)\cdots(a+n-1)}{n!}.
\]
The recurrence follows by equating formal coefficients; the product formula follows by induction. It remains meaningful at negative integer parameters, where the sequence terminates, and at zero. For positive noninteger rational \(a\), the series is nonrational.

For comparing different parameters, merely subtracting their two first-order recurrences would not control their difference. Instead elimination gives the **common** difference recurrence for \(y_n=h_n(a)-h_n(b)\):
\[
 (n+1)(n+2)y_{n+2}
 -(n+1)(2n+a+b+1)y_{n+1}
 +(n+a)(n+b)y_n=0.
\]
To verify it, substitute the individual ratio formulas separately: each sequence is annihilated by this same second-order operator, because
\[
 (n+1)(n+2)\frac{(n+a)(n+a+1)}{(n+1)(n+2)}
 -(n+1)(2n+a+b+1)\frac{n+a}{n+1}
 +(n+a)(n+b)=0.
\]
The expression factors \((n+a)[(n+a+1)-(2n+a+b+1)+(n+b)]=0\). The identical argument with \(b\) works, including vanishing numerator cases since the displayed cleared identity is polynomial.

The leading term \((n+1)(n+2)\) never vanishes for integer \(n\ge0\), for any parameter values. Thus \(y_0=y_1=0\) determines every later difference by induction, giving cutoff one. All singular-looking individual ratios at negative integer numerator parameters are harmless; only division by positive integer indices occurs. This supplemental formal-ODE family is explicitly separate from arbitrary graph-height populations.

## Census, strict failures and resources

The retained graph corpus has \(k=0,1,2,3\), parameters \(0,1/5,1/3,1/2,2/3,4/5,1\), 196 ordered parameter comparisons, and direct original-profile coefficients through volume ten. Every all-coefficient ideal witness through that volume is checked independently of the comparison routine. The binomial corpus includes negative integers, negative nonintegers, zero, positive nonintegers and positive integers, with 684 common recurrence identities through index eighteen and independent rising-factorial coefficients.

A delayed family \(F_b(x)=b x^{100}\) agrees at every earlier coefficient although it is nonconstant. Likewise consecutive ideals may repeatedly equal \(\langle a-a'\rangle\) before a later coefficient introduces \(b-b'\). Such sample payloads do not match any accepted certificate mechanism. A parameter count cannot provide a missing transition identity. Canonical denominator parsing rejects denominator degeneration before any arithmetic.

Resource limits and refusal are separate from mathematical outcomes. The retained corpus is small; exact coefficient sizes can grow rapidly at larger rational denominators. No timeout or refused resource request certifies inequality or infinite ambiguity. The replay data records source digest, full parameter census, singular strata, mechanism and sufficient cutoff, so the work can resume without treating finite empirical agreement as a theorem.
