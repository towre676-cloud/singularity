# NX31: target-directed rational period transport

This implementation adapts the archived PerfectPower exact differential module
and certified Taylor transport engines. Their Apache-2.0 license remains in
`handoff/nx36/companion_sources/perfectpower/LICENSE`. The adapter is deliberately
separate from finite rational graph observation closure. A supplied connection,
a geometrically derived connection and a connection in a marked integral period
basis support different interpretations; the software does not conflate them.

## Exact observable differential module

Let a column state satisfy \(X'=A(t)X\) over \(\mathbb Q(t)\). For a row \(c(t)\),
define \(D_Ac=c'+cA\). The product rule proves, by induction,

\[
\frac{d^j}{dt^j}(cX)=(D_A^jc)X.
\]

If \(c_r=\sum_{i<r}a_ic_i\) is the first field-linear dependence of these
rows, then every solution has \(y^{(r)}=\sum_{i<r}a_i y^{(i)}\). Conversely a
universal scalar differential relation supplies exactly this row dependence,
because arbitrary initial states are permitted at an ordinary point. Hence
the first dependence has minimum universal order for that connection and
target. A specially chosen solution can have a smaller scalar equation.

The implementation calls the pinned `DifferentialModule.observable` scalar
engine; it does not introduce another symbolic scalar-equation implementation.
For multiple outputs it forms their field row span and repeatedly applies
\(D_A\), retaining independent original rows. Rank increases at most the ambient
dimension. A returned basis \(B\), derivative-coordinate matrix \(T\), and output
coordinates \(L\) are checked by the exact identities

\[
B'+BA=TB,\qquad C=LB.
\]

The verifier additionally checks basis independence and equality with the span
generated by the target derivatives through ambient order minus one. This is
essential: an unnecessarily large invariant space can satisfy both displayed
identities while failing the minimality claim. For each target a first cyclic
dependence occurs by ambient order; differentiating that dependence keeps every
later derivative in the same span, including derivatives of its coefficients.
Thus the finite generation check is sufficient and rejects forged extra modes.

Numerators and denominators are exact polynomial coefficient arrays, in increasing
degree. Input dimensions are at most four and coefficient degrees at most six.
The source symbolic engine supplies its own work, degree and coefficient-bit
budgets. Public numerical entries reject floats and booleans. Input denominator
polynomials and a nonzero basis pivot minor are retained as exclusions; clearing
denominators does not license evaluation at their zeros. A conservative rational
interval test may refuse an ordinary segment when it cannot prove regularity.

## Initial information and numerical transport

The three-state example \(A_{12}=1,A_{33}=2\), all other entries zero, has
target \(c=(1,0,0)\). Its closure has rank two, its scalar equation is \(y''=0\),
and \(y(t)=a+bt\), independently of the exponentially growing third coordinate.
Both \(a\) and \(b\) must be enclosed initially. Keeping only the initial value of
the target is insufficient. Zero targets close at rank zero; supplying all
coordinate outputs closes at full rank.

The pinned transport engine encloses the fundamental matrix on pole-free
rational disks. If \(\|A\|\le M\) on a radius-\(R\) disk, the analytic fundamental
matrix is bounded by \(e^{MR}\). Cauchy's coefficient estimate gives a Taylor
remainder bounded by

\[
n e^{MR}\frac{\rho^{N+1}}{1-\rho},\qquad \rho=|h|/R<1.
\]

The engine uses rational upper bounds for the exponential and separately adds
outward center-rounding errors. Errors compose by submultiplicativity. For
\(S\) with center \(\widehat S\), row error \(\delta\), initial center \(x\), and
component radii \(e_i\), the adapter bounds initial uncertainty by
\(\sum_i|\widehat S_{ji}|e_i+\delta\max_i e_i\), and arithmetic error by
\(\delta\|x\|_\infty\). It reports these contributions separately after applying
the target row. A path crossing an excluded pole is refused.

## Source-grounded Legendre application

For the normalized complete elliptic period \(F(t)=2K(t)/\pi\),

\[
X=(F,F')^T,\qquad
A(t)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\[2pt]
1/[4t(1-t)]&(2t-1)/[t(1-t)]\end{pmatrix}.
\]

The application transports from \(t=1/2\) to \(9/16\), in a regular real chart.
The target has observable rank two, equal to the full rank: this family does
not provide an automatic dimension or speed improvement. The reduced and full
systems carry the same two initial derivative coordinates. The exact nilpotent
example above supplies a genuine rank reduction, separately from this geometric
validation.

Two independent direct evaluations validate the normalization. First,

\[
F(t)=\sum_{k\ge0}\binom{2k}{k}^2t^k/16^k,
\]

with coefficient bound one, gives positive rational remainder bounds for both
\(F\) and \(F'\). Second, the substitution \(v=\tan(\theta/2)\) gives

\[
F(t)=\frac4\pi\int_0^1
[1+(2-4t)v^2+v^4]^{-1/2}\,dv.
\]

Interval quadrature bounds the quartic separately on each rational cell,
encloses square roots by rational bisection, and bounds \(\pi\) through Machin's
alternating arctangent identity. It uses neither the connection nor the
hypergeometric coefficients. The narrow transported interval contains the
independent hypergeometric enclosure and lies inside the coarser Euler-integral
enclosure. Full setup, source seeds, arithmetic errors and output intervals are
retained in `results/nx36/nx31_acceptance.json`.

Replay with `PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/nx_analytic_decisions.py --check`.
These are exact Python identities and rational analytic enclosures, not a Lean
formalization of period integration, and they do not supply an integral flux
basis for another geometric family.
