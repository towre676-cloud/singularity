Relational Height Populations and Arithmetic Visibility

Exact examples, nested boundaries, and primewise singularities

Singularity project contributors

18 October 2026

Abstract

We study nonnegative integer heights on a finite graph, counted jointly by total height and quadratic edge energy. Removing a common translation gives canonical minimum-zero profiles. An additional span mark separates binary cuts from nested height configurations. We give explicit seven-vertex examples in which equal Laplacian characteristic polynomials, or equal complete binary cut-volume censuses, coexist with unequal height coefficients. The first differences occur at total heights two and three. For both pairs, the unmarked local coefficient tables through total height four, equality below the first difference, the first-difference polynomial and its stated factorization are checked by the Lean 4 kernel against a semantic definition of the coefficients. Apart from Lean itself, the only unverified link is a transcription script with recorded input digests. A third comparison, between the cube and the eight-vertex Möbius ladder, delays the first difference to total height four; it is checked by exact software replay only. For a fixed energy weight q in the closed unit interval, we prove the following. If the first difference occurs at total height r, it fixes the exponent of a zeta factor in the ratio of the associated prime products, and hence the power singularity at s=1/r. For the two seven-vertex pairs at q=1/2, the regular factor at s=1/r is enclosed in explicit rigorous intervals. The examples come with standard-library exact software, independent finite replay, and an exhaustive census of connected graphs through six vertices. General reconstruction, unbounded delayed visibility and historical priority are left open.

1 Question and background

A graph parameter can retain many numerical facts while forgetting the correspondence between the configurations that produced them. The binary cut-volume census is a useful example. It counts vertex subsets jointly by cardinality and boundary size, so equality of this polynomial is stronger than equality of a single energy moment. Nevertheless, it does not retain which subsets are nested or which edges cross two of their boundaries simultaneously. Integer heights provide a direct way to observe those relationships.

The ingredients have established antecedents. Integral cut lattices organize graph gradients modulo constants [BHN1997]. Bivariate Ising polynomials organize binary configurations [AM2009]. Integer-valued Gaussian models on lattices use the same squared nearest-neighbour energy [BPR2024]. Formal Euler transforms reorganize coefficient data into cyclotomic factors [Moree2005]. We use these ingredients with explicit marks and a restricted inverse question. The paper does not claim a new origin for the individual ingredients. Nor does it claim that the proposed scalar-height observations determine every graph. No completed priority search is claimed.

There are two logically distinct constructions. First, a local generating function records a finite graph’s integer heights. Second, an Euler product attaches that same local function to every rational prime. The first determines graph-information content. The second supplies an arithmetic organization and a precise comparison of singularities. Keeping this distinction visible prevents a prime product from being credited with information that it merely reencodes.

Results labeled SNG-… refer to the full proofs in the project monograph (docs/MONOGRAPH.md). The proofs given here are complete for the statements as displayed. Lean theorem names refer to the core Lean 4.12.0 package in formal/.

2 Local populations and their marks

Let G be a finite connected simple loopless graph on vertices 0,…,k−10,\ldots,k-1, with k≥2k\ge2. Write did_i for its degrees and AijA_{ij} for its adjacency matrix. Connectedness is used only in Section 5 and in the certified enclosures of Section 4. Propositions 1 and 2 and Theorem 3 hold without it. For a nonnegative integer vector a, put

|a|=∑iai,EG(a)=∑{i,j}∈E(G)(ai−aj)2. |a|=\sum_i a_i,\qquad E_G(a)=\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}(a_i-a_j)^2.

The unnormalized series and the fully marked normalized series are

LG(x,q)=∑a≥0x|a|qEG(a),ℬG(x,q,z)=∑mina=0x|a|qEG(a)zmaxa. L_G(x,q)=\sum_{a\ge0}x^{|a|}q^{E_G(a)},\qquad \mathcal B_G(x,q,z)=\sum_{\min a=0}x^{|a|}q^{E_G(a)}z^{\max a}.

Every coefficient of x is a finite polynomial in q, because there are only (m+k−1k−1)\binom{m+k-1}{k-1} nonnegative vectors of total height m (SNG-F01). Hence these definitions are formal before any analytic parameter is chosen. Counts are of original labeled assignments; no quotient by graph automorphisms is taken. For 0 at most q at most one and absolute value of x below one, comparison with (1−|x|)−k(1-|x|)^{-k} also gives analytic convergence.

Proposition 1 (translation normalization; SNG-F02). With BG(x,q)=ℬG(x,q,1)B_G(x,q)=\mathcal B_G(x,q,1),

LG(x,q)=BG(x,q)1−xk. L_G(x,q)=\frac{B_G(x,q)}{1-x^k}.

Proof. Every nonnegative vector has the unique form a=b+t𝟏a=b+t\mathbf1, where t=minat=\min a and minb=0\min b=0. Edge differences, and hence energy, are unchanged. Total height increases by kt. Summing the common translation gives the geometric factor. This is a formal identity with no convergence assumption. ▫\square

The coefficientwise form of Proposition 1 is kernel-checked for arbitrary edge lists, including disconnected graphs, as allCount_eq_shiftSum and allCount_rec (formal/Singularity/Normalization.lean). The span-marked identity, with z retained, is not formalized.

The span mark records a genuine distinction. The binary cut polynomial

IG(x,q)=∑S⊆V(G)x|S|q|∂S| I_G(x,q)=\sum_{S\subseteq V(G)}x^{|S|}q^{|\partial S|}

satisfies IG=1+xk+[z]ℬGI_G=1+x^k+[z]\mathcal B_G (SNG-F11). Thus the full marked series contains the binary census explicitly. The specialization z=1 merges spans; a general recovery theorem for the binary sector from that specialization is not established here.

Proposition 2 (nested-boundary energy; SNG-F09, SNG-F10). A normalized vector of span h is equivalent to a chain of nonempty proper sets

Sh⊆⋯⊆S1,a=∑t=1h𝟏St. S_h\subseteq\cdots\subseteq S_1,\qquad a=\sum_{t=1}^h\mathbf1_{S_t}.

Repeated sets are permitted. If e(U,W)e(U,W) counts edges between disjoint sets, then

|a|=∑t|St|,EG(a)=∑t|∂St|+2∑r<te(St,V\Sr). |a|=\sum_t|S_t|,\qquad E_G(a)=\sum_t|\partial S_t| +2\sum_{r<t}e(S_t,V\setminus S_r).

Proof. Take St={i:ai≥t}S_t=\{i:a_i\ge t\}. Each coordinate is recovered by counting its occupied thresholds, proving the bijection and total-height identity. On an edge, every nonzero threshold difference has the same sign. Expanding the square of their sum gives the individual threshold crossings and twice every pair of simultaneous crossings. For r<tr<t, simultaneous crossing means that one endpoint lies in StS_t and the other outside SrS_r. Summing edges proves the formula. ▫\square

The threshold reconstruction, the volume identity and the energy identity are kernel-checked for every edge list and every profile as reconstruct, vol_eq_levels and energy_nested. Nestedness and properness of the threshold sets are checked as thr_nested and thr_proper_of_minZero, and the span count as span_eq (formal/Singularity/Thresholds.lean). The converse direction of the bijection, from an arbitrary chain to a profile, is proved above but is not stated in Lean.

For two levels, this becomes E(𝟏S+𝟏T)=|∂S|+|∂T|+2e(S,V\T)E(\mathbf1_S+\mathbf1_T)=|\partial S|+|\partial T|+2e(S,V\setminus T), for nonempty S contained in proper T, allowing equality. The final term explains why a distribution of isolated cuts can miss the multilevel population.

3 Finite visibility and reference examples

Put ℓG,m(q)=[xm]LG(x,q)\ell_{G,m}(q)=[x^m]L_G(x,q). Splitting weak compositions by their multiplicity patterns gives (SNG-F14)

ℓG,1=∑iqdi,ℓG,2=∑iq4di+∑i<jqdi+dj−2Aij, \ell_{G,1}=\sum_iq^{d_i},\qquad \ell_{G,2}=\sum_iq^{4d_i}+\sum_{i<j}q^{d_i+d_j-2A_{ij}},

ℓG,3=∑iq9di+∑i≠jq4di+dj−4Aij+∑i<j<tqdi+dj+dt−2(Aij+Ait+Ajt). \begin{aligned} \ell_{G,3}={}&\sum_iq^{9d_i} +\sum_{i\ne j}q^{4d_i+d_j-4A_{ij}}\\ &+\sum_{i<j<t}q^{d_i+d_j+d_t-2(A_{ij}+A_{it}+A_{jt})}. \end{aligned}

These formulas also delimit the meaning of a small-depth witness. Volume one observes degrees. With degrees fixed, volume two observes the edge degree-sum polynomial MG(q)=∑ij∈Eqdi+djM_G(q)=\sum_{ij\in E}q^{d_i+d_j}, since then ℓG,2−ℓH,2=(q−2−1)(MG−MH)\ell_{G,2}-\ell_{H,2}=(q^{-2}-1)(M_G-M_H). The current volume-three witness separates an aggregated binary census by an ordered two-vertex height pattern. It is not a claim that the graph energy contains a new three-body interaction.

All reference inputs are explicit. On vertices 0,…,60,\ldots,6, the first pair has edge sets

Their degree multisets and Laplacian characteristic polynomials agree. The latter, computed exactly in integer arithmetic, is

The unique degree-four vertex lies in a triangle in the first graph and not the second, so they are nonisomorphic (SNG-E001). Direct coefficient enumeration gives the first difference

ℓG2,2−ℓH2,2=2q2(1−q)3(1+q)=2q2(1−q)2(1−q2). \ell_{G_2,2}-\ell_{H_2,2}=2q^2(1-q)^3(1+q)=2q^2(1-q)^2(1-q^2).

Its source is elementary: the degree-sum polynomials differ, MG2−MH2=2q4(1−q)2M_{G_2}-M_{H_2}=2q^4(1-q)^2. The pair therefore separates the height population from the Laplacian spectrum through degree mixing, not through a higher-order relation.

The second pair has edge sets

E(G3)={03,04,06,12,13,23,24,35},E(H3)={01,04,06,15,16,26,36,45}. \begin{aligned} E(G_3)&=\{03,04,06,12,13,23,24,35\},\\ E(H_3)&=\{01,04,06,15,16,26,36,45\}. \end{aligned}

Its graphs have the same complete binary cut-volume polynomial: enumeration of all 128 subsets of each graph agrees at every pair of subset size and boundary size. Their unique degree-four vertices have respectively one and two leaf neighbors, proving nonisomorphism (SNG-E002). The local coefficients agree through volume two, while

ℓG3,3−ℓH3,3=q3(1−q)(1−q3)2(1−q4)2. \ell_{G_3,3}-\ell_{H_3,3} =q^3(1-q)(1-q^3)^2(1-q^4)^2.

This entire discrepancy occurs in span two. Span one agrees by the binary-census equality, and span three agrees by the equal degree multisets. The degree-sum polynomials agree, which explains agreement at volume two. The finer joint degree table of the edges differs, and the unequal-height sector weights endpoint degrees through 4di+dj4d_i+d_j. Both differences are strictly positive on the open interval between zero and one, as their factorizations show.

4 Arithmetic assembly and the comparison theorem

For a positive integer nn, let vp(n)v_p(n) be its pp-adic valuation. Define

ℰG(n1,…,nk)=∑pEG(vp(n1),…,vp(nk)), \mathcal E_G(n_1,\ldots,n_k) =\sum_pE_G(v_p(n_1),\ldots,v_p(n_k)),

ZG(s,q)=∑n1,…,nk≥1qℰG(𝐧)(n1⋯nk)s. Z_G(s,q)=\sum_{n_1,\ldots,n_k\ge1} \frac{q^{\mathcal E_G(\mathbf n)}}{(n_1\cdots n_k)^s}.

For fixed q in the closed unit interval and real part of s greater than one, absolute convergence and unique factorization give ZG(s,q)=∏pLG(p−s,q)Z_G(s,q)=\prod_pL_G(p^{-s},q) (SNG-A01). The construction is multiplicative across primes, while each prime’s local factor retains the graph interaction among its exponent coordinates.

Theorem 3 (first-discrepancy visibility; SNG-A11). Fix q in the closed interval [0,1]. Suppose two graphs with the same number of vertices satisfy ℓG,m(q)=ℓH,m(q)\ell_{G,m}(q)=\ell_{H,m}(q) for m below r, and δ=ℓG,r(q)−ℓH,r(q)≠0\delta=\ell_{G,r}(q)-\ell_{H,r}(q)\ne0. Then their ratio Euler product converges for real s greater than 1/r and equals

RG,H(s,q)=ζ(rs)δKG,H(s,q), R_{G,H}(s,q)=\zeta(rs)^\delta K_{G,H}(s,q),

where K has a finite positive limit at s=1/r and is holomorphic and nonzero in a complex neighborhood of that point. Consequently

RG,H(s,q)∼KG,H(1/r,q)(rs−1)−δ(s↓1/r). R_{G,H}(s,q)\sim K_{G,H}(1/r,q)(rs-1)^{-\delta} \quad(s\downarrow1/r).

Proof. Constant terms are one, so cancellation of the lower local coefficients gives LG/LH=1+δxr+O(xr+1)L_G/L_H=1+\delta x^r+O(x^{r+1}). The ratio Euler product therefore has an absolutely summable logarithm for real s greater than 1/r. Every local factor is positive there. Multiplication by (1−xr)δ(1-x^r)^\delta removes the first discrepancy, yielding 1+O(xr+1)1+O(x^{r+1}). Thus

KG,H(s,q)=∏p(1−p−rs)δLG(p−s,q)LH(p−s,q) K_{G,H}(s,q)=\prod_p(1-p^{-rs})^\delta \frac{L_G(p^{-s},q)}{L_H(p^{-s},q)}

converges for real s greater than 1/(r+1). Near s=1/r, finitely many small-prime factors are nonzero by positivity and continuity; the remaining tail has a normally convergent logarithm. This proves the local complex statement. The zeta Euler product proves the factorization on the right of 1/r, and ζ(1+t)∼1/t\zeta(1+t)\sim1/t gives the asymptotic. ▫\square

The hypothesis concerns values at the chosen q. A polynomial discrepancy can vanish at an endpoint or at an interior root, which changes the first visible volume at that parameter. For the three reference pairs the polynomial differences are positive on 0<q<1, so the theorem applies there with r=2, 3 and 4. At q=1/2 the exponents are δ=3/32 at s=1/2, δ=11025/262144 at s=1/3, and δ=81/2048 at s=1/4 for the cube comparison. These are exact rational evaluations of the displayed differences. Nonintegral exponents give power or branch singularities, not ordinary poles. The quotient is initially a quotient of convergent positive series on s>1; its ratio Euler product supplies the extension used in the theorem. No global meromorphic-continuation claim follows. Theorem 3 is a human-readable proof; it is not formalized.

5 Synchronization and its singular limit

Connectedness implies zero energy exactly at constant heights. Hence ZG(s,0)=ζ(ks)Z_G(s,0)=\zeta(ks), whereas ZG(s,1)=ζ(s)kZ_G(s,1)=\zeta(s)^k (SNG-A04). More generally, the first local coefficient is αG(q)=∑iqdi\alpha_G(q)=\sum_iq^{d_i}. For fixed q in [0,1], removal of the linear Euler term gives (SNG-A09)

ZG(s,q)=ζ(s)αG(q)HG(s,q), Z_G(s,q)=\zeta(s)^{\alpha_G(q)}H_G(s,q),

initially for real part of s above one, with H holomorphic for real part of s above 1/2 and positive and nonzero near real s=1. Every fixed positive q therefore restores an s=1 divergence, while exact synchronization q=0 leaves the finite value ζ(k)\zeta(k) there. The order of these two limits matters.

Let d be minimum degree and b the number of vertices attaining it. Suppose q tends to zero and real s tends down to one, along sequences, with qdlog(1/(s−1))→c<∞q^d\log(1/(s-1))\to c<\infty. Then (SNG-A16)

ZG(s,q)ζ(ks)→ebc. \frac{Z_G(s,q)}{\zeta(ks)}\longrightarrow e^{bc}.

To justify this limit, use translation-normalized local factors and subtract their common linear term. For real s at least one, each such logarithm is bounded by an explicit constant times qλp−2sq^\lambda p^{-2s}, where λ is the edge connectivity. The bound comes from the weak-composition coefficient count and the fact that every nonzero minimum-zero profile has energy at least λ (SNG-A15). Summing over primes, the normalized product tends to one as q tends to zero, uniformly in s. Finally αG(q)=bqd+O(qd+1)\alpha_G(q)=bq^d+O(q^{d+1}) and logζ(s)=log(1/(s−1))+O(s−1)\log\zeta(s)=\log(1/(s-1))+O(s-1) supply the exponential limit. This argument describes a boundary layer in the declared real domain, independently of any global continuation question.

The first positive energy has a separate combinatorial interpretation. Integer coarea bounds energy below by the sum of threshold cut sizes, hence by λ times span. Equality at the minimum positive energy forces a binary minimum-cut indicator (SNG-A05). Consequently, initially for real part of s above one, the coefficient of qλq^\lambda in log(ZG/ζ(ks))\log(Z_G/\zeta(ks)) is the sum of prime-zeta functions ∑|∂S|=λP(|S|s)\sum_{|\partial S|=\lambda}P(|S|s), counting both cut orientations (SNG-A06). This links cut side sizes to an analytic coefficient without claiming that minimum cuts identify the whole graph.

6 Computational and formal scope

The released computational core uses integer and rational arithmetic and the Python standard library. It supplies weak-composition counts, marked sectors, exact Laplacian characteristic polynomials, formal Euler exponents with independent recomposition, constrained original-label rank and unrank, forest message passing, root-of-unity residue numerators, weighted hard contraction and primitive-profile inversion. A finite certificate records a graph source, coefficient range and declared comparison. Replay parses it strictly and recomputes all coefficients by two routes that do not reuse the builder’s enumerator: stars-and-bars compositions and level chains. It also checks the normalization and threshold identities. PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/reproduce.py --check replays the four retained certificates and the three comparison pairs. These are software-level exact checks relative to the Python interpreter.

The Lean package in formal/ crosses the remaining boundary only where stated. It kernel-checks the coefficientwise normalization identity of Proposition 1, the nested energy identity of Proposition 2, and the decisive unmarked coefficient tables of the first two pairs, as listed in Section 3. It does not formalize generating functions, the span-marked series, the prime products or any analytic statement of Sections 4 and 5. It is not linked to the Python implementation.

An exhaustive enumeration covers all labeled simple graphs on one to six vertices, 26,704 of which are connected on six vertices. Connected graphs were grouped into isomorphism classes by an exact minimal adjacency code over degree-preserving relabelings. This yields respectively 1, 1, 2, 6, 21 and 112 classes (results/small_census.json, scripts/census.py --max-order 6 --max-volume 6). For order six, exact polynomial local coefficients through volumes one, two and three distinguish respectively 68, 100 and 112 classes. In this population the binary cut census also separates all classes, so a connected nonisomorphic pair with equal binary census, such as the second pair, needs at least seven vertices. This is a complete finite census with an explicit generation boundary. It does not imply completeness at larger orders. In particular, the seven- and eight-vertex examples illustrate why visibility depth must remain part of the specification.

7 Open structural questions

The main inverse problem asks whether the fully marked series identifies a graph in a declared class. A negative answer should describe a transformation or an exact pair preserving all heights, rather than merely a long agreeing prefix. A complementary question asks whether nonisomorphic pairs can have first-separation depth tending to infinity. Both questions concern scalar marks. If every vertex receives an independent variable, labeled adjacency is recovered already from the one- and two-coordinate coefficients (SNG-F18), making that unrestricted version much easier.

The prime lift adds an analytic hierarchy to these local questions. It does not add graph information: coefficients at prime powers recover the local coefficients, and local coefficients determine the Euler product. Its contribution is to turn a finite first discrepancy into a controlled singularity, allowing relational depth and arithmetic location to be studied together.
