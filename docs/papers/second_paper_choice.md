# P36 selection: exact observation depth

Decision date: 18 October 2026. The selected paper is **Exact observation depth for integer-height graph populations**, source `docs/papers/locality.md`, claim register `docs/papers/locality_claims.md`. Its center is the sharp reconstruction theorem for simple 2-regular interiors of known order n, with the exact budget floor(n/2). Connected-support locality supplies the proof and compiler; the first-cycle coefficient supplies the reconstruction diagonal and explicit sharpness pairs.

## Verified primary comparisons

On 18 October 2026, the authors' arXiv manuscript of Patel and Regts, [arXiv:1607.01167v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.01167v3), was read at Section 3, especially Lemma 3.5, Theorem 3.1 and the colored extension, Theorem 3.7. It explicitly supports additive expansions on connected induced subgraphs and efficient bounded-degree coefficient computation. Therefore the selected paper treats connectedness, graph coloring and general low-order coefficient methodology as antecedents. Its contribution is the particular integer-height specialization and its sharp inverse result, not an independent origin claim for that framework. The SIAM publisher record verifies volume 46, issue 6, pages 1893–1919 and DOI [10.1137/16M1101003](https://doi.org/10.1137/16M1101003).

The author manuscript [arXiv:1707.05186](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05186) and the publisher's [Algorithmica record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00453-018-0511-9) give the later bounded-degree induced-count result. It reinforces the existing algorithmic framework. The manuscript does not present benchmark speedups against the project's separator implementation as superiority to Patel–Regts or to all exact graph-invariant algorithms.

Chertkov and Chernyak's author manuscript [arXiv:cond-mat/0603189](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0603189), *Loop series for discrete statistical models on graphs*, describes exact belief-propagation and generalized-loop corrections. It is cited as a neighboring partition-function tradition. The present support-log proof does not use the same BP normalization and makes no claim to originate a loop expansion. Bauerschmidt–Park–Rodriguez's [arXiv:2202.02286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.02286) verifies the discrete Gaussian model and its lattice scaling question; it is not a completeness theorem for this finite graph series.

The bounded literature check supports a precise positioning, not historical priority. Neither the selected article nor its acceptance record calls these results the first of their kind. A journal referee may identify additional antecedents without invalidating any displayed proof or finite receipt.

## Independent review and reproduction

A separate review agent read the complete proofs and implementation, verified the primary Patel–Regts and Chertkov–Chernyak comparisons, and independently ran `PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/locality_family.py --check` in the release worktree. The review required explicit ambient degrees, known order n, exclusion of q=0 and q=1 in the inverse, finite transfer-matrix truncation, an arithmetic-operation complexity model, and the one-way nature of the decorated-statistics implication. Those restrictions appear in the selected article. Its final review is retained in `docs/papers/locality_claims.md`.

The complete repository archive includes the article sources, rendered PDF/HTML/LaTeX, implementation, tests and receipts. The integration release records actual clean-checkout checks and PDF inspection in the session log and `results/publication_release.json`; the existence of this source file alone does not certify that release step.

## Precise open agenda after selection

The scalar fixed-pinning inverse problem remains: decide whether nonisomorphic finite simple graphs can have equal complete \(A_H\), with a proof or an exact full-series-preserving construction. Long prefix agreement alone is insufficient. At finite budget, determine which linear combinations of decorated connected-set counts the q-polynomial actually separates; Theorem SNG-F55 proves the forward sufficient-statistics implication, not its converse.

The graph-realization questions at budgets 38 with a terminal edge and 11 without remain separate from the cycle families. Their candidate vectors come from the symmetric-context quotient, while the selected family gives controlled late nonzero discrepancies. Efficient reconstruction beyond 2-regular interiors and stronger budgets for the compiler are further research targets. A hybrid compiler using separator methods inside each connected footprint is a concrete next algorithmic direction. The moment enrichment and ordered charge charts remain available as a distinct complete-observation route, with their own computational and theoretical budget limits.
