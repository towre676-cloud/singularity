Exact Observation Depth for Integer-Height Graph Populations

Connected supports, delayed distinctions, and sharp reconstruction

Singularity project contributors

18 October 2026

Abstract

For a finite simple graph, we count nonnegative integer vertex heights jointly by their sum and a pinned quadratic edge energy. We prove a connected-support expansion for the formal logarithm: the coefficients through total height R depend only on connected induced vertex sets of size at most R, decorated with their ambient degrees. This gives an exact bounded-degree compiler whose graph-size cost is linear at fixed R. The expansion identifies the first coefficient at which equal-order regular graphs of girth at least g can differ: it is the difference of their g-cycle counts times an explicit polynomial. Our central reconstruction theorem determines every n-vertex simple 2-regular graph from the pinned series through volume floor(n/2), at every fixed real energy weight strictly between zero and one. The budget is sharp for every n at least six. In particular, a cycle of length 2g and two disjoint g-cycles agree below volume g and have a nonzero explicit discrepancy at g. Their dominating-terminal blocks remain indistinguishable below that volume under every fixed closing context. We supply complete formal-series proofs, independent finite enumeration receipts, cycle reconstruction for all 57 simple 2-regular graphs on at most fourteen vertices, and retained bounded-degree timing comparisons. The connected-cluster principle and low-order graph-coefficient methods have established antecedents; the claim here is the stated height-population specialization and its exact reconstruction and observation-depth theorems.

1 Population, budget and contribution

Let H=(V,E) be a finite simple loopless graph. Heights are functions h:V to the nonnegative integers. They remain attached to the original labeled vertices; no quotient by graph automorphisms is taken. Define J(h)=∑v∈Vhv,EH(h)=∑uv∈E(hu−hv)2, J(h)=\sum_{v\in V}h_v,\qquad E_H(h)=\sum_{uv\in E}(h_u-h_v)^2, and, for an integer λ≥0\lambda\ge0, Fλ(H;x,q)=∑hxJ(h)qEH(h)+λ∑vhv2. F_\lambda(H;x,q)=\sum_h x^{J(h)}q^{E_H(h)+\lambda\sum_vh_v^2}. \tag{1} The coefficient of xjx^j is a polynomial in q with nonnegative integer coefficients. It counts a finite population of (j+n−1n−1)\binom{j+n-1}{n-1} profiles when n=|V|>0n=|V|>0. All identities below are initially identities in ℚ[q][[x]]\mathbb Q[q][[x]]; evaluation at q0∈(0,1)q_0\in(0,1) is coefficientwise. No infinite analytic expansion or convergence assertion is needed. We write AH=F2(H)A_H=F_2(H) and LH=F0(H)L_H=F_0(H).

A budget R means that coefficients of x through degree R are available. It bounds total integer height, rather than the number of occupied vertices alone or the maximum height. A profile at volume R occupies at most R vertices. The logarithm strengthens this elementary observation: only connected occupied footprints need to be compiled independently. We exploit that identity to establish a sharp observation threshold for an infinite graph class.

The broad computational idea is established. Patel and Regts [PR2017, Section 3] compute low-order graph-polynomial information from connected induced subgraph counts, including colored variants. Their additivity criterion explains the vanishing of disconnected terms. Their later induced-count algorithm [PR2019] supplies further bounded-degree context. Our ambient-degree labels and unbounded-height formal series require the specific specialization proved below; we do not claim a new connected-cluster principle. Bauerschmidt, Park and Rodriguez [BPR2024] study the integer-valued Gaussian model on lattices and its high-temperature scaling, a different question from finite-volume exact graph reconstruction. Chertkov and Chernyak [CC2006] express partition functions through belief-propagation loop corrections. The present paper uses a direct formal logarithm and does not claim to originate loop expansions or to provide a new belief-propagation approximation.

The principal result is Theorem 6: the exact and sharp budget ⌊n/2⌋\lfloor n/2\rfloor for simple 2-regular interiors of known order n. The preceding local identities provide its proof and an implementation. General completeness of the single fixed-pinning series AHA_H is not asserted.

2 Exact connected-support expansion

Theorem 1 (connected-support locality; SNG-F55). For every nonempty S, ΦS\Phi_S vanishes when H[S]H[S] is disconnected, has no term below degree |S||S|, and depends only on H[S]H[S] decorated by wvw_v. Proof. Give each vertex its own variable yvy_v, replacing xJ(h)x^{J(h)} by ∏vyvhv\prod_vy_v^{h_v}, and call the resulting series F. Forcing heights outside T to zero is the substitution yv=0y_v=0 outside T. The substitution commutes with formal logarithms. A monomial of logF\log F supported on U therefore survives in logZT\log Z_T exactly when U⊆TU\subseteq T. Its multiplicity in (3) is ∑U⊆T⊆S(−1)|S|−|T|, \sum_{U\subseteq T\subseteq S}(-1)^{|S|-|T|}, which equals one when U=S and zero otherwise. Hence ΦS\Phi_S consists exactly of monomials with support S. Such monomials have total degree at least |S||S|. If H[S]H[S] splits into nonempty components, the restricted energy is a sum over those components, ZSZ_S is a product, and its logarithm is a sum. It has no monomial meeting distinct components, so the exact-support part vanishes. Finally, the restricted exponents involve only edges inside S and the labels wvw_v. Partitioning all monomials by support proves (4), and specializing every yv=xy_v=x proves the assertion. ▫\square

This is a sufficient-statistics theorem, not an inverse equivalence: equal decorated small-subgraph counts imply equal truncated series. Equal truncated series need not determine all those counts individually, since their polynomial contributions can be dependent.

3 The first cycle visible to a budget

For a regular graph of degree d, put a=qd+λ,f=q−2−1,κd,λ=af=qd+λ−2(1−q2). a=q^{d+\lambda},\qquad f=q^{-2}-1, \qquad \kappa_{d,\lambda}=af =q^{d+\lambda-2}(1-q^2). \tag{7} The temporary Laurent notation f is harmless: each final expression below is a polynomial when d is at least two. The girth of an acyclic graph is understood to be infinite. Let Ng(H)N_g(H) count unoriented simple g-cycles.

Theorem 3 (first-cycle coefficient; SNG-F56). Let g be at least three. Suppose H and H’ are d-regular graphs of the same order, both of girth at least g. Then Fλ(H)≡Fλ(H′)(modxg),[xg](Fλ(H)−Fλ(H′))=(Ng(H)−Ng(H′))κd,λg. F_\lambda(H)\equiv F_\lambda(H')\pmod{x^g}, \qquad [x^g](F_\lambda(H)-F_\lambda(H')) =(N_g(H)-N_g(H'))\kappa_{d,\lambda}^{\,g}. \tag{8}

Proof. We first justify the tree counts used in the locality formula. For a tree T on k vertices, the number of all graph homomorphisms into a d-regular n-vertex graph is ndk−1nd^{k-1}, obtained by choosing the root image and then one of d neighbors for each child. Group these maps by their equality partition of the vertices. After identifying equal vertices and suppressing parallel edges, the quotient is connected. A quotient with a loop has no map into a simple graph. For k at most g, any nontrivial quotient has fewer than g vertices; an injective realization of a cyclic quotient would violate the girth condition. Every contributing proper quotient is therefore a smaller tree. Induction on k now shows that the number of injective homomorphisms of T depends only on n,d and T for k at most g.

For k below g an injective tree image has no extra edge among its vertices: adding one to the tree would create a cycle on at most k vertices. Thus the number of induced copies of every such T is its injective homomorphism count divided by |AutT||\operatorname{Aut}T|, independently of H. All ambient-degree labels also agree. Theorem 1 proves equality of logarithms, and hence series, below degree g.

At degree g, contributions from supports smaller than g still agree. An induced graph on exactly g vertices is either a tree or a chordless g-cycle whenever it is connected. An injective tree image can have an extra edge only if its tree is the path on g vertices: the cycle formed by that edge must use every vertex. Each g-cycle has exactly g spanning paths. Consequently the induced-path count is the universal injective path-image count minus gNg(H)gN_g(H); every other g-vertex tree count is universal.

Only height one at every support vertex contributes at total degree g. The coefficient of ∏v∈Syv\prod_{v\in S}y_v in logF\log F is ag∑D⊆E(H[S])(S,D) connectedf|D|. a^g\sum_{\substack{D\subseteq E(H[S])\\(S,D)\text{ connected}}}f^{|D|}. \tag{9} To verify (9), expand the binary weight on S as a|S|∏e∈E(H[S])(1+f)a^{|S|}\prod_{e\in E(H[S])}(1+f). Partition each chosen edge set into its connected components. The exponential formula, or direct expansion of the formal logarithm followed by this component partition, leaves precisely connected spanning edge sets. A path contributes agfg−1a^gf^{g-1}. A cycle contributes ag(gfg−1+fg)a^g(gf^{g-1}+f^g), from its g spanning paths and its full edge set. Subtracting the g missing induced paths leaves agfga^gf^g per cycle. This proves the logarithmic discrepancy. Since all lower coefficients agree and the constant coefficients are one, the first discrepancy of the series equals that of their logarithms. Equation (8) follows. ▫\square

For the cube and eight-vertex Möbius ladder, d=3, g=4 and the square counts are six and four. Equation (8) gives 2q4(1−q2)42q^4(1-q^2)^4 for L and 2q12(1−q2)42q^{12}(1-q^2)^4 for A. The former is exactly the retained reference discrepancy. The triangular prism and K3,3K_{3,3} give the analogous volume-three difference from their two versus zero triangles. These applications explain particular fixtures; they do not assert that cycle counts determine the entire height series.

To state the context consequence precisely, form BHB_H by adding distinct nonadjacent terminals s,t, each joined to every vertex of H. A closing context C is a fixed finite simple graph with two designated ordered terminals. It is glued to BHB_H by identifying corresponding terminals and retaining all other vertices separately. Terminal edge conventions are unchanged by H, and the context has no additional identification with the interior. Write ZC,HZ_{C,H} for the resulting unpinned local height series.

Proof. Assign heights s,t to the two terminals. For an interior profile of volume j, the edges from terminals to H contribute m(s2+t2)+2∑v∈Hhv2−2j(s+t). m(s^2+t^2)+2\sum_{v\in H}h_v^2-2j(s+t). 5 Sharp reconstruction of every 2-regular interior

For k at least one, let φk\varphi_k be the exact-support contribution (3) for a k-vertex path, with every vertex assigned ambient degree two and pinning two. Theorem 1 gives φk=O(xk)\varphi_k=O(x^k), so b(x,q)=∑k≥1φk(x,q),Dℓ=logACℓ−ℓb b(x,q)=\sum_{k\ge1}\varphi_k(x,q),\qquad D_\ell=\log A_{C_\ell}-\ell b \tag{13} are well-defined formal series, computable to every finite budget.

Theorem 6 (sharp 2-regular reconstruction; SNG-F57). Fix an order n and a real q0∈(0,1)q_0\in(0,1). The coefficients of AH(x,q0)A_H(x,q_0) through degree R=⌊n/2⌋R=\lfloor n/2\rfloor determine the isomorphism type of every simple 2-regular graph H of order n. For every n at least six this uniform budget is sharp. Over ℚ(q)\mathbb Q(q), or after the same specialization, the cycle counts are recovered recursively by logAH−nb=∑ℓ≥3cℓDℓ,cr=κ−r[xr](logAH−nb−∑3≤ℓ<rcℓDℓ). \log A_H-nb=\sum_{\ell\ge3}c_\ell D_\ell, \qquad c_r=\kappa^{-r}[x^r]\left(\log A_H-nb-\sum_{3\le\ell<r}c_\ell D_\ell\right). \tag{14}

Proof. A simple 2-regular graph is a disjoint union of cycles of lengths at least three. For a cycle of length ℓ\ell, each connected proper induced subset is a path. There are ℓ\ell copies of each path size 1≤k<ℓ1\le k<\ell. Therefore Theorem 1 gives logACℓ=ℓ∑k<ℓφk+ΦCℓ,Dℓ=ΦCℓ−ℓ∑k≥ℓφk. \log A_{C_\ell}=\ell\sum_{k<\ell}\varphi_k+\Phi_{C_\ell}, \quad D_\ell=\Phi_{C_\ell}-\ell\sum_{k\ge\ell}\varphi_k. In particular Dℓ=O(xℓ)D_\ell=O(x^\ell). The multilinear path contribution at size ℓ\ell is q4ℓfℓ−1q^{4\ell}f^{\ell-1}; the cycle contribution is q4ℓ(ℓfℓ−1+fℓ)q^{4\ell}(\ell f^{\ell-1}+f^\ell). Their difference shows Dℓ=κℓxℓ+O(xℓ+1). D_\ell=\kappa^\ell x^\ell+O(x^{\ell+1}). \tag{15} The series A multiplies over connected components, and logarithms add. Subtracting the total vertex background nbnb proves the first identity in (14). Equation (15) makes that identity triangular, proving its recursive inverse. At q0∈(0,1)q_0\in(0,1), κ(q0)≠0\kappa(q_0)\ne0, so every division is valid.

Recover all cycle counts with length at most R. Two remaining cycles would each have length greater than R, requiring at least 2R+2>n2R+2>n vertices. Hence there is at most one remaining cycle, and its length is the remaining vertex count. The recovered multiset of lengths determines H up to isomorphism.

For sharpness, put g=⌊n/2⌋g=\lfloor n/2\rfloor. If n=2g, compare C2gC_{2g} and Cg⊔CgC_g\sqcup C_g. If n=2g+1, compare C2g+1C_{2g+1} and Cg⊔Cg+1C_g\sqcup C_{g+1}. For n at least six all component lengths are admissible. Their logarithmic differences are respectively 2Dg−D2g2D_g-D_{2g} and Dg+Dg+1−D2g+1D_g+D_{g+1}-D_{2g+1}, whose first nonzero coefficients occur at g by (15). Thus no smaller budget distinguishes every pair. For n=3,4,5 there is only one simple 2-regular isomorphism type, so no analogous lower bound is claimed. ▫\square

The hypothesis that n is known is part of the theorem. It is natural for fixed-order graph comparison and avoids hiding order recovery inside the final remainder step. The rational implementation takes exact input coefficients, validates the recovered nonnegative integer multiplicities and remaining length, and can reject data that are inconsistent with a 2-regular graph.

From the repository root, PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/locality_family.py --check reproduces the retained structural receipt in results/locality_family.json. 7 Scope and next questions

Finite agreement below volume g is now explained and attained by an explicit family, and within the cycle class a sharp inverse theorem is available. It does not follow that the fixed-pinning series reconstructs every finite graph. Nonisomorphic graphs with equal complete A-series remain a legitimate target. The connected-support identity supplies a sufficient condition for truncated equality, but its aggregated q-polynomials can merge different decorated statistics. An exhaustive prefix search therefore cannot be promoted to a completeness theorem.

Past the first cycle, equal cycle counts need not imply equal coefficients: overlapping cycles and other connected footprints contribute. The project has additional loop and overlap results (SNG-F60–SNG-F61) and a complete moment enrichment (SNG-F58). They are deliberately outside this paper’s central proof and are not prerequisites for Theorem 6. A further algorithmic question is to combine separators inside the connected footprints with the fixed-budget compiler, extending the useful budget without requiring bounded treewidth of the full graph. These questions retain the same explicit volume, parameter and graph-class boundaries as the proved results.
